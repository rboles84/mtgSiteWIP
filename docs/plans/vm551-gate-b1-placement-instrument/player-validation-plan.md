# VM-551 Gate B1 Real-Player Validation Plan

Status: Gate B1 semantic design is owner approved, but this remains protocol design only. Player-validation preparation, recruitment, data collection, implementation, and consent handling require separate authorization.

## Purpose

The pilot tests whether players understand the questions, can find representative answers, recognize the observations and limitations behind a result, and receive a useful exploration state without feeling steered.

It does not test whether players like praise, whether a label is desired, or whether the system is calibrated.

## Participant groups

Recruit:

- new Commander players;
- intermediate players;
- experienced players;
- players who strongly identify with one deck;
- players whose deck behavior differs from their preferred identity or experience;
- players who receive an unexpected, tied, close, mixed, or insufficient result.

Experience, budget, power expectation, deck behavior, table preference, theme attachment, and self-described identity are recorded separately.

## Stage 1 — item comprehension

Use moderated cognitive interviews before adaptive evaluation.

- Show questions in small balanced blocks, not as an identity quiz.
- Require at least eight independent player reviews per question.
- Each Gate item includes at least four newer-player reviews.
- Each Hall and Crucible item includes at least two players whose deck experience fits and two whose experience does not.
- Ask participants to paraphrase the prompt and selected answer.
- Ask which words required explanation and whether options overlap.
- Capture free-text “none of these” even when no such live option is proposed.
- Compare C01 paraphrases and choices across the calm/open Gate item, the behind Grixis item, and the disrupted Temur item without treating them as independent evidence.
- For the C05 Hall and Bant items, ask whether the player interpreted the prompt as commander reliance or generic creature reliance.
- Stratify jargon findings by `RULES_DEFINED`, `COMMUNITY_STANDARD`, and `INSTRUMENT_OPERATIONAL` rather than treating every definition as the same kind of authority.
- Re-show community-standard terms without their proposed explanation only for comprehension research; verify that the proposed explanation, rather than prior expertise, makes the displayed item understandable.
- Compare interpretation before and after the revised board, board-wipe, permanent, graveyard, engine, interaction, and color-identity definitions. A wording-induced construct change routes revision, not scoring compensation.

Eight reviews are a qualitative routing minimum, not statistical validation. Material disagreement keeps the item provisional.

## Stage 2 — full adaptive shadow journeys

Run the preserved 4 Gate + 2–3 Hall + 0–1 Crucible journey.

Collection lasts at least 14 days and continues while any required cohort, item, Crucible exposure, or high-risk identity remains below its documented floor. This is a future protocol requirement, not authorization to recruit or collect data.

Coverage controls sample size:

- each of 18 Crucible questions receives at least six completed eligible exposures;
- each of 12 Hall questions receives at least ten completed eligible exposures;
- each same-color guild/college boundary receives at least twelve reviews across item and journey work;
- each high-confusion or insufficient-evidence identity receives at least twelve eligible result/challenge reviews;
- at least twenty journeys correctly stop without Crucible;
- each required cohort has at least twelve completed journeys.

Because one journey shows at most one Crucible, the Crucible floor requires at least 108 completed eligible journeys. This is a coverage minimum, not statistical power or calibration.

## Stage 3 — retake and explanation review

- Invite Stage 2 participants to retake after 7–14 days.
- Keep the same instrument version.
- Record changed deck context and recent games first.
- Compare construct observations, dependency groups, route, public state, and identity/close/insufficient outcome separately.
- Interview strong-to-strong changes, unexpected results, and changed high-confidence answers.

## Measures

### Question comprehension

- paraphrase accuracy;
- recognition of the concrete Commander situation;
- jargon requests;
- option overlap;
- missing-option frequency;
- time to a confident answer;
- whether the participant answered a different category than intended;
- whether calm, behind, and disrupted C01 responses are stable, contextually different, or misunderstood;
- whether commander-specific questions were answered about the commander rather than a favorite creature;
- comprehension and definition reliance by jargon authority class;
- whether each revised definition changes interpretation of the actual construct.

### Answer confidence

- 1–5 confidence after each response;
- why it fits;
- whether confidence concerns wording, deck context, or experience;
- distinction between conditionality and lack of understanding;
- whether conditional answers are understood as contextual or representational evidence rather than midpoint choices.

### Perceived steering

- which answer seemed most admirable, skilled, powerful, fun, or wanted by the quiz;
- whether order or tone implied preference;
- whether an identity was guessable;
- whether the participant changed an honest answer to pursue/avoid a label.

### Result recognition

Do not ask only “Did you like your result?”

Ask which observations fit, which do not, which describe the tested deck but not the player, which describe a future preference, whether a close/tied/mixed/insufficient state is more honest, and what additional evidence would justify a stronger result.

Ask whether Colorless and Five-Color results feel as legitimate and fully explained as other named identities rather than like fallback or novelty states.

### Result usefulness

- Can the participant name a useful comparison or exploration step?
- Can they explain what the result does not claim?
- Does it help compare decks or commanders?
- Does it create a browsing question without prescribing a deck?
- Do recommendations fit observed behavior rather than only color?
- Do color-count observations remain visibly secondary to prior behavior, or do participants believe the edge/mono-multicolor item decided the result by itself?

### Retake stability

Measure per-construct agreement, dependency-group agreement, route stability, public-state stability, named/close/insufficient stability, context-explained change, strong-to-strong flips, one-answer flips, and whether uncertain responses become directional with experience.

Exact identity agreement is not the only measure. A close pair remaining close can be stable if order changes.

### Explanation understanding

- trace selected answer → observation → result state;
- identify at least one limitation;
- understand that rank is not adjacency;
- understand why Crucible was or was not asked;
- distinguish behavior evidence from theme or philosophy.

## Predeclared item thresholds

These thresholds route revision; they do not establish calibration.

- At least 80% paraphrase the intended construct without importing another category.
- Fewer than 15% require a missing directional option.
- Fewer than 20% identify a directional answer as clearly more virtuous, skilled, or desired.
- No advanced term remains unexplained for newer players.
- Median confidence is at least 3/5 among participants with relevant experience.
- Conditional answers are selected for stated dependency, not confusing wording.
- C01 contextual differences are recorded rather than stacked or averaged; systematic calm/behind/disrupted divergence blocks scoring authority until C01 is split or restricted.
- Commander-reliance items are interpreted as commander-specific, not generic creature centrality.
- Community-standard terms are understood after the proposed explanation by players who did not already know them.
- No Crucible is shown without its competitor set and two independent observations.

Any fail returns the item to wording or construct review. Do not compensate with scoring.

## Predeclared journey thresholds

- At least 85% explain the primary observation behind the public state.
- At least 80% name a limitation or uncertainty.
- Fewer than 10% report identity-revealing steering.
- No strong result uses only dependent observations.
- No one-answer strong-to-strong flip; the state must pass through close, mixed, or insufficient.
- Every high-risk identity has a reviewed direct boundary observation before a strong state.
- Colorless and Five-Color meet the same explanation, recognition, and usefulness standards as other named identities.
- No C15 answer overpowers prior behavioral evidence or creates a one-answer edge/mono-multicolor flip.
- Recommendation relevance is evaluated against behavior; color-only relevance fails.
- Unexpected results receive qualitative review and are not automatically failures.

Thresholds may change only before a new versioned round.

## Unexpected-result interview

1. Ask which answer or observation caused surprise.
2. Ask whether the response described one deck, all decks, a future deck, or table behavior.
3. Ask whether a conditional answer was missing.
4. Show the observation trace without identity names and retest recognition.
5. Compare the strongest expected competitor.
6. Classify the issue as comprehension, representation, mapping, stopping, explanation, or genuine discovery.
7. Do not overwrite evidence to match the preferred label.

## Analysis boundaries

- Report sampled counts and denominators only.
- Do not infer population prevalence.
- Do not call result recognition accuracy.
- Do not tune and evaluate on the same sample.
- Preserve contradictions and minority responses.
- Separate wording evidence from identity-mapping evidence.
- Publish unknown and insufficient findings.
- Calibration requires a separate method and owner decision.

## Exit

The player pilot may recommend wording revision, conditional-answer changes, item retirement, eligibility changes, provisional mapping, close/insufficient preservation, or a separately reviewed implementation candidate.

It cannot authorize production cutover, deployment, or certification.
