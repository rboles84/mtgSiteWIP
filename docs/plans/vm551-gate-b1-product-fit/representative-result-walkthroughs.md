# Representative Result Walkthroughs

These are non-production demonstrations. They do not calculate scores, simulate the unimplemented pilot, claim accurate placement, or authorize a result-state heuristic. Each state shown is one already supported by Gate A; a future implementation may emit it only under an approved contract.

## 1. Same-color guild/college — Simic with Quandrix close

**Plausible observations:** the player prefers resources visible on the battlefield, several overlapping engines, incremental advantage, and long setup only when each step contributes. In the direct Green-Blue comparison they choose overlapping engines rather than one central compounding engine.

**Gate A-compatible state:** `close` — Current best fit: Simic; close alternative: Quandrix. This example assumes the separate close rule has direct support and passes; no numeric confidence is implied.

**Why Simic appeared:** the observations describe an adaptive network of creatures, lands, counters, or small engines that can keep functioning after one part is removed.

**Plausible alternative:** Quandrix, because the player also accepts setup and likes value structures.

**Observable distinction:** the current observation favors overlapping, replaceable growth. The Atlas's stronger identity boundary is organic adaptation versus explicit mathematical multiplication; the approved C06 question does not fully test that boundary.

**Limitation:** Simic/Quandrix identity associations are evidence-required hypotheses. A close display must not present the C06 answer as proof that the player prefers Simic philosophy.

**Optional profile enrichment:** Master Planner may be offered as a reflective lens if the player says compounding across turns is itself satisfying. It did not cause placement.

**Commander discovery direction:** land/counter/creature engines with overlapping value pieces; compare against doubling, X-spell, or explicit scaling commanders.

**Vox Mana next step:** open the Simic/Quandrix Matrix comparison, then two Maze lanes: “overlapping creature or land engines” and “doubling or X-value systems.”

## 2. Mono-color — White

**Plausible observations:** the player prefers protection before a wipe, stated deal terms, visible collective pressure, and a single-color constraint when the same play pattern is available in several colors.

**Gate A-compatible state:** `primary` — Current best fit: White. No close alternative is shown unless it independently qualifies.

**Why White appeared:** the observation trace centers protection, enforceable commitments, public structure, and deliberate mono-color constraint.

**Plausible alternative:** Azorius, because holding interaction and formal commitments can also support procedure-centered White-Blue play.

**Observable distinction:** mono-White should require evidence that one-color limits are part of the desired challenge; Azorius should require procedure, planning, or held interaction beyond protection alone.

**Limitation:** the pilot has no direct mono-White discriminator other than the bounded mono/multicolor item, and that item cannot create a one-answer flip.

**Optional profile enrichment:** Vorthos can explain attachment to an order-, duty-, or community-themed deck only if the player chooses that lens.

**Commander discovery direction:** mono-White protection, tokens, equipment, recursion, or rules-setting commanders with a stated method of ending the game.

**Vox Mana next step:** compare White with Azorius and Selesnya in Matrix, then open a Maze path for “protect a shared board and recover after a wipe.”

## 3. Three-color — Bant

**Plausible observations:** the player wants a visible battlefield, prefers protection over recovery, wants the 99 to keep functioning after repeated commander removal, and likes a central commander refined by a supportive network.

**Gate A-compatible state:** `close` — Current best fit: Bant; close alternative: Azorius. This is a presentation example, not a calculated outcome.

**Why Bant appeared:** together the observations describe a worthy centerpiece made stronger by a functional, protective community rather than a commander-or-bust deck.

**Plausible alternative:** Azorius, because preparation, protection, and controlled board development overlap.

**Observable distinction:** Bant concentrates visible support behind a champion or creature-forward line; Azorius centers procedure and managed stability even when no champion is carrying the plan.

**Limitation:** Bant is high-confusion, its C05 association is unvalidated, and its strongest audit competitors include four-color and mono identities. The result must preserve “current fit” language.

**Optional profile enrichment:** Vorthos may enrich the honorable-community lens, or System Architect may enrich the support-network structure; neither determined Bant.

**Commander discovery direction:** exalted/Voltron support, creature-forward value, enchantress, blink, or protected boards whose 99 remains functional.

**Vox Mana next step:** view the Bant dossier's champion-versus-community tension, compare Azorius and Selesnya in Matrix, then explore protected-champion Maze paths.

## 4. Colorless

**Plausible observations:** the player enjoys a narrow construction pool, prefers a repeatable central plan, and chooses “make a narrow pool do more” in the Colorless/Five-Color comparison.

**Gate A-compatible state:** `insufficient` — Not enough evidence to distinguish a fit. Colorless may be offered only as a deliberate browse path, not as the named placement.

**Why no placement is named:** those observations describe constraint satisfaction but do not independently establish a Colorless behavioral identity. The explicit edge answer cannot be allowed to name the result by itself.

**Plausible alternative:** Five-Color, if later observations show the real satisfaction is making many roles cohere rather than working inside absence-of-color limits.

**Observable distinction:** narrow-pool problem solving versus breadth made coherent.

**Limitation:** Colorless has insufficient distinctiveness evidence. Artifact preference, big mana, or choosing a colorless commander cannot substitute for independent behavioral observations.

**Optional profile enrichment:** System Architect may help the player map dependencies in a colorless engine, but it cannot support Colorless placement.

**Commander discovery direction:** browse artifact, unusual-mana, big-mana, or commander-defined colorless challenges; ask which constraint is actually enjoyable before recommending a lane.

**Vox Mana next step:** neutral identity browser first, then Matrix comparison with Five-Color and Maze paths scoped by the player's stated play pattern.

## 5. Five-Color / WUBRG

**Plausible observations:** the player chooses concept over fixed breadth, wants a stable central plan with different tools, prefers multicolor role combination when the play pattern is held constant, and chooses “make a broad pool cohere” in the edge comparison.

**Gate A-compatible state:** `mixed` — broad color access is supported, but the reading does not collapse theme, toolbox preference, and engine structure into a definitive identity claim.

**Why WUBRG is plausible:** several observations point toward using many color roles in service of one integrated concept rather than collecting options without a plan.

**Plausible alternative:** a narrower multicolor identity matching the same central play pattern.

**Observable distinction:** WUBRG should express totality organized around a concept; a narrower identity should be preferred when the same Commander pattern does not need all five roles.

**Limitation:** Five-Color breadth may be driven by theme, commander rules text, available cards, or color access. The pilot cannot infer one cause from another, and no one breadth answer may name WUBRG.

**Optional profile enrichment:** Johnny/Jenny may explain concept-first expression or Vorthos may explain a five-color typal/story commitment if the player explicitly chooses those lenses.

**Commander discovery direction:** five-color typal, legends, domain, or toolbox commanders where every color has a named job; also show a narrower comparison using the same win shape.

**Vox Mana next step:** use Matrix to compare WUBRG with the most behaviorally similar narrower identity, then open paired Maze searches for the five-color and constrained versions of the concept.

## Owner-use conclusion

The walkthroughs demonstrate the intended advantage over a type label: each result exposes an observation trace, a plausible neighbor, the actual distinction, an honest limit, and a Commander discovery action. The advantage disappears if the result hides evidence gaps, treats a rank-two score as semantic adjacency, or uses profile enrichment as placement authority.
