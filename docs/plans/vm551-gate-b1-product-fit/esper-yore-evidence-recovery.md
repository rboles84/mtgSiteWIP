### 7. Smallest defensible player-facing candidate

**Scenario**

> You stopped the immediate threat and drew enough cards to choose a direction. What do you most want those new options to do?

**Answers**

1. **Set up a closing plan.** Use what you learned to choose a specific route to win, then organize your cards around it.
2. **Keep options open.** Hold several possible answers and decide after you see what the table does next.
3. **Use the opening now.** Act on the weakness you just found before it disappears.
4. **It depends on the deck.** Some of my decks commit to a route, while others stay flexible or act immediately.

Intended observation only: information-to-plan conversion posture. No answer is Esper-specific. If the future architecture cannot combine this with at least two reasonably independent observations that reach White structure and Black focused implementation, the question must not name Esper.

### 4. Accepted player observations, contradictions, limitations, and evidence roles

Accepted player evidence confirms why engine and rebuilding questions overselect:

- Players may want a stable core with varied payoffs, or a repeated finish reached through changing routes (`docs/research/placementResearch/YT-commander-personality-matrix-refined-player-evidence.md:74-88,204-216`). Neither pattern implies Yore.

- Players explicitly separate generic casting behavior from purpose: tutors may seek answers rather than combo pieces, or remain because they are on theme (`docs/research/placementResearch/YT-the-case-against-tutors-refined-player-evidence.md:33-51`).
- Combo and engine use can be a primary plan, backup, emergency closure, accidental synergy, flavor piece, or power option (`docs/research/placementResearch/YT-edhrecast-real-reason-commander-players-hate-combo-refined-player-evidence.md:1291-1317`).

It also demonstrates the methodological limit: those are System Architect and Master Planner behaviors shared across identities, not evidence that a player rejects natural surrender.

Evidence role: accepted observations prove that rebuilding, redundancy, modularity, and conversion are recognizable Commander experiences. Counterexamples prove that the same answers arise from replayability, theme, resilience, power, novelty, toolbox use, or deck maintenance. That is enough to reject a Yore mapping, not enough to create one.
