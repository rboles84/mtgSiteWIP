# EDHMatch Experience Benchmark

Benchmark snapshot: owner-supplied local capture, used read-only. This document does not reproduce its questions, answer language, result descriptions, profile names, or page structure.

## What EDHMatch does well

- **Immediate purpose.** A player knows at once that the experience concerns Commander and that a result is coming.
- **Visible pacing.** Numbered steps and progress make the remaining effort predictable.
- **Recognizable emotional choices.** Many options are easy to answer without rules expertise.
- **Satisfying reveal mechanics.** A primary result, distribution, and sharing affordance provide closure.
- **Immediate next action.** Commander-search calls to action connect the label to something a player can explore.

Archscry should match that clarity: one-sentence framing, a visible six-to-eight-question ceiling, concise options, an unmistakable result state, and a next action that is clearly about Commander discovery.

## What Archscry should deliberately avoid

- **Taxonomy conflation.** Motivation, aesthetic taste, social negotiation, mechanical appreciation, and long-term engine preference should not compete as though they were one axis.
- **Result giveaway.** An answer should not visibly announce which named result it awards.
- **Self-labeling as evidence.** Asking a player which profile they already admire may be interesting reflection, but it is not behavioral placement evidence.
- **Personality diagnosis.** Deck behavior, table preference, power, skill, budget, and internal motivation are distinct.
- **Shallow explanation.** A generic profile paragraph does not answer why this result appeared or why its neighbor did not.

## How Archscry can exceed the benchmark

- Use an adaptive six-to-eight-question route rather than a fixed nine-step accumulation.
- Capture observable Commander preferences rather than compete seven broad player types.
- Place among 37 identity expressions while preserving close, tied, mixed, and insufficient outcomes.
- Show at least two independent observations before identity-specific interpretation.
- Explain the trace: what the player chose, the primary fit, the nearest plausible alternative, and the observation that separates them.
- Offer an identity-specific Commander direction, then connect to the dossier, Matrix, Maze, or recommendation surface.
- Offer Atlas/profile enrichment as optional context after placement, never as the cause of placement.

## Concrete implications

1. The opening screen should state the six-to-eight-question ceiling and that answers describe a preferred Commander pattern, not personality.
2. Each question should fit on one decision surface: prompt, short answer title, and plain explanatory sentence.
3. The result reveal should lead with the observed pattern and limitation before atmospheric identity language.
4. A nearby alternative should be useful only when the result can name the separating observation; rank two is not automatically semantic adjacency.
5. Every result needs a Commander exploration action. A general “learn more” link is insufficient.
6. Uncertainty must remain a public result state. A confident-sounding label cannot substitute for missing independent evidence.
