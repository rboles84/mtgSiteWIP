# Ink / Altruism — RGWU Four-Color Identity Research Report
## Vox Mana Source Packet

*Research conducted June 2026. All card data verified against Scryfall and Gatherer.*

---

## Executive Summary

- **RGWU** (Red, Green, White, Blue) is the four-color identity defined by the **absence of Black** — the color of self-interest, ambition, and personal power-seeking.
- The combination is colloquially known as **"Ink"** after Ink-Treader Nephilim (Guildpact, 2006), and officially themed as **"Altruism"** in Commander 2016, where Kynaios and Tiro of Meletis served as the primary commander of a group-hug precon deck.
- Neither "Ink" nor "Altruism" is a universally official faction name; "Altruism" is the Commander 2016 deck theme label, adopted by Scryfall as a searchable nickname. "Ink" is player shorthand derived from the Nephilim name.
- The philosophical core of RGWU is **radical cooperation**: passion without cruelty, growth without hoarding, community without coercion, and knowledge without gatekeeping — because the one force that would turn any of those into private advantage has been removed.
- The three primary RGWU legendary commanders by EDHREC deck count are Aragorn, the Uniter (21,000+ decks), Omnath, Locus of Creation (17,800+ decks), and Kynaios and Tiro of Meletis (12,100+ decks).
- RGWU cards mechanically concentrate around **landfall, shared resource distribution, spell-copying, and triggered-ability stacking** — patterns that produce communal board states rather than private extraction.
- The absence of Black removes tutoring, targeted discard, sacrifice-for-value, reanimation, and "pay life for power" effects — mechanics that express private ambition in Magic's design system.

---

## Identity Thesis

**RGWU is the worldview of abundance shared rather than captured: passion, nature, order, and knowledge arranged in a circle that has no closing wall.**

The missing Black pressure is the thesis: when a system contains emotion, growth, community, and reason but has removed the engine of individual ambition, what emerges is not weakness but a different kind of strength — one that expresses itself through generosity, public prosperity, cooperative momentum, and an ethics of giving as strategy rather than sacrifice.

## Historical Basis

### The Nephilim Cycle — Guildpact (2006)

The five Nephilim were printed in *Guildpact* (released February 2006) as the first and, for a decade, only four-color cards in Magic history. They were printed as non-legendary Creatures of the type "Nephilim," each costing exactly one mana of each of their four colors.

**Key design context:** Mark Rosewater has stated publicly that four-color cards are extremely difficult to design because there is minimal mechanical space shared by four colors simultaneously. The Nephilim were unusual design experiments, not a supported framework, and no new Nephilim have been printed since Guildpact. Rosewater has indicated he would not retroactively errata them to legendary status.

The RGWU Nephilim is **Ink-Treader Nephilim** (Guildpact #117, Rare).

**Card data (verified Scryfall):**
> {R}{G}{W}{U} — Creature — Nephilim — 3/3
> *Whenever a player casts an instant or sorcery spell, if that spell targets only Ink-Treader Nephilim, copy the spell for each other creature that spell could target. Each copy targets a different one of those creatures.*
> Flavor text: *"When it awoke, the mirrors of the world reflected only darkness."*

The mechanic is a **mandatory, table-wide spell replication engine**. Any targeted spell that hits Ink-Treader propagates to every creature that spell could legally target. The controller receives copies of even opponent-cast spells. The effect is inherently communal: one targeted action becomes distributed across the entire battlefield.

**On the name "Ink":** The card name is "Ink-Treader Nephilim," not "Ink." Players and community resources use "Ink" as shorthand derived from this card name — it is **not an official faction name**. No Wizards publication designates this combination as "Ink" as a formal identity. Scryfall does not use "Ink" as a search keyword; it uses "altruism."

**Symbolic reading of "Ink"** (interpretive, not official): Ink is the medium by which knowledge is copied and distributed. A text written in ink can be reproduced, shared, given away without the original losing its value. The Ink-Treader's mechanic — copying an action and replicating it across every valid target — resonates with the idea of open transmission: one act becomes many, one spell becomes a library. The flavor text's reference to mirrors and darkness suggests a creature that reflects rather than absorbs, redistributes rather than consumes. Whether this reading informed the original design is unconfirmed; it is a plausible interpretive frame.

### Commander 2016 and Kynaios and Tiro of Meletis

Commander 2016 (released November 2016) was Magic's first product to produce four-color legendary creatures, filling the design gap left by the non-legendary Nephilim. Each of the five precon decks was built around a four-color combination and given a thematic identity label.

**Official Commander 2016 theme labels** (confirmed via MTG Wiki and Wizards product documentation):
| Combination | Excluded color | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| WUBR | Green | Artifice |
| UBRG | White | Chaos |
| BRGW | Blue | Aggression |
| **RGWU** | **Black** | **Altruism** |
| GWUB | Red | Growth |

"Altruism" is the official theme name for the RGWU Commander 2016 deck. Scryfall uses these theme names as searchable color identity nicknames (confirmed via Scryfall search syntax documentation: *"the four-color nicknames chaos, aggression, altruism, growth, artifice are supported"*). These are semi-official: Wizards used them as product design labels; Scryfall adopted them as search keywords; they are not canonical in-universe faction names.

**Kynaios and Tiro of Meletis** (Commander 2016 #36, Mythic Rare):

> {R}{G}{W}{U} — Legendary Creature — Human Soldier — 2/8
> *At the beginning of your end step, draw a card. Each player may put a land card from their hand onto the battlefield, then each opponent who didn't draws a card.*
> Flavor text: *"Look what we fought for. Look what we built together."*

**Mechanical analysis:** The card guarantees card draw for the controller, then offers each player a free land drop, and compensates any player who doesn't take the land with a card draw. The effect is **symmetrical in structure but asymmetrical in impact**: a player who built their deck to exploit land drops (ramp synergies, landfall triggers) benefits disproportionately from the same effect given to everyone. This is the core mechanical philosophy of group-hug in MTG — generosity as leverage.

**Lore basis** (MTG Wiki / Wizards lore source, confirmed): Kynaios and Tiro were legendary human soldiers on the plane of Theros. They were lovers who led a rebellion against the tyrant Agnomakhos, an immortal archon who had ruled the region for generations. With the aid of the god Ephara, who granted mortals magic, they overthrew Agnomakhos, founded the polis of Meletis as a *"beacon of freedom and enlightenment,"* and were chosen by the people as its guardians. Critically: after ruling according to the highest philosophical and ethical principles, they **voluntarily relinquished power** and established a philosopher-led republic. Two great statues — the Guardians of Meletis — were built in their honor.

They are based on the Athenian lovers Harmodius and Aristogeiton, the Tyrannicides who assassinated the Athenian tyrant Hipparchus in 514 BCE.

This lore is the perfect RGWU character archetype: warriors who fought not for conquest but for liberation, who built not an empire but a city of free thought, and who — most importantly — gave up their power once it had served its purpose. The flavor text "Look what we fought for. Look what we built together" encodes a plural, collective triumph, not a personal one.

### Later RGWU Four-Color Representatives

#### Omnath, Locus of Creation (Zendikar Rising, 2020)
> {R}{G}{W}{U} — Legendary Creature — Elemental — 4/4
> *When Omnath enters, draw a card. Landfall — Whenever a land you control enters, you gain 4 life if this is the first time this ability has resolved this turn. If it's the second time, add {R}{G}{W}{U}. If it's the third time, Omnath deals 4 damage to each opponent and each planeswalker you don't control.*

Mechanics: enter-the-battlefield draw, layered landfall rewards (life → mana → damage), four-color mana generation. Banned in Standard and Historic post-release due to power level. **Identity relevance:** Omnath escalates through natural abundance (land drops) rather than resource denial. Growth generates power; nothing is taken from opponents until the third land — a significant buildup before aggression.

#### Aragorn, the Uniter (The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth, 2023)
> {R}{G}{W}{U} — Legendary Creature — Human Noble — 5/5
> *Whenever you cast a white spell, create a 1/1 white Human Soldier token. Whenever you cast a blue spell, scry 2. Whenever you cast a red spell, Aragorn deals 3 damage to target opponent. Whenever you cast a green spell, target creature gets +4/+4 until end of turn.*

Each of the four colors triggers a distinct effect. The most popular RGWU Commander by EDHREC deck count (21,473 decks as of June 2026). **Identity relevance:** Aragorn is explicitly a unifier — the card's name encodes the identity. Each color contributes its distinct strength, triggered by its own spells. The card rewards playing all four colors without Black's extractive tools.

#### The Fourteenth Doctor (Secret Lair, 2023)
> {R/G}{W}{U} — Legendary Creature — Time Lord Doctor — 3/4
> *When you cast this spell, reveal the top fourteen cards of your library. Put all Doctor cards revealed this way into your graveyard and the rest on the bottom of your library in a random order. You may have The Fourteenth Doctor enter as a copy of a Doctor card in your graveyard that was put there from your library this turn. If you do, it gains haste until end of turn.*

**Color identity note:** The mana cost uses the hybrid symbol {R/G}, which means the card's color identity includes R, G, W, and U — full RGWU. Designer Gavin Verhey confirmed this card was intentionally built so that it can serve as the Commander for a deck containing all other Doctor cards. **Identity relevance:** The Fourteenth Doctor copies identity rather than originating it — it becomes whoever it needs to be. The mechanic of revealing knowledge (the library) and transforming through what is found resonates with the "Ink" reading of shared and transmitted identity.

### Four-Color Naming Context

No universally official proper names exist for the five four-color combinations in Magic. The naming landscape:

1. **Nephilim names** (player shorthand, derived from card names): Ink (RGWU), Yore (WUBR), Glint (UBRG), Dune (BRGW), Witch (GWUB). These are community-created shorthand, not official MTG designations.
2. **Commander 2016 theme names**: Altruism (RGWU), Artifice (WUBR), Chaos (UBRG), Aggression (BRGW), Growth (GWUB). These are official product labels used by Wizards for the precon decks and adopted by Scryfall as search keywords.
3. **Scryfall syntax**: Accepts "altruism" as a color identity search term for RGWU (documented in Scryfall search syntax reference).

For Vox Mana purposes: both "Ink" and "Altruism" are usable labels with distinct resonances. "Ink" is evocative and player-derived; "Altruism" is the closest to an official product designation.

## Included Color Contributions

### Red
Red contributes passion, freedom, impulse, and direct action. In RGWU, Red is the force that keeps the system from becoming cold or mechanical. It provides emotional honesty — the capacity to feel strongly and act on feeling without manipulation. Red brings speed, aggression when needed, and the quality of responding immediately to the present moment. Mechanically: burn, haste, combat damage triggers, chaos effects. Philosophically: Red in RGWU is the energy that animates cooperation, not the rage that breaks it.

### Green
Green contributes growth, nature, tradition, and abundance. It is the engine of resource generation: ramp, land acceleration, large creatures, creature synergy. Green believes the natural order provides enough for everyone if not interrupted. In RGWU, Green feeds the communal table — it is the garden, the forest, the mana base. It gives the philosophy of Altruism its material foundation: you can afford to be generous because the land produces enough. Mechanically: landfall synergies, ramp spells, +1/+1 counters, creature buffs.

### White
White contributes order, law, community, and selflessness. It provides the structure within which Red's passion and Green's abundance operate constructively. White cares about the collective, not the individual — rules exist to protect everyone, not to elevate any one person. In RGWU, White is the social contract: the agreement that makes giving meaningful because it is protected by shared norms. Mechanically: life gain, tokens, protection, pillow-fort effects, symmetrical wraths when needed.

### Blue
Blue contributes knowledge, logic, perfection, and control. It is the intellect of the combination — the capacity to observe, plan, and understand. In RGWU, Blue is the open library: knowledge gathered not to be hoarded but to be distributed. Blue provides the card draw, the scry, the information advantage that allows the pilot to benefit most from the table's shared growth. Mechanically: card draw, counterspells (defensive), scry, copying effects.

## Excluded Color Analysis

### What Black Would Normally Provide

According to MTG's color pie design (documented extensively by Mark Rosewater):
- Targeted discard (attacking opponents' hands)
- Tutoring (finding the exact card needed by paying any price)
- Reanimation (reclaiming value from death)
- Sacrifice-for-value mechanics (losing small things for large gains)
- "Pay life for power" trade (personal resource exploitation)
- Creature removal through destruction/exile with no restriction
- Ambition-driven card advantage (drawing cards at a cost)
- Willingness to exploit others for personal gain
- Ruthless pragmatism: doing whatever is necessary to win

### What Black's Absence Creates

Removing Black from RGWU does not create a naive or toothless identity. It creates a **structurally different relationship to victory**. The result:

**No extraction.** RGWU cannot drain, discard, or force opponents to lose resources by targeted action. It cannot tutor for the perfect answer. This means the philosophy cannot win by impoverishing others.

**No sacrifice economy.** There is no mechanism to trade small values for large ones at someone else's expense. Growth must be genuine, not engineered through exploitation of weaker pieces.

**No private information control.** Black gives knowledge as a personal weapon (tutoring, hand attack). RGWU's knowledge mechanisms (Blue's scry, draw, search) tend toward *public* or *symmetric* information.

**Communal momentum as the only engine.** Without Black's self-advancement tools, the only way RGWU scales is by growing the collective resource pool and positioning itself to benefit most from that growth. This is **generosity as asymmetric strategy**: give everyone land drops, but have more landfall triggers than anyone else. Give everyone card draw, but have built more win conditions per card than anyone else.

**Philosophical consequence.** The worldview that emerges is not sainthood — it is a genuine system where sharing is not idealism but mechanism. Black asks "what can I take?" RGWU asks "what happens if I give this to everyone?" The RGWU player may still win by more than anyone else — but they win *alongside* a table that grew, not *over* a table that was dismantled.

**Failure mode of Black's absence.** Without Black's self-sufficiency tools, RGWU can be *too* dependent on the table not developing threats. Group-hug builds that give resources symmetrically may accelerate an opponent who wins before the RGWU player can benefit. The design tension is real: Black's absence removes private insurance.

## Mechanical Identity

### Group-Hug and Symmetrical Benefits
The defining mechanical pattern of the RGWU archetype. Effects that give resources to all players, not just the pilot. Kynaios and Tiro embody this: land drops for everyone, cards for everyone. The strategic depth is in *how much more you benefit from the same effect*. This is not charity; it is efficient scaling.

**EDHREC data (community metagame, not official):** As of June 2026, Kynaios and Tiro of Meletis has 12,132 registered Commander decks on EDHREC. The top archetype tags are: Group Hug (1,400+ decks), Wheels (616 decks), Lands Matter (284 decks), Pillow Fort (189 decks). This confirms the community reads the card primarily as a group-hug anchor.

### Communal Card Draw and Ramp
Blue provides card draw; Green provides ramp; White provides life buffer; Red provides speed. All four can be expressed symmetrically. Wheel effects (discard hand, draw new hand — for everyone) are a strong RGWU pattern because they reset information parity while the pilot's deck is built to benefit more from the reset. Land-based ramp given to all players is similarly asymmetric in practice.

### Political Protection and Table Management
Without Black's direct threat-elimination tools, RGWU must use **social dynamics** as a defensive layer. The player who is giving everyone resources is politically expensive to attack: why destroy the person giving you cards and land drops? This is a pillow-fort through goodwill rather than enchantments alone — though enchantment-based protection (Ghostly Prison effects) fits the identity.

### Shared Growth and Resource Acceleration
RGWU strategies often produce explosive collective board development. Omnath, Locus of Creation's landfall chain generates life, mana, and damage across a single turn cycle of land plays. Aragorn, the Uniter triggers four distinct effects per spell color. The identity rewards playing a wide variety of card types and colors — a naturally communal deck construction philosophy.

### Spell-Copying and Reflection
Ink-Treader Nephilim is the extreme expression of this pattern, but the mechanic of copying actions and distributing them table-wide is RGWU-flavored. An effect applied to one becomes an effect applied to all. Mirrors, reflection, distributed transmission.

### Pillow-Fort and Defensive Diplomacy
When not winning through raw resource advantage, RGWU plays defense through politics and passive protection (taxing effects, life totals). The archetype does not want to be the threat; it wants to be the neutral ground that survives long enough for its abundance to overwhelm.

### Commander Archetypes (EDHREC-sourced, community data)
- **Group Hug (Kynaios and Tiro primary):** Maximizes symmetrical resource giving; wins by having more payoffs per shared resource.
- **Landfall (Omnath primary):** Chains land plays into massive triggered advantage; Red/Green ramp, White life buffer, Blue draw.
- **Legends Matter / Color-Triggered (Aragorn primary):** Rewards casting spells of each color; triggers stack in a single turn.
- **Doctor Tribal (Fourteenth Doctor):** Creature type synergy, copying, transmutation of identity.
- **Wheels (Kynaios secondary):** Mass card draw reset; Teferi's Puzzle Box, Howling Mine, etc.

## Philosophical Identity

### Values
- Shared prosperity over private accumulation
- Knowledge as a public good, freely distributed
- Power exercised in service of the community, not over it
- Emotion and passion as engines of cooperation, not domination
- Growth that makes the whole ecosystem larger, not just one position
- The strength of being trusted: opponents don't attack those who feed them

### Fears
- Being exploited: giving to someone who takes everything and wins
- The table developing faster than expected
- Having no private recourse when the cooperative strategy fails
- Becoming the table's resource engine rather than its beneficiary
- Overextension: too much given, not enough returned

### Strengths
- Political longevity: opponents hesitate before attacking their benefactor
- Explosive scaling through collective momentum
- Diversity of win conditions spread across four colors
- Emotional intelligence: reading the table, managing relationships
- Difficult to identify as a threat until it is already winning

### Failure Modes
- Accelerating an opponent who wins before RGWU can capitalize
- No tutoring means no guaranteed answers to combo threats
- Symmetrical resource gain can backfire against faster strategies
- Group dynamics do not scale in true 1v1 environments
- Can be perceived as naive by experienced players and targeted early as a "table accelerator"

### Internal Contradictions
The central contradiction of RGWU is that **generosity is also self-serving**. Giving resources to opponents in order to be politically safe is not pure altruism — it is asymmetric strategy wearing the costume of altruism. This is not a weakness; it is the most interesting philosophical tension in the identity. The Ink/Altruism player does not need to resolve this contradiction. They live inside it. They give because giving *is* the strategy, and the fact that strategy and ethics align is not hypocrisy — it is the design of the system.

## Creative Direction

### Visual Motifs
- **Open libraries:** shelves of illuminated manuscripts accessible to all, unrestricted reading rooms, catalogued scroll vaults without locks
- **Communal tables:** long harvest tables shared by many, festival banquets where the host is just another guest
- **River monasteries and teaching halls:** knowledge flowing downstream, students becoming teachers without leaving
- **Ink flowing into irrigation canals:** the image from the Nephilim translated literally — written knowledge feeding living systems
- **Mirror pools that dissolve the individual:** reflection as identity dissolution; the self becomes part of the collective image
- **Civic observatories:** astronomical knowledge shared with the public; the sky belongs to everyone who looks
- **Seed vaults open to all:** abundance stored and distributed, not hoarded; the future fed by the past's gifts
- **Treaties written for future generations:** agreements that outlast the individual signers; ink that matters after the author is gone

### Environmental Motifs
- Floodplain cities built at the confluence of rivers (multiple currents meeting and distributing)
- Terraced gardens that share water through gravity rather than ownership
- Crossroads settlements where travelers leave what they no longer need
- Archipelagos connected by open waterways — no single island controls the passage
- Ancient forests with wide clearings used as gathering grounds

### Character / Society Motifs
- The teacher who gives away all their methods rather than preserving a monopoly on knowledge
- The guardian who steps down when the crisis ends (Kynaios and Tiro as archetype)
- The cartographer who publishes maps rather than keeping routes secret
- The healer who trains others to the point of making themselves unnecessary
- The leader who measures success by how many people no longer need them
- A society without guilds that restrict entry — crafts taught openly

### Avoided Motifs (Would Feel Too Black)
- Secret knowledge, esoteric traditions, and mystery schools
- Hierarchical access to wisdom (initiates vs. outsiders)
- Leaders who cultivate dependence to secure their own position
- Archives with forbidden sections
- Prosperity that explicitly requires others to have less
- Protection that comes from fear rather than affection

### Website Hero Background Ideas
- Deep teal-to-amber gradient suggesting twilight over a still lake, with faint ink-diffusion patterns
- Illustrated topographic lines suggesting shared terrain — open land, no boundaries
- A slow-motion animation of ink spreading through water
- Fractalized mirror pools in tetracolor (red, green, white, blue reflections overlapping)
- A soft starfield with a warm ground below — the sky free, the land abundant
- Dense, flowing calligraphy that dissolves at the edges into color

## Comparison to Other Four-Color Identities

### WUBR / Yore / Artifice (excluding Green)
Without Green, there is no natural abundance, no ramp from the land, no trust in organic growth. Artifice builds structure through intelligence, craft, and manufactured systems — it imposes order on matter rather than cooperating with it. RGWU *grows*; Artifice *constructs*. RGWU distributes; Artifice optimizes. The emotional register of Artifice is colder — Blue/White/Black/Red is a system of precision and ambition; it has Black's extraction engine at its core.

### UBRG / Glint / Chaos (excluding White)
Without White, there is no social contract, no communal structure, no protection of the collective. Chaos has Black's ambition, Red's impulse, Green's wildness, and Blue's cunning — but no order. It does not seek shared benefit; it seeks unpredictable advantage. RGWU is cooperative where Glint is anarchic. Glint may produce wild table effects, but they serve the individual's entertainment or advantage, not the table's wellbeing.

### BRGW / Dune / Aggression (excluding Blue)
Without Blue, there is no cool reflection, no accumulated knowledge, no capacity to observe before acting. Aggression has Black's hunger, Red's fire, Green's physical force, and White's martial order — but no wisdom. It is a powerful, forward-leaning combination that wins through force rather than through the patient accumulation RGWU favors. Dune can give too (Green/White share tokens and anthem effects) but with Black's extraction and no Blue's foresight, the giving is always in service of immediate pressure.

### GWUB / Witch / Growth (excluding Red)
Without Red, there is no passion, no immediacy, no emotional directness. Growth (GWUB) is patient, strategic, and deliberate — it grows through counters, life manipulation, and carefully controlled resource accumulation. It has Black's private tools and Blue's knowledge, which makes it more individually self-sufficient than RGWU. But it lacks RGWU's warmth. The absence of Red removes the fire from cooperation — Growth is cool where Altruism is warm. Growth may give when it serves a cold calculation; Altruism gives because giving *is the system*.

## Vox Mana Dossier Draft

### Identity Line
RGWU is the worldview of those who discovered that generosity, applied with precision, is the most powerful force at the table.

### Worldview
Ink / Altruism holds that the world grows largest when no one holds the walls closed. Remove the instinct to hoard — to secure advantage by denying it to others — and what remains is not innocence but a different kind of strategy: one in which passion fuels cooperation, nature sustains everyone equally, order protects the collective rather than any individual, and knowledge flows outward rather than accumulating in private hands. This is not idealism. It is the design of a system that has calculated: *the table's growth is my growth; the table's abundance is my foundation.* What looks like sacrifice is asymmetric leverage. What looks like altruism is an architecture.

### Play Pattern
RGWU decks develop through communal momentum. They give opponents land drops and card draw, accelerating the whole table — while having built more payoffs per accelerated resource than anyone else. They win politically before they win mechanically: the opponent who could stop them hesitates, because they've been receiving benefits. When the critical mass arrives — a third landfall trigger, a board full of triggered abilities, a wheel that resets hands — the generosity reveals itself as infrastructure. The deck does not race to win; it engineers a situation in which winning is the natural conclusion of a process everyone participated in.

### Visual Identity
RGWU's aesthetic is the **open archive at golden hour**: warm light through tall windows, stone worn smooth by generations of use, ink stains on wooden tables that have hosted a thousand conversations. The palette is amber, living green, warm white, and the deep teal of still water before sunrise. Textures are natural and worn — river stone, vellum, old wood. Ornament is civic, not personal: carvings of maps and astronomical charts, not crests or heraldry. The space feels shared. There are no private rooms.

### Keyword Tags
`cooperative-abundance` · `open-knowledge` · `asymmetric-generosity` · `communal-momentum` · `earned-trust`

### What This Identity Is Not
1. **Not naive:** RGWU is not unaware of its own advantage in giving. The generosity is structural, not sentimental.
2. **Not pacifist:** Red's aggression and Green's force are present. When combat matters, this identity fights. It simply fights *last*, when it is most advantaged.
3. **Not egalitarian without strategy:** Giving everyone the same resources when you have more payoffs is not equality — it is sophisticated leverage.
4. **Not communal at the expense of identity:** RGWU does not dissolve the self; it builds a self whose prosperity *is* the community's prosperity.
5. **Not a support role:** This is a win condition, not a sidekick. Group-hug decks that never win have misread the archetype.

## Open Questions / Weak Evidence

**1. Origin of "Ink" as RGWU shorthand**
The community usage of "Ink" as the RGWU four-color name is clearly derived from "Ink-Treader Nephilim," but no definitive primary source documents when this usage originated or who first applied it. It appears consistently in community discussions but should be distinguished from official naming at all times.

**2. Symbolic intent behind Ink-Treader's design**
Whether the name "Ink-Treader" was chosen to suggest the philosophical resonances (knowledge transmission, copying, public record) or was purely a flavor-name for a Ravnican creature type is undocumented. Mark Rosewater's design notes for the Nephilim cycle (if any are publicly available) do not appear to address this specifically. The symbolic reading in this report is **interpretive inference**, not confirmed designer intent.

**3. Complete list of RGWU-identity cards**
The five anchors (Ink-Treader, Kynaios and Tiro, Omnath, Aragorn, Fourteenth Doctor) are confirmed. A full count of all cards with RGWU color identity (including non-legendary multicolor and cards with RGWU symbols in text boxes) would require a live Scryfall query (`id:rgwu` or `id=altruism`). This report does not claim a complete count of such cards.

**4. Guildpact Nephilim creature type significance**
The "Nephilim" creature type and its lore significance within the Ravnica setting is not deeply developed in official Magic lore. The theological resonance (Nephilim as the children of divine and mortal union in various mythological traditions) is potentially rich but not confirmed as a design reference by Wizards.

**5. Commander 2016 "Altruism" as officially endorsed identity name**
While Wizards used "Altruism" as a product theme label, there is no confirmed statement from Wizards that this is intended as the permanent canonical identity name for RGWU. Scryfall's adoption of it as a search keyword is the most authoritative practical source. Players, designers, and community resources use it widely; its semi-official status is clear but not absolute.

**6. Red's mechanical representation in Kynaios and Tiro**
Community commentary has noted that Red is underrepresented in Kynaios and Tiro's actual card mechanics — the card's abilities (land drops, draw) read more White/Blue/Green than Red. The MTGSalvation comment "I don't feel like R is represented at all in its abilities" is a noted community observation. The counter-argument (supported by one community source) is that four-color identities are best understood through what color is *absent* rather than through individual color slice analysis — making Black's absence more meaningful than Red's presence. This is a reasonable design philosophy reading but is interpretive, not official.
