# Would Magic Players Want to Only Play Colorless?

Yes, some Magic: The Gathering players specifically choose to play only colorless decks, though it is a niche and challenging playstyle.

In Commander, this can happen naturally when the player chooses a colorless commander. Wizards’ Commander format page explains that a Commander deck’s cards must fit the commander’s color identity, and color identity can come from mana symbols anywhere on the card. Colorless cards are allowed in Commander decks generally, but a colorless commander does not allow colored color identities.

Source:
- Wizards — Commander Format: https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats/commander

## Why Players Choose Colorless

### Unique Challenge

Building without the five colors requires creative deckbuilding.

A strict colorless deck gives up the normal colored tools for removal, draw, tutors, protection, recursion, and stack interaction. That makes the restriction itself part of the appeal.

### Flavor and Lore

Fans of Eldrazi, artifacts, Wastes, and alien or artificial aesthetics may enjoy the thematic feel of colorless.

Important source note:
- Magic has official five-color philosophy support.
- Colorless has strong mechanical and aesthetic expressions, especially Eldrazi and artifacts, but it is not officially presented as a sixth color philosophy equivalent to white, blue, black, red, and green.

Sources:
- Wizards — Let’s Talk Color Pie: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/lets-talk-color-pie
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28

### Format Legalities

In Commander, choosing a colorless legendary creature as commander means the deck cannot include cards with colored color identity.

This does not mean every artifact or land is automatically legal. Some artifacts and lands have colored mana symbols or other color-identity markers that matter in Commander.

Source:
- Wizards — Commander Format: https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats/commander

### Mana Flexibility

Many colorless cards, artifacts, and lands are reusable across many decks.

Correction:
- The safer claim is “many,” not “all.”
- Commander color identity still restricts cards with colored mana symbols.
- Colorless mana is a type of mana, not a sixth color.

Sources:
- Wizards — Commander Format: https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats/commander
- Wizards — Catching Up on Commander Changes: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/catching-commander-changes-2017-08-15

## Major Downsides

### High Financial Cost

Top-tier colorless lands and powerful artifacts are often expensive.

This is a metagame / market observation rather than a rules fact. It should be checked against current card prices when writing deck-buying advice.

### Weak Interaction

Colorless decks often struggle with cheap, efficient, instant-speed removal and stack interaction compared to blue, white, black, red, and green decks.

This is a deckbuilding tendency, not an absolute rule.

### Limited Card Pool

A strict colorless build gives up a large majority of Magic’s colored card pool.

Correction:
- Do not use “95% of Magic’s history” unless you run a current database query.
- Use “a large majority of the card pool” unless exact math is needed.

### Slow Starts

Colorless decks often rely on mana rocks, utility lands, Tron-style mana engines, or other ramp packages before they can deploy major threats.

## Popular Colorless Strategies

### Eldrazi Tribal

Casting massive alien monsters such as Ulamog, Kozilek, and other Eldrazi threats.

Source:
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28

### Artifact MUD / Stax

Using artifact mana, taxing pieces, prison effects, and lock pieces to constrain opponents.

Examples often associated with this space include cards like Lodestone Golem, Trinisphere, and Chalice of the Void, depending on format legality.

Source note:
- This is archetype language. Always check format legality before recommending specific cards.

### Tron

Assembling Urza’s Mine, Urza’s Power Plant, and Urza’s Tower to generate seven mana quickly.

This is a real colorless-mana engine, though Tron decks are not always strictly colorless.

# Colorless Philosophy vs. Colored Lore

In Magic lore and design writing, color identity usually represents worldview, philosophy, and emotional driver. Removing color changes the player’s relationship to the game’s normal color-pie language.

Source:
- Wizards — Let’s Talk Color Pie: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/lets-talk-color-pie

## The Blind Eternities / Colorless

Colorless most often reads through two broad Magic expressions:

- Distant and cosmic: Eldrazi, Wastes, void, alien scale, reality outside normal life
- Artificial and cold: artifacts, machines, construction, automation, utility

This is a design interpretation, not a single official colorless philosophy.

## Colorless Lore: The Void and the Machine

A careful sourced version:

Colorless is best treated as a mechanical and aesthetic absence from the five-color system rather than a fully official sixth philosophy. In practice, Magic often expresses colorless through artifacts, Eldrazi, Wastes, generic utility, and effects that sit outside normal color-pie commitments. For a profile system, colorless can represent distance, abstraction, machinery, alien scale, or refusal of the emotional and ecological commitments represented by the five colors.

Sources:
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Prerelease Primer: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-of-the-gatewatch-prerelease-primer
- Wizards — Catching Up on Commander Changes: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/catching-commander-changes-2017-08-15

## Design Governance: The Colorless Counselor

There is a concrete, official reason colorless feels "almost like a color" without being one.

Wizards maintains an internal body called the Council of Colors that reviews every set to keep cards consistent with color-pie philosophy. It is built around one counselor per color — but it also includes a dedicated colorless counselor, making colorless the only non-color with a permanent seat at that table.

What this supports:
- Colorless is treated *almost like a color* at the level of design oversight. It gets its own steward and its own consistency review.
- It is still handled as separate from the five. A colorless seat next to five color seats is, structurally, an admission that colorless sits outside the pie rather than inside it.

What this does NOT support:
- It does not mean colorless has a color-pie philosophy. The counselor governs colorless *mechanics and consistency*, not a worldview maxim. Rosewater has stated colorless has no inherent philosophy and no slice of the pie.

Canon tier:
- Confirmed (design governance). The Council and the colorless counselor are described directly by Wizards.
- The interpretation that this "almost-a-color" governance still does not grant a philosophy is supported by Rosewater's own Q&A statements but is framed here as synthesis built on those statements.

Sources:
- Wizards — The Council of Colors, Revisited: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/the-council-of-colors-revisited
- Rosewater (Blogatog) — colorless has no slice of the pie: https://markrosewater.tumblr.com/post/665510193750310912/is-the-colorless-part-of-the-color-pie-documented

## Mono-Color Lore: The Purists

Mono-colored decks represent dedication to a single fundamental truth.

Examples:
- Mono-white: order, structure, peace, community, law
- Mono-blue: knowledge, improvement, perfection, control
- Mono-black: ambition, self-interest, power, opportunity
- Mono-red: emotion, freedom, impulse, action
- Mono-green: growth, nature, acceptance, interdependence

Source:
- Wizards — Let’s Talk Color Pie: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/lets-talk-color-pie

## Guild Lore: Societal Dynamics

Two-color pairings represent tensions and cooperation between two color philosophies.

For example, a two-color identity can feel more social, institutional, practical, or ideological than a single-color identity because it balances two drives instead of one.

Source:
- Wizards — Let’s Talk Color Pie: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/lets-talk-color-pie

## Shard / Wedge Lore: Biomes and Cultures

Three-color combinations often represent more complex identities, cultures, ecosystems, or factions than mono-color or two-color identities.

Examples include Tarkir clans and other factional identities where three colors produce a richer cultural shape.

Source note:
- This is a franchise-pattern observation. For specific factions, source each setting/faction separately.

## What Choosing Colorless Means

Choosing colorless can be framed as rejecting the emotional, ideological, or ecological dynamics of the five-color system.

A careful version:
- You are not choosing “a sixth color.”
- You are choosing an identity outside the normal color-pie commitments.
- The resulting profile can point toward cosmic horror, artifact logic, automation, neutrality, austerity, or anti-color gameplay.

# Psychological Profile: Who Is the Colorless Player?

Using Wizards of the Coast’s psychographic player profiles — Timmy/Tammy, Johnny/Jenny, and Spike — colorless can map to several different player motivations.

Canon tier:
- Confirmed (Wizards): the psychographic profiles themselves — Timmy/Tammy, Johnny/Jenny, Spike — are official design vocabulary.
- Synthesis (Vox Mana inference): the specific mapping of each profile onto colorless gameplay (Johnny = artifact machine, Timmy = Eldrazi spectacle, Spike = denial/clean mana) is interpretive. Wizards has not published a "colorless player profile." Treat this section as a useful design model, not confirmed canon.

Source:
- Wizards — The Three Magic Psychographics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/the-three-magic-psychographics

## 1. The “Mad Scientist” Johnny / Jenny

### Motivation

Creative self-expression through restriction.

### Why Colorless Fits

Johnny/Jenny may see the colorless restriction as a puzzle. Building without colored tools forces the player to solve problems through artifacts, unusual engines, recursion loops, and nonstandard card interactions.

### Strategy

High-synergy artifact loops, resource engines, and strange alternate win conditions.

## 2. The “Cosmic Horror” Vorthos

### Motivation

Deep immersion in story, flavor, aesthetic, and narrative consistency.

### Why Colorless Fits

Vorthos may be drawn to a battlefield that feels barren, alien, or outside ordinary life. Eldrazi decks especially support the feeling of being an outside threat that does not belong to the plane it is attacking.

Source:
- Wizards — Melvin and Vorthos: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/melvin-and-vorthos-2007-05-07-0

### Strategy

Eldrazi tribal, Wastes-heavy mana bases, All Is Dust-style one-sided color punishment, and large cosmic threats.

## 3. The “Inflexible Architect” Spike

### Motivation

Efficiency, optimization, and controlling variance.

### Why Colorless Fits

Spike may value colorless because it avoids traditional color screw in strict colorless decks. If every mana source can cast the deck’s spells, the mana base can feel mathematically clean.

Correction:
- This does not mean all colorless decks are more consistent overall.
- Colorless decks can still suffer from curve, ramp, matchup, and interaction problems.

### Strategy

Artifact prison, Eldrazi Tron, MUD, fast mana, taxing effects, and early lock pieces.

# Deep-Dive Existential Questions for Colorless Players

## Does choosing Colorless mean you love Artifacts, or do you love Wastes?

This is a major internal flavor split.

One camp loves the industrial, constructed, machine-like, Urza/Mishra/artifact feel. Another camp prefers alien, barren, biological, or cosmic void energy associated with Eldrazi and Wastes.

This is not a rules distinction, but it matters for profile design.

## Is a Devoid card truly colorless?

Mechanically, yes: a card with devoid is colorless.

Official rulings reinforce the precision here: a card with devoid is *just* colorless — it is not "colorless and the colors of its mana cost." If another effect gives a devoid card a color, it becomes only that new color, not that color plus colorless. Devoid also works in all zones, not only on the battlefield. (Ulalek, Fused Atrocity rulings, 2024-06-07.)

Commander color identity, however, still looks at colored mana symbols in the card’s cost and rules text. That means many devoid cards are not legal in a strictly colorless Commander deck.

Sources:
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28
- Wizards — Commander Format: https://magic.wizards.com/en/formats/commander
- Scryfall — Ulalek, Fused Atrocity (official rulings, 2024-06-07): https://scryfall.com/card/m3c/4/ulalek-fused-atrocity

## What does it mean when the color wheel is your enemy?

Colorless decks can be built to punish colored permanents or colored spell systems.

Example:
- All Is Dust makes each player sacrifice all colored permanents they control, which can be asymmetrical in a colorless-heavy deck.

Source:
- Wizards — Modern Horizons 3 Commander Decklists: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/modern-horizons-3-commander-decklists

# The History of Colorless Mana: From Cost to Identity

Colorless mana went through a long rules-language evolution before Wizards gave it a dedicated symbol.

## Era 1: Generic Cost Confusion

For many years, players could confuse generic mana costs with colorless mana production because older templating did not visually separate them as cleanly.

A careful sourced version:
- Generic costs, such as {3}, can be paid with any type of mana.
- Colorless mana is mana with no color.
- Oath of the Gatewatch introduced the dedicated colorless mana symbol {C} to distinguish colorless mana from generic costs.

Sources:
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Prerelease Primer: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-of-the-gatewatch-prerelease-primer
- Wizards — Odds and Ends: Oath of the Gatewatch, Part 1 (Rosewater, 2016-02-01): https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/odds-and-ends-oath-gatewatch-part-1-2016-02-01

Source note:
- The earlier draft cited "A Solemn Oath, Part 1," which could not be verified at the stated URL. It is replaced here with Rosewater's "Odds and Ends: Oath of the Gatewatch, Part 1," a verified official article in which he discusses colorless mana and devoid working together to drive the Eldrazi colorless theme.

## Era 2: Rise of the Eldrazi and the Devoid Paradox

Rise of the Eldrazi introduced the world-eating Eldrazi as major colorless threats.

Battle for Zendikar used devoid to make cards colorless even when they had colored mana costs. This let Eldrazi-themed cards function as colorless for mechanical purposes while still requiring colored mana to cast.

Source:
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28
- Wizards — Odds and Ends: Oath of the Gatewatch, Part 1 (Rosewater, 2016-02-01): https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/odds-and-ends-oath-gatewatch-part-1-2016-02-01

## Era 3: The Great Reform and the {C} Symbol

Oath of the Gatewatch introduced the diamond colorless mana symbol, {C}.

This mattered because:
- {C} means one colorless mana.
- Generic mana costs can be paid with any type of mana.
- Costs containing {C} must be paid with colorless mana.
- Wastes was introduced as a basic land that taps for {C}.

Correction:
- This made colorless costs behave more like a color requirement in deck construction and gameplay.
- It did not make colorless an official sixth color.

Sources:
- Wizards — Oath of the Gatewatch Mechanics: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/oath-gatewatch-mechanics-2015-12-28

# Final Clean Position

Colorless is a real and appealing player/deckbuilding lane, but it should be framed carefully.

Use this framing:

Colorless is not a sixth color. It is an identity outside the normal five-color philosophy system. In deckbuilding, it creates restriction, austerity, artifact reliance, Eldrazi scale, and unusual mana requirements. In player profiling, it can map to several different motivations: the Johnny/Jenny who likes building machines, the Timmy/Tammy who wants enormous Eldrazi moments, the Spike who wants resource denial and clean mana, or the Vorthos who wants alien or artificial flavor.

Avoid this framing:

Colorless is Magic’s sixth color, with a fully official philosophy equivalent to white, blue, black, red, and green.
