### **1\. Core Identity**

The shard of Bant is a highly structured, idyllic society defined by its green, white, and blue mana alignment.1 Originally a portion of the unified, mana-rich plane of Alara, Bant was isolated millennia ago when an unknown planeswalker sundered the plane into five distinct shards.5 Each shard was restricted to three allied colors of mana, forcing their environments and cultures to evolve in complete ignorance of one another's existence.3 Bant retained green, white, and blue mana, leaving it completely devoid of red and black mana.3
The absence of red and black mana fundamentally shaped Bant's physical and psychological landscape.3 The ecological concepts of entropic decay, absolute death, chaotic fire, and raw selfishness were virtually non-existent on the shard.3 While basic physical mortality and fire existed as natural phenomena, the magical and philosophical expressions of these elements were absent.3
Consequently, Bant developed into an ultra-ordered paradise of rolling meadows, high castles, and glittering beaches, where societal traditions became codified into absolute, unyielding law.3 Early Bantian magic focused entirely on healing, protection, and growth, lacking any capacity for destructive decay or entropic corruption.4
Wizards of the Coast design commentary identifies Bant as a "white-centered" faction.1 This white center dictates the shard's socio-political framework, prioritizing the collective community and structured order over individual desire.4 Green mana reinforces this structure by fostering an innate respect for natural growth, ancestral heritage, and destiny, while blue mana contributes a relentless pursuit of truth, absolute structural perfection, and codified law.3
This synthesis of mana created a highly cooperative, non-combative society.4 Rather than engaging in devastating, chaotic wars, the kingdoms of Bant resolved territorial and political disputes through highly ritualized, single-combat duels.10 This cultural combat pattern is directly represented by the "exalted" keyword mechanic.1
In these duels, the entire community funnels its magical, spiritual, and physical energy into a single champion, allowing white's structured cooperation to find expression in a singular, focused defender.8
To maintain the illusion of this perfect, peaceful world during its period of isolation, visual representations of Bant in the early *Shards of Alara* release intentionally downplayed direct conflict.9 Illustrative guidelines prohibited the depiction of active fear, mechanical protection, or direct combat targets, showcasing casters without their victims to emphasize a clean, highly stylized society untouched by the grim realities of death and chaos.9

### **3\. Faction Structure**

Bant's population is composed of humans, avian-humanoid avens, rhino-humanoid rhoxes, and warrior angels.3 These races live in structured harmony, supported by semi-intelligent, feline mounts known as leotau.4 The leotau exist in three distinct, highly specialized subspecies: the white-coated *orisil*, favored almost exclusively by the Blessed caste; the golden, fleet-footed *mherva*, utilized by the Sigiled knights; and the large, calico-dappled *grohm*.34
Societal organization on Bant is dictated by a rigid, birth-determined caste system, though individuals can transcend their birth ranks by earning sigils through acts of extraordinary heroism.4 Pretending to occupy a higher caste is a severe offense punishable by imprisonment, while performing the duties of a lower caste results in permanent demotion.4

#### **The Social Castes of Bant**

* **The Blessed:** The ruling nobility of Bant.4 They represent the highest mortal class and are the only caste permitted to interact directly with the angels.4 Bantian folklore dictates that if Blessed children are kept pure and free from lawlessness, they will physically transform into angels upon their deaths.4
* **The Sighted:** Composed of clerics, ascetics, and monks, including the majority of Bant’s rhox population.4 They occupy a spiritualist role and must obey the administrative decrees of the Blessed.4 Intriguingly, the Sighted maintain their own empirical insights into spiritual matters and do not rely on the spiritual authority of the angels.4
* **The Sigiled:** The highly respected knightly class of Bant.4 These warriors are measured entirely by the quantity of physical, magical sigils they have accumulated through deeds of battlefield courage.4 Earning a sigil immediately increases an individual’s power and caste rank.21
* **The Mortar:** The commoner class, constituting the vast majority of Bant’s population.4 They serve as farmers, laborers, and artisans.4 The Mortar are culturally revered as the "keepers of common sense," acting as a grounded counterpoint to the abstract spiritualism of the Sighted and the rigid, honor-bound codes of the Sigiled.4
* **The Unbeholden:** Outlaws, thieves, and rebels who exist entirely outside the rule of the Blessed.4 They represent those who operate without Letters of Marque.4 While technically outcasts, they are rarely malicious, and many families have at least one member among the Unbeholden.4 Their exploits are commonly depicted in children's fables to illustrate the consequences of lacking self-discipline.4

#### **Geographic Realms of the Shard**

The geography of Bant is divided into distinct, competing kingdoms.3 Valeron runs along the southern coast, a massive territory of rolling plains and copses of trees famed for its swift cavalry, the Sun-Dappled Court, and its aven royal family.3 Valeron maintains a historical rivalry with Jhess, an eastern island nation that dominates the coastline with its sleek naval vessels and highly skilled marines.3 While Valeron’s swift land forces prevent Jhessian incursions inland, Jhessian naval superiority prevents Valeron from expanding into the cove systems.17
The coastal city of Serul Cove operates as a major trading hub where rogues frequently peddle stolen or forged sigils.4 Akrasa, a northern kingdom, contains the highest concentration of ancient towers, castles, and members of the Blessed caste, who rule with direct backing from the military.22 In Akrasa, the authority of the Blessed is absolute, with folklore claiming that "the ground itself listens" when a Blessed speaks.22

#### **The Role of Angels and Asha**

Angels on Bant are spiritual protectors rather than rulers, strictly forbidden from directly interfering in the political affairs of mortals.21 They are formed when human heroes die; the departed spirit is fused with the land and sky's "meta-sigils," which are physical manifestations of Bant's natural elements.21 The Bantian term for angel is *bantuthroi*, which translates in the ancient tongue to "flesh of our volition," suggesting a deep metaphysical connection between mortal moral agency and divine manifestation.4
Asha, the legendary guardian archangel of Bant, ruled from the Cathedral of Bliss, a massive temple of glowing marble that floats permanently in the sky.4 Since Asha’s death before the Sundering, her throne has remained completely vacant, as no angel has emerged with the spiritual magnitude required to claim her title.4

### **4\. The Great Tension**

#### **Supported Interpretation: The Vulnerability of Codified Innocence**

Bant’s central ideological and systemic conflict is the inherent fragility of an idealized, hyper-ordered society when forced to interact with unaligned, external forces. Having evolved in a pocket environment devoid of black and red mana, Bant was structurally incapable of conceptualizing treachery, deceit, or absolute, total warfare.3
The shard’s entire defensive infrastructure rested on the assumption that all combatants would adhere to the honorable, ancestral laws of single-combat dueling.10 This reliance is mechanically highlighted by the "exalted" mechanic, which concentrates all the resource investments of the collective into a single champion, presenting an incredibly vulnerable point of failure.8
When the Conflux forced the five shards to collide, Bant's peaceful structure collapsed.9 Facing the mental assault of Esper's etherium mages and the relentless, soul-less undead legions of Grixis, Bant's knights found that their traditional combat codes were suicidal.10
The forced adaptation—exemplified by Knight-General Rafiq being forced to paralyze his mind-controlled friend Mubin, and Elspeth Tirel unleashing a destructive wave of raw white magic following the death of her squire Aran—led to a profound, tragic loss of cultural innocence.10 Bant's absolute order was exposed not as an impregnable shield, but as a delicate, artificial dome that shattered upon contact with the wider, chaotic Multiverse.13

### **9\. Faction Mechanics**

#### **The Exalted Keyword**

The primary gameplay mechanic representing the shard of Bant is "exalted," created by designer Brian Tinsman for the Bant sub-design team.8 Exalted is a triggered ability that rewards the player for declaring exactly one attacking creature during the combat phase.15

### **10\. Research Summary and Reliability Notes**

#### **Confirmed Canon vs. Supported Interpretation**

This dossier maintains a strict separation between verified canonical facts and logical narrative synthesis. Verified canonical facts include the three-color mana composition of the shard, the rigid structures of the five social castes, the historical details of the Conflux war (specifically Rafiq's conflict with Esper, his paralysis of Mubin, and his slaying of Malfegor), and the modern-era warning delivered to Rafiq by Estel on behalf of Tezzeret.3
Supported interpretations include the structural vulnerability of the exalted combat system when exposed to Esperite mind-control or Phyrexian infection, and the logical progression of the Jhessian maritime nation fostering the highest concentration of the Unbeholden caste due to its geographic proximity to Esper's trade borders.4

#### **Identified Timeline and Narrative Gaps**

* **Elspeth Tirel’s Chronology:** There is documented friction between *A Planeswalker’s Guide to Alara* and subsequent story publications regarding Elspeth’s precise age of arrival, training, and knighthood.34 The *Guide* values (arrival at 13, squireship at 17, knighthood at 20\) are treated as the primary narrative baseline for this report.34
* **Post-Conflux Borders:** The exact administrative boundaries of the integrated Alara plane remain highly abstract, though the relative proximity of Valeron to Esper's Upper Vectis is canonically established.17
* **The Term "Topa":** Listed in secondary discovery documents, "Topa" was completely absent from all reviewed official Wizards of the Coast publications and story texts, resulting in its absolute rejection from the primary factual dossier.1
