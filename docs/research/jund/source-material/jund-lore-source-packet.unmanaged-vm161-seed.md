## 2. Color Philosophy — The Three-Color Synthesis

### 2a. Red (Dominant / Center)

Red is Jund's ideological engine. Its core philosophy: **your emotions are not a flaw to be corrected — they are a compass. Follow them.**

> *"I believe in being true to who you are. I believe in following your gut. I believe in not letting others stand in your way. I believe in doing what feels right."*
> — Red, MaRo's Jund interview (source: `mark_rosewater_official_three_color/Jund_Following Your Heart`)

Red's operating framework: emotions are a primal, powerful force that guide you toward what you truly need. They do not cause problems — they reveal problems that already existed. Repressed emotion is explosive; expressed emotion is just honest. Fighting your emotions is a losing battle. The solution is to embrace, accept, and be them.

> *"It's awesome. Imagine a world of total freedom, a place where anyone can do whatever they want... The great part of this is that the creatures that remain are true specimens of triumph over adversity. They have taken whatever the world could throw at them and managed to work their way through it."*
> — Red on Jund, MaRo Jund article

Red's enemies: White (imposes arbitrary moral systems that strip individuality) and Blue (removes the emotional source of all meaning in a quest for cold rationality). On Jund, the literal physical absence of both White and Blue mana makes this explicit: no laws, no written language, no long-term planning, no institutions, no restraint of any kind.

Red is also — crucially — more than anger. It is passionate, loyal, devoted, and capable of deep love. Red is not just a destructive color. It is the color of living fully. The game does not always show this, but it is central to Red's philosophy and to understanding Jund's relationship with violence: it is not malicious, it is vital.

**Key red mechanics**: Haste (act immediately, feel immediately), direct damage, impulse draw, cascade, wheel effects, burn, fight spells.

---

### 2b. Black (Allied Support)

Black wants **power** through ruthless self-interest. It accepts the world as it is — competitive, predatory, and indifferent to sentiment — and acts accordingly. On Jund, Black's amoral pragmatism reinforces Red's impulsive freedom into something darker: not just "do what you feel" but "do what it takes, without guilt."

> *"I like Black. Black's the only other color that really gets the idea of looking out for your own needs, of understanding that you have to do the things that make you content."*
> — Red on Black, MaRo Jund article

**Black's role in Jund**: It provides the **ruthless dimension** — the understanding that in a survival-of-the-fittest world, you cannot afford sentiment about the weak. Where Red's violence is passionate and sometimes accidental, Black's is deliberate and calculated. Black translates Red's desire for freedom into a "might makes right" worldview: those who survive have earned their position by demonstrating superiority.

**BR alliance (Rakdos)**: Both oppose White's community morality. Black seeks power; Red seeks freedom. Together they produce pure hedonism — doing what you want, when you want, with no obligation to anyone else. On Jund, the BR axis produces the clan structure: not peaceful cooperation, but temporary alliances of convenience that dissolve the moment mutual interest ends.

> *"Black/red believes that everyone needs to look out solely for themselves. The welfare of other people? That's their business."*
> — MaRo, Rakdos article

**BG tension (Golgari enemy pair)**: Black and Green are enemy colors — parasitism vs. symbiosis, individual vs. ecosystem. Black destroys; Green builds. Their shared ground is the life/death cycle and the graveyard as a resource. On Jund, Black mediates this tension by treating Green's natural abundance as prey rather than partner. The ecosystem isn't something to protect — it's something to consume. This is Jund's darkest internal logic.

**Key black mechanics in BRG context**: Sacrifice, discard, removal, graveyard exploitation, tutoring, life payment for power, creature destruction.

---

### 2c. Green (Allied Support)

Green wants **acceptance** — the natural order is correct, your role within it is real, and the best thing you can do is live it without overthinking. You are part of an ecosystem. Your instincts are trustworthy because they evolved for exactly this environment.

> *"Green de nitely doesn't try to overthink things. Oftentimes, Green doesn't even think at all. I also like Green's feral side. When you want to go on a rampage, Green's your color."*
> — Red on Green, MaRo Jund article

**Green's role in Jund**: It provides the **ecological foundation** — the volcanic jungles, the food chain, the tukatongue trees, the biological logic that makes Jund a functioning world rather than just a wasteland. Where Red provides the emotional drive and Black provides the ruthless intent, Green provides the raw biological power that lets the creatures of Jund become genuinely terrifying. Green's creatures are Jund's biggest bodies; Green's mana ramp fuels Jund's most explosive plays.

**RG alliance (Gruul)**: Both oppose Blue's knowledge-seeking and intellectual restraint. Red follows impulse; Green follows instinct. Neither thinks before acting. Red is self-centered in its impulses; Green is communally instinctive. Together they produce pure action: maximum force, zero hesitation, no plan B. On Jund this manifests as the food chain itself — creatures just act on what their biology demands.

> *"Red and Green are the two colors that least value thinking. Red follows its impulses. Green is driven by its animal instincts. Blue is passive. Red and Green are active. They figure out what they want and they, quite simply, go for it."*
> — MaRo, Gruul article

**BG tension**: Green wants the ecosystem to thrive; Black wants to exploit it. On Jund, this creates the strange equilibrium of the food chain: the ecosystem is brutal and consuming, but it is also perfectly self-sustaining. Dragons eat everything, but their predation keeps population levels in check. Goblins willingly offer themselves to dragons as divine food, completing the cycle. Black's selfishness and Green's ecology produce, paradoxically, a stable world.

**Key green mechanics in BRG context**: Mana ramp, large creatures, creature protection, landfall, natural growth, land recursion, +1/+1 counter scaling.

### 2d. The BRG Synthesis — Why These Three Work Together

**RG (allied)** — Both oppose Blue's restraint. Both act immediately. Red's impulse + Green's instinct = the least thinking, most immediate guild combination. No plan. No second-guessing. Just what you feel and what your body tells you. On Jund, this is the foundational ecology: creatures that eat without strategy because strategy requires Blue.

**BR (allied)** — Both oppose White's community morality. Red's freedom + Black's self-interest = individualism without apology. The strong do what they want; the weak become food. No obligation to the community. No guilt about what you take. On Jund, this is the tribal structure: clan loyalty exists only as long as it serves you personally.

**BG (enemy)** — The productive tension that gives Jund its complexity. Black wants to destroy and consume; Green wants to grow and sustain. On Jund, Red mediates this by framing everything as immediate appetite: you don't destroy for Black's long game or sustain for Green's cosmic balance — you eat because you are hungry right now. The BG tension resolves into the food chain, where destruction and growth are the same cyclical process viewed from different positions.

> *"They both get my destructive side. Black and Green are each willing to blow stuff up. Also, from time to time when I go a little crazy and let loose, Black and Green are there to back me up."*
> — Red on Black and Green together, MaRo Jund article

**What the absence of White and Green means structurally**:
- **No White** = no moral law, no structured cooperation, no self-sacrifice for the collective, no institutional peace, no written language, no codified hierarchy. Might makes right. The only law is the food chain.
- **No Blue** = no long-term planning, no intellectual restraint, no scientific inquiry, no technological advancement, no abstract systems of control. No one on Jund thinks three steps ahead. They act, immediately, on what they feel or what they must.

## 3. Cosmological Origin — The Sundering and Jund's Formation

Alara was once a unified five-color plane. After the Sundering, Jund retained Red, Black, and Green while losing White and Blue entirely. The consequences compounded over centuries of isolation into the primordial world of the Shards era.

Without White, Jund never developed institutional order. The ancient kingdom of Vithia (which would become Grixis's Sedraxis) at least had a knightly caste system before it fell — Jund had no equivalent. Human clans on Jund speak **only local dialects**: there is no shared written language, no formal law, no treaty mechanism. Every compact is a temporary arrangement between parties who will fight each other when it suits them.

Without Blue, Jund never developed long-term planning, technology, or science. The Shamanic Circle is as close as Jund gets to scholarly tradition, and it involves drinking a slow poison and surviving elemental violence by instinct and raw magical power — not by studied preparation.

The result is the most purely natural shard of Alara: a world where every system that emerged did so from biological necessity rather than societal design. The food chain is not a political choice. It is the only organizing principle that evolution produced when left entirely to Red, Black, and Green.

## 4. The World of Jund — Geography

The physical landscape of Jund is a primordial expanse of geothermal instability, dense predatory foliage, and environmental hazard at every elevation.

| Zone | Character | Ecological Role |
|---|---|---|
| **High Plateaus** | Wind-swept rocky elevations battered by storms and volcanic breath from nesting dragons | Primary nesting ground for young territorial dragons; habitat of the tukatongue tree |
| **Active Volcanoes (Monte Jhal)** | Geothermal peaks continuously releasing ash and toxic haze | Ancient dragons perform the **Shriek of Flame** ritual here — diving into craters to trigger massive eruptions that clear valleys of competing life |
| **Lowland Jungles** | Muddy, swampy environments choked with thick predatory vines and deep channels | Primary hunting grounds for nomadic Viashino thrashes and ambush predators |
| **Tar Pits (The Seethe)** | Toxic tar-filled swamp channels where Viashino form clan bases | Ambush territory; clan strongholds; biological weapon supply |
| **Boneheaps** | Scattered skeletal remains from old kills | Resource sites for improvised weapons |
| **The Bloodhall** | A grand meeting place used by the Vision Shaman | Where the elite hunting party for the Life Hunt is organized every eight years |

**The tukatongue tree**: The primary food staple for Jund's human population. Highly resilient, with supple wood, spiky bark, and extremely tough structure. Roots are ground into paste for eating; spiky bark is wrapped around primitive weapons and annealed with shamanic magic. It is the one organism on Jund that humans farm rather than hunt.

**Sangrite crystals**: Magical mineral deposits found on Jund's volcanic terrain. The Highclan Tol Hera uses sangrite to magically augment human physical combat capabilities. Post-Conflux, sangrite's significance dramatically expands: Esper's vedalken launch resource-harvesting expeditions specifically to extract sangrite for potential etherium synthesis, and Breya later discovers that carmot (also Jund-native) combined with sangrite is the key to creating new etherium. Jund's geology is literally what breaks Esper's etherium scarcity crisis.

## 5. The Food Chain — Species and Hierarchy

Jund's entire social and biological structure flows from a single organizing principle: **eat or be eaten**. Every species occupies a position in the food chain, and that position determines everything about its culture, religion, and relationship to the rest of the world.

| Species | Position | Cultural Relationship to the Food Chain |
|---|---|---|
| **Dragons / Hellkites** | Absolute apex; four-limbed fire-breathing predators | Territorially dominant; objects of religious worship by goblins; perform the Shriek of Flame to reshape the landscape |
| **Viashino** | Humanoid crocodilians; nomadic and aggressive | Form cooperative hunting bands called "thrashes"; ambush hunters in canyon terrain; respect strength, despise weakness |
| **Humans** | Nomadic tribal hunters; extremely high mortality | Survive through high-risk rituals; maximum lifespan rarely exceeds thirty; organize around clan leaders (Tols) determined by combat |
| **Goblins** | Small, rat-like, highly reproductive | Exist primarily as prey; **actively offer themselves up to dragons as holy food**, viewing consumption as a divine honor — the most extreme expression of Green's acceptance of one's role in the ecosystem |

**Goblin theology**: The goblins' religious relationship to dragons is one of Jund's most distinctive lore elements. They do not merely accept being eaten — they celebrate it. Being consumed by a dragon is the highest possible honor. This is Green's "accept your role in the natural order" philosophy taken to its logical extreme in a world without Blue's questioning intellect or White's moral protection of the weak.

## 6. Human Clan Structure

Human clans on Jund have no central government, no written law, and no permanent territory. Leadership is determined by the Tol — the clan leader, who holds the position by demonstrated combat excellence and can be challenged and replaced at any time.

**Bloodbraids**: Human warriors weave braids into their hair from the tendons of fallen foes. Each braid represents a significant kill. The number and quality of bloodbraids is the primary status marker of Jundian society. Kresh the Bloodbraided holds twenty-two braids — an extraordinary distinction.

### The Three Human Trials

Every Jundian human clan enforces three core rites of passage that define the individual's relationship to the food chain:

**The Dark Climb** (age ten): Children must scale an active volcano — the hazardous nesting ground of young, highly territorial dragons — and retrieve a shard of volcanic glass. Those who return successfully receive an obsidian weapon crafted from the glass and annealed with shamanic magic. Those who do not return are not mourned. The food chain claimed them.

**The Shamanic Circle** (for magically gifted children, also age ten): Children demonstrating natural magical aptitude are isolated in the shamans' enclosure. On their tenth birthday, they must drink the **Dreamfire Draught** — a slow-acting poison that acts as a magical beacon, attracting violent elemental entities. The child must bind or destroy the elemental using their own raw magic to survive the poison's effects. Survivors are recognized as true shamans. The Dreamfire Draught's botanical or mineral origin is not documented in official sources — this is a confirmed open thread.

**The Life Hunt** (every eight years): A shaman receives a mystical vision of a falling dragon and is designated the Vision Shaman. They summon the most decorated hunters from all Jundian tribes to the Bloodhall. This elite party must collectively track and kill a dragon. Desertion is the ultimate dishonor. More often than not, the dragon slaughters the hunting party, which also destroys their home clans. The Life Hunt is institutionalized suicide with a small chance of glory — and a dragon kill is worth everything.

### Known Clans

| Clan | Character | Notable Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Highclan Tol Hera** | Led by Javid Hera; occupy high ground; believe in active dragon extermination | Use sangrite crystals to augment combat; most aggressive anti-dragon philosophy |
| **Tol Antaga** | Splinter clan formed when Kresh defeated Javid Hera in single combat | The most prominent clan in Alara-era Jund; Kresh rallied it against Nicol Bolas |
| **Clan Nel Toth** | Specialize in primordial elemental shamanism fueled by blood and flesh sacrifice | Meren's birth clan; the darkest of Jund's human traditions; where Black's influence on Jund's human culture is most visible |
| **Ripclan (Tol Durek)** | Reside in the volcanic valleys | Named but minimally documented |
| **Riftclan Tol Breot** | Led by Breot the Slayer | During the Conflux, half of this clan was physically separated and transported directly into Grixis's wasteland |

## 7. Key Historical Figures

### Kresh the Bloodbraided
Jund's most famous human warrior and the Tol of Tol Antaga. He survived three Life Hunts — a near-impossible achievement — and wears twenty-two bloodbraids representing his kills. During the Conflux, Kresh was rallied by Ajani Goldmane to lead the unified Jundian coalition against Nicol Bolas's forces.

**What he represents**: Kresh is the human ideal of Jund — not a ruler, not a king, but proof that individual excellence, passion, and survival instinct can create a leader without any institutional framework to support it. He holds authority because he has demonstrated, repeatedly, that he deserves it.

**Critical overclaiming note**: Kresh is not a planetary king. He is the Tol of one splinter clan. Jund has absolutely no centralized government, crown, or global ruler. His role in unifying Jundian forces during the Conflux was a temporary military coalition, not sovereignty.

### Meren of Clan Nel Toth
A human shaman apprentice who failed her elemental binding trial during the Shamanic Circle — but survived using primitive necromancy rather than the expected elemental magic. This "failure" made her an outcast. She was exiled from Jund and fled to Grixis post-Conflux, where she mastered necromancy under the tutelage of Grixis's lich lords. She eventually returned to Jund and slaughtered her former clan.

**What she represents**: Meren is Jund's BG axis made personal — she took Black's death mastery and applied it to Green's creatures, becoming the most powerful entity her birth culture ever produced, then turned that power against them. She also demonstrates what happens when Jund's "survival through any means" philosophy produces someone with Blue-level strategic patience — someone who plans across years and returns with overwhelming force.

### Karrthus, Tyrant of Jund
A massive legendary dragon native to Jund. Canonically attacked Ajani Goldmane when he first planeswalked to Jund, before Sarkhan Vol intervened to save him. Represents the absolute apex of Jund's native predator class — a legendary creature with confirmed story presence.

**Dragon sapience note**: Karrthus was captured by Nicol Bolas and had his mind wiped before being given to Sarkhan as a reward. The fact that Bolas specifically mind-wiped Karrthus implies some cognitive architecture worth wiping — suggesting more than pure animal instinct. However, this is an inference, not a confirmed sapience statement. Official sources frame Jundian dragons as apex predators operating primarily on powerful instinct, distinct from the sapient dragon cultures of Dominaria or Tarkir. The *Planeswalker's Guide to Alara* implies no intelligent, sentient races exist on Jund in the way other shards have them. Whether legendary individuals like Karrthus possess language and complex cognition remains an open thread — animalistic operational framing is confirmed; the exact sapience ceiling is not.

### Rakka Mar
A powerful fifty-year-old elemental shaman and direct agent of Nicol Bolas. Acted to manipulate Kresh and other human clans into organizing catastrophic Life Hunts specifically timed to power up Jund's obelisks — the planar stabilizers Bolas needed to destroy to trigger the Conflux. She is one of the few confirmed human figures on Jund with an extended magical lifespan, suggesting her elemental mastery sustains her far beyond the typical thirty-year human ceiling.

### Sarkhan Vol
A dragon-worshipping planeswalker who visited Jund before the Shards era's events. He viewed Jund's raw, uncivilized violence as the ultimate, unspoiled expression of natural purity. He was eventually recruited by Nicol Bolas.

**Documented arrival sequence**: Sarkhan's arrival on Jund is now confirmed in story sources. He searched for a world where dragons reigned and found Jund. His path led him to the cavern of **Malactoth** — but rather than accept his worship, Malactoth attacked him. Kresh the Bloodbraided and Rakka Mar intervened and saved his life. He spent a few days recovering in the cave's wreckage, then left Rakka Mar's company (suspecting her intentions were not pure) to explore Jund alone. During his wandering he encountered a young Ajani Goldmane, fresh from his own spark igniting. He subsequently encountered Nicol Bolas and pledged his service.

**Conflux-era role**: During the Conflux, Sarkhan assisted Rakka Mar with the Obelisk of Jund — one of the planar stabilizers Bolas needed destroyed to trigger the convergence. As reward, Bolas granted Sarkhan control of Karrthus and a dragon flight, which he led in the assault on Naya.

**What remains open**: The extended period between his arrival and his recruitment — characterized as roughly two years of wandering after the Malactoth/Kresh encounter — contains no documented clan interactions, Life Hunt participation, or specific tribal relationships in any official source. The thread is partially closed (arrival and recruitment sequence confirmed) but the two-year wandering period itself remains open.

### Malactoth
A dragon spawn whose nesting site was Monte Jhal. Confirmed as a specific named dragon associated with Jund's primary volcanic location. Represents the young territorial dragon class — the intermediate tier between the massive Hellkite Overlords and the dragon fodder that goblins offer themselves as.

## 8. The Conflux — Jund's Role

### What happened to Jund

The Conflux's impact on Jund was the introduction of White and Blue mana into a world that had never had them — and the physical collision of Jund's borders with Esper and Grixis.

**The mana disruption**: White and Blue mana flooding into Jund introduced thought, planning, and structural calculation into an ecosystem that had run entirely on raw instinct for centuries. Some native creatures became less violent and more thoughtful — but this cognitive shift initially created a chaotic, destabilizing wildness rather than orderly behavior. The food chain that had been perfectly calibrated to a BRG mana environment was suddenly confused by novel forces.

**The resource wars**: The Conflux immediately made Jund a target for its neighbors:
- **Esper** launched systematic military and resource-harvesting expeditions into Jund to extract **sangrite crystals** and **carmot** for potential etherium synthesis
- **Grixis** began looting Jund's swampy battlefields to raise massive crocodilian skeletons as additional undead troops
- **Jund's dragons** flew into foreign territories in response, devastating unfortified settlements in Bant and Naya
- **Riftclan Tol Breot** was physically split by the planar collision — half the clan was transported directly into Grixis's wasteland

### Post-Conflux

After the Conflux, Jund warriors expanded their Life Hunts across the newly accessible territories of Naya and Bant. From Jund's perspective, the Conflux was not a political crisis — it was a massive expansion of available prey. The world got bigger, and bigger worlds have bigger things to hunt. Jund adapted to reunited Alara exactly as it adapts to everything: by treating it as a new arena for the food chain.

## 9. Mechanical Identity

### 9a. Devour — The Definitive Mechanic

Devour is Jund's faction mechanic, designed by Bill Rose (lead designer) and Devin Low (lead developer) for Shards of Alara.

**Rules text**: When a creature with Devour N enters the battlefield, you may sacrifice any number of creatures. That creature enters with N +1/+1 counters for each sacrificed creature.

**Design intent**: Devour is the direct mechanical expression of Jund's food chain — the strong literally consume the weak to become permanently larger and more powerful. It consolidates multiple small bodies into a single massive threat. The +1/+1 counters are permanent: consumption results in genuine growth, not temporary power.

**The design challenge solved**: To make Devour viable without crippling card disadvantage, the design team created "good food" cards — cheap creatures with beneficial ETB or dies triggers that realize most of their value upon arrival or death:
- **Sprouting Thrinax**: Creates three 1/1 Saprolings when it dies — perfect Devour food that replaces itself
- **Tukatongue Thallid**: Creates a 1/1 Saproling when it dies — expendable but not wasteful

**Storm Scale rating**: Devour sits at Level 6 on the Storm Scale — it has a dedicated fanbase but finding the right mechanical environment to bring it back to Standard-legal sets remains a significant design challenge.

**The Planechase Jund card**: Perfectly codifies the mechanic's philosophy — its static effect grants Devour 5 to all BRG creature spells, while its chaos roll generates two 1/1 red Goblin tokens to feed the devourer. This is Jund's food chain reduced to a single card.

### 9b. Block Evolution

- **Shards of Alara**: Established Devour as Jund's core mechanic. Key cards: Hellkite Overlord, Dragon Broodmother, Kresh the Bloodbraided, Sprouting Thrinax, Tukatongue Thallid, Terminate, Blightning.
- **Conflux / Alara Reborn**: Post-Conflux cross-shard integration. **Cascade** mechanic introduced — Bloodbraid Elf becomes the iconic Jund card, generating free additional spells and setting the stage for Jund's transition into a competitive Constructed identity.
- **Commander Integration**: The sacrifice/graveyard axis evolved into the Aristocrats archetype in Commander. Prossh, Korvold, Lord Windgrace, and Henzie represent the modern BRG Commander expression — sacrifice engines and value loops replacing the pure aggression of Alara-era Jund.

### 9c. "Jund 'em Out" — Player Culture

> **Important authoring note**: "Jund 'em out" is player-developed shorthand, not Alaran lore.

In competitive Constructed (especially Modern), Jund became synonymous with fair, interactive midrange Magic. The strategy: efficient discard and removal to dismantle opponent plans → drag both players into a low-resource topdeck war → win that war through superior individual card quality (Tarmogoyf, Dark Confidant, Liliana of the Veil, Bloodbraid Elf).

Wizards directly acknowledged this cultural weight by printing the playtest card **"Jund 'em Out"** in Mystery Booster 2 — a BRG sorcery with Retrace that randomly copies Abrupt Decay, Blightning, or Bloodbraid Elf.

This player identity — the sense of winning through grinding efficiency and raw card quality rather than combo elegance — is a genuine part of Jund's cultural resonance. But it is entirely separate from Alaran lore and should not be conflated with the shard's in-world identity.

## 10. Commander Expressions — BRG Archetypes

*Sourced from `vox_mana_second_commander_recommendations_enhanced.jsonl` — 6 BRG records identified.*

### Sacrifice / Token Scaling
**Prossh, Skyraider of Kher** ("Power Hungry") — Sacrifice/Kobold token scaling. Cast Prossh generating Kobold tokens equal to mana spent; sacrifice tokens for Food Chain or other payoffs; recast for more tokens. Green generates the mana, Black provides the sacrifice willingness, Red drives the aggression.

> *"Captures Jund's most explosive identity — Prossh converts all three colors into a Kobold token flood that scales with each recast."*

**Critical note**: Prossh is native to **Dominaria** (the Kher Ridges), not Alara. His Jund color identity is mechanical, not lore. Do not present Prossh as an Alaran figure.

**Recommended for**: Competitive-leaning players; Food Chain combo potential; highest-ceiling sacrifice commander of this era.

---

### Lands as Engine
**Lord Windgrace** ("Nature's Vengeance") — Land sacrifice and recursion engine with landfall payoffs. Black sacrifices lands, Red destroys with them, Green grows through them. Windgrace makes the mana base itself the primary renewable resource.

> *"Demonstrates how Jund treats lands as weapons — the mana base as fuel rather than foundation."*

**Hearthhull, the Worldseed** ("World Shaper") — Sacrifice lands for powerful effects, then recur from graveyard to repeat the loop. Combo-oriented. Green's land connection + Black's willingness to consume + Red's explosive payoffs = lands become disposable combo pieces.

**Recommended for**: Land strategy enthusiasts; combo-oriented Jund players who want something beyond creature aggro.

---

### Graveyard Value
**Disa the Restless** ("Graveyard Overdrive") — Fill the graveyard and leverage Lhurgoyf tribal synergies. Black commands the graveyard, Green fills it with large creatures, Red adds the aggressive edge.

> *"Shows how Jund graveyard strategies can gain unique identity through unusual creature type synergies."*

**Recommended for**: Graveyard enthusiasts wanting a Jund deck with a distinctive creature-type twist.

---

### Blitz / Haste Value
**Henzie "Toolbox" Torre** ("Riveteers Rampage") — Cast creatures with Blitz (haste, sacrifice at end of turn, draw a card); Henzie reduces Blitz costs with each attack. Green's enormous creatures become Red's temporary weapons and Black's sacrificial tools.

> *"Blitz is Jund's philosophy of burning everything for maximum immediate impact, consequences be damned."*

**Recommended for**: Players who want big-creature haste value with a distinct identity beyond typical aggro.

---

### -1/-1 Counter Combo
**Auntie Ool, Cursewretch** ("Blight Curse") — -1/-1 counter placement on own and opponents' creatures for combo payoffs. Black's harm-and-sacrifice willingness, Red's chaos, Green's creature base turn debuffs into strategic advantages.

**Recommended for**: Experienced players wanting unusual, rarely-seen mechanics with combo potential.

## 11. Vox Mana Placement Signals

**Jund as primary destination** for players who signal:
- Creature-forward aggression without Blue's complexity or White's structure
- Comfort with sacrifice — treating smaller creatures as fuel is natural, not costly
- Emotional engagement: "I want to feel my plays, not calculate them"
- Big things eating small things as a satisfying game loop
- Graveyard as renewable resource, not final state
- Strong individual card preference: "I want each card to be good by itself"
- Physical dominance fantasy: growing the biggest creature on the board

**Jund as stretch recommendation** from:
- Gruul (RG) players who want Black's graveyard depth and sacrifice mechanics added to their aggression
- Rakdos (BR) players who want Green's mana ramp and large creature base under their destruction
- Golgari (BG) players who want Red's aggression and haste converting their attrition into threats

**Confidence structure**:
- High confidence: all three BRG signals present (creature aggression + sacrifice comfort + emotional impulsive play style)
- Moderate confidence: RG signals dominant + graveyard interest
- Low confidence: pure combo signals without creature density — routes Sultai or Grixis instead

## 12. Jund vs. Adjacent Identities — Separator Notes

*Vox Mana expression-level placement guidance. Not canon diplomacy — operator routing logic.*

**Jund vs. Gruul (RG)**: Gruul is raw aggression without Black's death mastery, sacrifice depth, or graveyard exploitation. A Jund player who loses the Black signal goes Gruul. The Black signal on Jund manifests as: comfort with sacrificing own creatures, graveyard as resource, reanimation interest, removal-focused play. Without it, route Gruul.

**Jund vs. Rakdos (BR)**: Rakdos is hedonism and destruction without Green's creature density, mana ramp, or natural growth scaling. A Jund player who loses the Green signal goes Rakdos. The Green signal on Jund manifests as: large creature preference, mana ramp interest, land-as-resource play, wanting to "go big" rather than "go wide." Without it, route Rakdos.

**Jund vs. Golgari (BG)**: Golgari is life/death cycle mastery without Red's passion, aggression, and immediacy. A Jund player who loses the Red signal goes Golgari. The Red signal on Jund manifests as: haste preference, impulsive play style, explosive rather than patient wins, combat aggression as the primary plan. Without it, route Golgari.

**Jund vs. Grixis (UBR)**: Both share BR but Jund has Green (growth, natural power, instinct) where Grixis has Blue (calculation, information, manipulation). Jund wants to overwhelm through physical dominance; Grixis wants to dominate through information asymmetry. Both are aggressive but for completely different reasons. The separator: does the player want to *outpower* opponents (Jund) or *outmaneuver* them (Grixis)?

**Jund vs. Naya (RGW)**: Both share RG but Jund has Black (predatory self-interest, death mastery) where Naya has White (community wonder, reverence for behemoths, collective celebration). Jund consumes; Naya celebrates. Both love big creatures, but Jund feeds them smaller ones and Naya protects them with the whole tribe. The separator: does the player relate to nature as something to **exploit** (Jund) or **revere** (Naya)?

**Jund vs. Sultai (UBG)**: Both share BG but Jund has Red (passion, aggression, immediacy) where Sultai has Blue (patience, planning, information). Sultai is the most calculating of the graveyard-focused identities; Jund is the most explosive. The separator: does the player want to **act immediately** with force (Jund) or **accumulate advantage** patiently (Sultai)?

**Jund vs. "Modern Jund" player identity**: A player who describes themselves as wanting to "Jund 'em out" — grinding resource-efficient attrition with individually powerful cards — may be describing a playstyle that routes to Jund the shard (sacrifice, aggression, green ramp) or may actually be signaling a more controlling or fair-midrange identity that would route elsewhere. The term is player culture, not a reliable placement signal on its own. Verify the underlying mechanics preference.

## 13. Known Unknowns — Resolved Thread Register

The following six open threads were researched after initial packet assembly. Each entry records the thread, its resolution status, and what remains genuinely open for architecture authoring.

---

### Thread 1 — Post-Conflux Fate of Riftclan Tol Breot's Grixis-Transported Half
**Status: PERMANENT OPEN — Transport confirmed; Grixis-side fate unaddressed**

The transport event is canon and well-sourced. Official story text (*Thraximundar's War*) describes the moment directly: Riftclan Tol Breot was camped in a rocky enclave in the Rift Valley when the world shuddered, twisted, and blasted them into a fetid hellscape. Half the clan had simply vanished. The narrative then follows Thraximundar's path through the Grixis side of events rather than tracking the transported Jundians.

The broader Grixis context at that moment: when the Conflux hit, white and green mana flooded into Grixis, disrupting vis harvesting and fueling creatures with new energy that drove them to invade nearby planes. A group of living Jundian warriors materializing in that environment would have been prime vis prey — but no source addresses their specific fate.

**For authoring**: The transport is confirmed canon. What happened to the Grixis-side half — survival, absorption into Grixis's undead food chain, or eventual return to unified Alara — is completely unaddressed. Permanent open thread.

---

### Thread 2 — Jundian Dragon Sapience and Language
**Status: PARTIALLY OPEN — Animalistic framing confirmed; legendary sapience ceiling unstated**

The *Planeswalker's Guide to Alara* contains the implication that Jund has no intelligent, sentient races in the way other shards do. Official sources consistently frame Jundian dragons as apex predators operating primarily on powerful instinct, distinct from the sapient dragon cultures of Dominaria or Tarkir. Shamans on Jund bargain with **elementals** in the Shamanic Circle trial — not with dragons — reinforcing that dragon-to-human communication is not an established practice.

However, one significant data point complicates the pure-instinct framing: Nicol Bolas captured Karrthus and had his **mind wiped** before giving him to Sarkhan. The fact that Bolas performed a targeted mind-wipe implies some cognitive architecture worth wiping — suggesting more than pure animal instinct. This is an inference, not a confirmed sapience statement.

**What is confirmed**: Jundian dragons are operationally animalistic apex predators in story and flavor. The Planeswalker's Guide implies no intelligent sentient races exist on Jund in the conventional sense.

**What remains open**: Whether legendary individuals like Karrthus possess language, complex cognition, or sapience comparable to Dominarian Elder Dragons is never directly stated. The exact sapience ceiling is unconfirmed.

**For authoring**: Write Jundian dragons as operationally apex predators. Note the Karrthus mind-wipe as a data point that may imply more cognition than pure instinct, without asserting confirmed sapience.

---

### Thread 3 — The Origin of the Dreamfire Draught
**Status: PERMANENT OPEN — Effects fully documented; composition and source never explained**

The ritual use is thoroughly documented across multiple official sources. From "All the Cairns of Jund": she looked at the vial before her, its contents pulsating with a strange phosphorescent glow — delirium, searing pain, and attunement to elemental forces follow. The *Planeswalker's Guide* confirms it as a clan-wide shamanic tradition: shamans take the Dreamfire Draught, which attracts violent elemental entities; the shaman must bind or destroy the elemental to survive the poison.

No official source — not the *Planeswalker's Guide*, not *Alara Unbroken*, not "All the Cairns of Jund" — names a botanical, mineral, or mana-infused source for the draught. Its origin, composition, and production method are entirely unaddressed.

**For authoring**: Use the Dreamfire Draught freely as a documented Jundian cultural practice. Its composition is a genuine open thread — do not invent an origin.

---

### Thread 4 — Jund's State During and After the Phyrexian Invasion
**Status: PERMANENT OPEN — Invasion confirmed; Jund-specific response unaddressed**

The invasion of Jund is confirmed at the same level as Bant and Esper: a simultaneous Machine Legion strike on all three shards. The Maelstrom awoke in defense of Alara broadly. Official sources note that no detailed story content was published for this front — neither Alaran natives nor Tezzeret interacted specifically with Jund's defense.

The inference gap is notable: by the post-Conflux era, blue and white mana had flooded into Jund, disrupting the food chain, causing dragons to fly to new lands, and making shamans struggle for control. How that altered ecology interacted with a Phyrexian mechanical invasion — whether Jundian dragons proved natural obstacles to compleation or simply flew elsewhere — is entirely unaddressed.

**For authoring**: Jund was invaded. Nothing more is confirmed. Do not speculate on outcomes.

---

### Thread 5 — Sarkhan Vol's Specific Jund Activities
**Status: PARTIALLY OPEN — Arrival and recruitment documented; two-year wandering period open**

The documented sequence is now confirmed from official story sources:
1. Sarkhan's search led him to Jund seeking a world where dragons rule supreme
2. He found **Malactoth**'s cavern — Malactoth attacked rather than accepting worship
3. **Kresh the Bloodbraided** and **Rakka Mar** intervened and saved him
4. After recovery, he left Rakka Mar (suspecting her motives) to explore alone
5. During wandering, he encountered a young **Ajani Goldmane**, fresh from spark ignition
6. He subsequently encountered **Nicol Bolas** and pledged service
7. **Conflux role**: assisted Rakka Mar with the Obelisk of Jund → rewarded with Karrthus and a dragon flight → led the assault on Naya

**What remains open**: The extended period of wandering between recovery at Malactoth's cavern and his encounter with Bolas — characterized as roughly two years — contains no documented clan interactions, Life Hunt participation, or specific tribal relationships in any official source.

**For authoring**: Arrival sequence and Conflux role are confirmed and usable. The two-year wandering period is a genuine open thread.

---

### Thread 6 — Long-Term Post-Conflux Fate of Jund's Human Clans
**Status: PARTIALLY OPEN — Immediate aftermath and Nel Toth survival confirmed; long-term arc open**

The immediate post-Conflux picture is confirmed: Jund warriors expanded their Life Hunts across the reunited Alara's hunting grounds. On Jund itself, white and green mana disrupted the food chain, creatures became less violent but more erratic, and the angry shamans struggled to maintain control as dragons flew to new territories.

Crucially, "All the Cairns of Jund" (Michael Yichao, 2015) — an official story set years after the Conflux — shows **Clan Nel Toth** still operating as a recognizable tribal unit, with its shamans still practicing their traditions including the Dreamfire Draught trial. Meren returns to find the current shamans and slaughters them. This confirms at least some clan structures persisted into the later post-Conflux era in recognizable form.

**What remains open**: Whether the broader nomadic clan system eventually dissolved into New Alara's equilibrium, whether Life Hunts continued as a cross-shard practice long-term, and whether other clans (beyond Nel Toth) maintained tribal structure are not documented beyond these snapshots.

**For authoring**: Jundian clans survived the Conflux in at least partial form — Nel Toth is confirmed evidence. Write clan continuity as supported but not fully charted.

## 14. Canon Reliability Audit

**Tier 2 / Synthesized (repo dossier)**:

High-quality synthesis document with external citations referencing *A Planeswalker's Guide to Alara* and *Alara Unbroken* as primaries.

### Flagged reliability notes

**Kresh as planetary king**: Kresh is the Tol of Tol Antaga. He rallied a temporary military coalition during the Conflux. He is not, was not, and cannot be a king of Jund — Jund has no such institution. Do not claim Kresh "rules" Jund.

**Prossh is not Alaran**: Prossh, Skyraider of Kher, is native to Dominaria's Kher Ridges. His BRG color identity is mechanical, not geographic. Do not place Prossh in Alaran lore.

**Sek'Kuar and Vaevictis Asmadi**: Both carry BRG color identity but are Dominarian natives with no Alaran connection. Treat their Jund color identity as mechanical only.

**Modern Jund midrange is player culture**: "Jund 'em out," Tarmogoyf, Dark Confidant, Liliana of the Veil in Jund decks — these are tournament community creations. None of these characters or cards have canonical presence on the shard of Jund.

**Devour cards in supplement sets**: Not all sacrifice or Devour-adjacent cards represent Alaran lore. Cards from Planechase or supplemental sets with Devour mechanics are designed for their specific mechanical environments and do not necessarily extend Jund's story canon.

**Jund is not "pure evil" or "pure chaos"**: Red's philosophy is freedom and emotional honesty — not malice. Jund's violence is ecological, not sadistic. The goblins choose to be eaten. The food chain is not cruel — it is simply the only law. Authoring should frame Jund's violence as the natural expression of a world without White's protection of the weak or Blue's plans for long-term optimization.
