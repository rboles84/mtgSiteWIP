## 2. Color Philosophy — The Three-Color Synthesis

### 2a. Green (Dominant / Center)

Green is Naya's ideological engine. Its core philosophy: **we are a tiny piece of a gigantic puzzle. We need to spend less time dwelling on our individual piece and more time on the puzzle itself.**

> *"All the other colors try so hard to make a place for themselves in the world. We don't need to make a place. We have a place. Rather than expend so much energy to become something we are not, we have to learn to accept who we are."*
> — Green, MaRo's Naya interview (source: `mark_rosewater_official_three_color/Naya_Searching Within`)

Green's operating framework: nature is the ultimate problem-solver. Every question the other colors are searching for answers to has already been answered by nature, if only they would stop long enough to observe it. You were born with your destiny. Your instincts are lessons from within — hard-wired knowledge that your body already carries. The key to life is not seeking; it is finding your role and upholding your part.

> *"Naya is the embodiment of everything I've been talking about. It's a plane where nature gets to take center stage, where nothing exists to rein it in. Enough red that things aren't too orderly but enough white that they aren't too chaotic. There's a great balance. And as a result, growth on Naya is through the roof. The plants, the creatures, nature in general — everything is growing as big as it can get."*
> — Green on Naya, MaRo Naya article

Green's enemies: **Black** (instrumentalizes death for narrow self-interest, destroys whole ecosystems on a whim, is threatened by the very concept of interdependence) and **Blue** (sees nature as something to harness, harnesses technology to limit nature's growth, uses science to create unnatural things). On Naya, the physical absence of both Blue and Black mana makes this explicit: no technology, no intellectual manipulation, no selfish exploitation, no ambient ambition.

Green is also explicitly **not passive**. Preventing artificial change requires as much effort as creating it. Green's philosophy is active — embrace life, live it fully, find your potential and live it. It is not inaction; it is directed, purposeful action aligned with what already is.

**Key green mechanics**: Creature scaling, mana ramp, +1/+1 counter growth, natural growth triggers, lands-as-abundance, large creature threshold rewards.

---

### 2b. White (Allied Support)

White wants **peace** through community order — the welfare of the group, expressed through structure, law, and collective protection. On Naya, White's contribution is warmth and communal frame without its most oppressive institutional instincts. There are no rigid legal systems on Naya (no Blue to make law incomprehensibly complex, no Black to corrupt institutions for private gain). What remains is White's genuine love for the whole.

> *"I like White. White understands the idea of the larger picture. It understands the need to evaluate how each piece fits. I also like that White understands that there is a higher calling; that things happen for a purpose. White embraces life. White embraces creatures. White works towards a greater good."*
> — Green on White, MaRo Naya article

**White's role in Naya**: It provides the **civilizational structure** — the councils, the elf-leadership, the surviving Nacatl caste hierarchy of the Cloud Nacatl, the sense that Naya's peoples are organized communities rather than just individual survivalists. White is why Naya has social bonds rather than just a jungle. Without White, Naya would be Jund — pure Red/Green with no communal organization at all.

**GW alliance (Selesnya)**: Both oppose Black's selfishness. Both are creature-centered, community-oriented, and care about the group over the individual. GW is the backbone of Naya's civilization — the social contract that allows small peoples to survive among colossal beasts by organizing together.

> *"The overlap between the two goals is that each wants its way of life to exist unimpeded. Green wants nature to thrive; White wants the community to thrive. On Naya, these are the same goal — the community IS nature, nested within it."*
> — synthesis from Selesnya article (source: `mark_rosewater_official_two_color/selesnya_Group Think`)

**Green's critique of White**: White is too obsessed with structure and rules. White cannot comprehend that things can happen haphazardly for a reason. White connects with nature "with gloves on" — it appreciates nature but can't fully surrender to it. White wants everything to make sense, and nature is full of things that happen on a whim.

**Key white mechanics in RGW context**: Token creation, protective enchantments, life gain, mass buffing, organized army structures, communal scaling.

### 2c. Red (Allied Support)

Red wants **freedom** through emotional honesty — following your heart, acting in the moment, not letting overthinking get between you and what you feel. On Naya, Red's contribution is the feral immediacy that prevents White's communal structure from becoming a rigid cage.

> *"Red completely gets the chaotic quality of nature. Red embraces nature's feral side. It isn't afraid to get its hands dirty. Also, Red is willing to disconnect from thinking. Red's okay with things happening without having to understand why. Red is very loyal. Red understands the bond that comes from being rather than thinking."*
> — Green on Red, MaRo Naya article

**Red's role in Naya**: It provides the **feral energy** — the combat immediacy, the hunting instinct, the willingness to act before thinking. Without Red, Naya would be Bant — structured, peaceful, beautiful, and entirely too organized to capture the wild chaos of a world ruled by gargantuans. Red is why Naya's nature feels alive and dangerous rather than managed and safe.

**RG alliance (Gruul)**: Both oppose Blue. Both act without thinking. Red's impulse + Green's instinct = the least calculated color combination — pure action in the moment. On Naya, this is the combat expression: creatures act on what they feel, when they feel it. The gargantuans do not plan their movements. The Wild Nacatl hunters do not deliberate their attacks. They feel and they act.

**RW tension (Boros/enemy pair)**: Red and White are enemies — chaos vs. order, freedom vs. law. On Naya, Green mediates this perfectly. Green says: "There is a natural balance between chaos and structure. Both exist in nature. Stop fighting each other and find the equilibrium." This is the formula MaRo explicitly states: "enough red that things aren't too orderly but enough white that they aren't too chaotic." The RW enemy pair resolves into Naya's defining balance rather than tearing it apart.

> *"Red/white is always focused on its goal but its ends don't always match its means. Red/white often finds itself breaking the very rules it has set out to create."*
> — MaRo, Boros article (source: `mark_rosewater_official_two_color/boros_Disorderly Conduct`)

**Green's critique of Red**: Red is too individualistic for Green's taste. It shares Black's willingness to advance the individual at the sake of the group — less selfishly than Black, but still too willing to prioritize personal desire over communal need. Red also destroys things, and Green's people have trouble with fire.

**Key red mechanics in RGW context**: Haste, direct damage, combat aggression, impulsive card draw, extra combat steps, fight spells, goad.

### 2d. The RGW Synthesis — Why These Three Work Together

**GW (allied)** — Selflessness and community against Black's individualism. Green provides nature; White provides structure. Together: grow the community, protect the community, scale the community. This is the social foundation of Naya — the reason small peoples can live safely alongside creatures that could crush entire cities.

**RG (allied)** — Action without thinking against Blue's restraint. Green provides instinct; Red provides impulse. Together: act in the moment, trust your body, don't overthink. This is the ecological foundation of Naya — the raw biological energy that makes everything on the shard grow to its maximum size.

**RW (enemy)** — The productive tension that gives Naya its distinctive balance. Red wants chaos; White wants order. Left unmediated, they would tear Naya apart. Green mediates: nature already contains both chaos and structure in perfect balance. The storm that destroys the old and the ecosystem that rebuilds from its wreckage. This is Naya's philosophical claim: the natural world already solved the chaos/order problem. Stop fighting and look at it.

**What the absence of Blue and Black means structurally**:
- **No Blue** = no technology, no scientific inquiry, no harness-and-control relationship to nature, no intellectual deconstruction of the ecosystem. Nature is not a puzzle to be solved — it is a reality to be accepted.
- **No Black** = no selfish ambition, no cynical exploitation of individuals or ecosystems, no self-interest that places the individual above the whole. The *Jungle Shrine* flavor text states this directly: "Ambition and treachery are scarce on Naya because of the awe owed to terrestrial gods." The absence of Black is not merely philosophical — it is social. There is not enough ambient selfishness on Naya to sustain the predatory power structures that Black enables elsewhere.

## 3. Cosmological Origin — The Sundering and Naya's Formation

After the Sundering, Naya retained Red, Green, and White while losing Blue and Black. The consequences of that loss shaped the shard into a paradise — and created its distinctive vulnerability.

Without Blue, Naya never developed technology or the desire to reshape nature through intellect. The Nacatl's Empire of the Clouds is the closest thing to a technological civilization Naya produced — and it was eventually destroyed from within by its own ruling class's over-structuring (the Coil, its 121 laws). Without Black, Naya never developed the ambient selfishness or cynical ambition that corrupts institutions in other settings. What remains is a world where the only organizing principle is awe: awe of nature, awe of the gargantuans, awe of whatever is larger than you.

This innocence is Naya's greatest strength and its greatest vulnerability. Without Blue's critical intellect, Naya's peoples cannot easily detect manipulation. Without Black's cynicism, they cannot recognize exploitation until it is already happening. When Nicol Bolas's agents needed to destabilize Naya to expose its Obelisk, they did not need armies — they only needed to trigger a single tribal murder (Jazal Goldmane) to set off a chain of civil war that brought down the Tower of Qasal and awakened Progenitus.

## 4. The World of Naya — Geography and Ecology

Naya is structured **vertically** — its geography is organized by altitude, and each level hosts different peoples and creatures.

| Level | Character | Inhabitants |
|---|---|---|
| **The Canopy** | Sunlit upper forest; lianas and high branches; filtered golden light | Cylian elves; their spiritual world; the domain of Mayael |
| **The Forest Floor** | Dense lower jungle; carnivorous plants; massive logging ants | Humans, druids, nomadic hunters; the most dangerous living terrain |
| **High Mountains** | Rugged peaks above the canopy | Nacatl leonin; the Cloud Nacatl strongholds; Cold and exposed |
| **The Deep Below** | Far below the forest floor | Where Progenitus sleeps; tended by Keepers |

**Key named locations**:
- **The Sacellum**: A temple site associated with Naya's spiritual practices; named but not extensively described in available sources
- **The Tower of Qasal / Qasal**: The last settlement of the Cloud Nacatl; formerly a great tower; destroyed during the Conflux's manipulated tribal war; the tower's destruction exposed the Nayan Obelisk and helped awaken Progenitus
- **Antali**: Mountain ruins
- **The Binding Wall**: Named location; not extensively described in available sources

**Named environmental phenomena**:
- **The Whitecover**: A permanent mist that settles at night across the forest. Named for its coverage of the entire canopy. The Whitecover Gaze ritual (see Mayael) involves this mist as a spiritual medium. Even gargantuans can hide within it. One of Naya's most distinctive environmental features.
- **Wanat trees**: Nomadic trees that shift slowly across the soil — a confirmation that on Naya, even the flora follows instinct and movement. Nature here is not static.
- **Bloodthorn groves**: Toxic flora; another expression of nature's dual quality — beautiful and lethal simultaneously.

**The behemoths — ecological and cultural apex**:

Naya's defining ecological fact is not the food chain. It is scale. Gargantuans — enormous creatures whose size dwarfs entire human settlements — dominate the landscape. They are not predators in the Jund sense. They are simply too large to interact with smaller life meaningfully. They do not hunt Naya's peoples. They simply exist, and that existence is so overwhelming that Naya's entire civilization organized itself around it.

> *"Naya teems with monsters to whom both nature and civilization pay homage."*
> — *Rakeclaw Gargantuan* flavor text

> *"Ambition and treachery are scarce on Naya because of the awe owed to terrestrial gods."*
> — *Jungle Shrine* flavor text

This reverence is not merely cultural sentiment. It is structural: a world where the largest things command that much awe has no room for the petty selfishness or competitive ambition that Black enables. The gargantuans are the reason Naya's social contract holds. They are too large to be worshipped individually — they are worshipped as a class, as the manifestation of nature's maximum potential.

## 5. Naya's Peoples and Society

### 5a. The Cylian Elves

The dominant spiritual force of Naya. The elves hold religious and social authority within Naya's jungle civilization — the official Alara page confirms Naya's forces move under "the elves' decrees" after the Conflux, which establishes their social leadership beyond doubt.

**Cylia — the confirmed founding figure**: Cylia is not a spiritual force or a planeswalker. She was a mortal female elven shaman — the youngest daughter of an elven shaman — who acted during the Sundering itself. When the unknown planeswalker began draining Alara's mana and the plane began to shatter, a young Cylia climbed to the top of a mountain armed with a vial of poison and a small dagger carved from a thorn, and begged Progenitus not to destroy the plane. Displeased with her, the worldsoul blinded the young elf. With blood from her mutilated eyes dripping into the vial, Cylia dipped the dagger into the mixture and flung her weapon into the hydra's heart. The poison revealed Progenitus's true nature — a five-headed hydra avatar — and all life on Alara was saved, though the plane had already separated into the Shards. Because her blood had mixed with the poison and entered Progenitus's body, Cylia gained a permanent connection with the worldsoul and the ability to sense magic. Both traits pass to all successive leaders of Naya's elves. These seers are called the **Anima**. In her honor, the elves of Naya call themselves the **Descendants of Cylia** — the Cylian elves. Source: official web fiction and MTG Wiki citing *A Planeswalker's Guide to Alara*.

**The Whitecover Gaze**: When Mayael was barely out of adolescence, the previous Anima — near death — summoned her to the Sacellum (a temple crafted from living jungle trees), named her as successor, and instructed her to undergo the **Whitecover Gaze** ritual: blinding herself in exchange for the ability of powerful magical sight. During the ritual, Mayael experienced the past lives of all previous Anima back to Cylia herself and her encounter with Progenitus during the Sundering. Mayael's physical blindness is therefore not merely symbolic — it is the same wound Cylia bore, inherited and renewed in each Anima.

**Godtrackers**: Mayael is aided by an elite group of warriors called **Godtrackers** who track the physical movements of the gargantuans through the jungles and mountains. They ignore all territorial boundaries and tend to stir up conflicts wherever they go — their job is to follow the behemoths, not to maintain peace. They are the operational extension of the Anima's spiritual guidance into the physical world.

### 5b. The Nacatl (Leonin)

The Nacatl are a leonin people native to Naya — their tribe is the birth culture of Ajani Goldmane. Their history is Naya's most dramatic social narrative: the rise and fall of the **Empire of the Clouds**, a glorious technologically advanced civilization that governed by a codified legal document called the **Coil**.

**The Coil — original nature**: The Coil was originally a code of **individual liberty**, not a rigid legal system. Its 121 laws stated that each Nacatl was free to pursue happiness however they wanted, as long as they did not hurt another Nacatl. The Empire of the Clouds had **no spiritual culture** at this time — the Coil was their ethics code, their substitute for religion. The laws were engraved on a giant disc of granite and also chiseled into the **Binding Wall** — a massive white granite slab outside **Antali**, the capital of the Empire of the Clouds. The Empire maintained an extensive system of roads, bridges, and aqueducts across Naya.

**The Coil's degeneration**: By the time of its collapse, the Coil had become so convoluted that most Nacatl could not understand it or follow it even if they wanted to. Enforcement shifted from societal pressure to coercion: the **Pride of Judges** began threatening citizens with physical harm unless they followed the letter of the law, looping a coil of rope that looked disturbingly like a noose over offenders' doors. Open warfare broke out in the streets as the **Claws of Marisi** and the **Pride of Judges** fought for dominance. Marisi's revolutionaries triumphed.

**Marisi and the Breaking of the Coil**: Marisi is officially described as "a legend among the Nacatl of Alara" and "their most influential historical figure." His revolution shattered the Coil and destroyed the institutional framework of the Empire of the Clouds. **Critically: Nicol Bolas manipulated Marisi's revolution** — Bolas got Marisi to break the Coil as part of his broader scheme to foment cross-shard chaos and prepare for the Conflux. Marisi's revolution was not spontaneous. The Breaking of the Coil split the Nacatl into two peoples:

| Group | Character | Location |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloud Nacatl** | Conservative; preserved the old ways and what remained of Cloud culture | High mountain peaks; last surviving settlement is **Qasal** |
| **Wild Nacatl** | Primal nomads; fully abandoned structured civilization | Forest floor and jungle; Ajani's tribe |

**Jazal Goldmane**: Ajani's brother. The kha (chief/king) of Ajani's Wild Nacatl tribe. **Murdered by agents of Nicol Bolas** specifically to trigger tribal warfare that would destroy the Tower of Qasal and expose the Nayan Obelisk — the planar stabilizer Bolas needed to destroy to trigger the Conflux. Jazal's murder is the single act that brought Nicol Bolas's interference to Naya. His death set off the chain of events culminating in Progenitus's awakening.

**Ajani Goldmane**: Albino Nacatl planeswalker. Native of Naya. His planeswalker arc is one of the central Alara story threads — he rallied Naya's forces and ultimately thwarted Bolas's apotheosis during the Conflux. He later witnessed the Phyrexian invasion from a distance and was subsequently compleated by New Phyrexia. He was later freed. **Note**: Ajani represents one Nacatl perspective, not all of Naya's peoples.

### 5c. Humans and Druids

Human presence on Naya is confirmed by card evidence: Drumhunter (Human Druid Warrior) and Keeper of Progenitus (Elf Druid) both confirm humanoid and elf druid classes. Human populations are less central to Naya's surviving lore than the Nacatl or elves, but they occupy the forest floor and midland zones as hunters and shamans.

## 6. Progenitus — The World Below

**Progenitus is the Avatar of the Alaran Worldsoul** — this is the official designation, confirmed in both the MTG Wiki Avatar article and Jenna Helland's official Wizards web fiction "The Soul of the World" (January 2009). It is a five-headed Hydra Avatar of enormous power, worshipped as a Hydra-God. The "world soul" framing is **not** secondary-source speculation — it is the official canonical title.

**Critical scope clarification**: Progenitus is the worldsoul of **all of Alara**, not merely of Naya. It slept in Naya, but its nature is planar rather than shard-local. A further detail from the same sources confirms the Sundering's impact on Progenitus itself: due to the lack of Blue and Black mana in the Valley of the Ancients where Progenitus rested, **two of its heads withered away**, and the hydra was doomed to slumber under Naya's soil. The withering of two heads directly represents Alara's own Sundering damage to the worldsoul — the same fracture that separated the shards also damaged Progenitus.

**The Conflux awakening**: Bolas's agents instigated Jazal's murder, triggering the internal civil wars that culminated in the destruction of the ancient towers and the **full awakening of Progenitus**. Progenitus's awakening was a consequence of Bolas's manipulation of Naya — he did not intend to awaken it, but the chain of events his agents set off (murder → civil war → tower destruction) inadvertently released the sleeping worldsoul. Post-awakening, Progenitus was confirmed to be **roving the lands for inscrutable purposes** — it did not return to dormancy after the Conflux.

## 7. Key Historical Figures

### Mayael the Anima
Blind Elf Shaman. The Anima. Spiritual leader of the Cylian elves. Her card mechanic directly expresses Naya's Power 5 theme: tap to exile the top six cards of library, put a creature with power 5 or greater from among them onto the battlefield. She is the gatekeeper between the mortal world and the gargantuans.

Mayael underwent the **Whitecover Gaze** ritual — blinded in order to receive spiritual sight connecting her to the ancient spirit Cylia. Her physical blindness is the price of her spiritual vision. She navigates by sensing the tracks and movements of the behemoths, guiding her people through Naya's spiritual landscape.

After the Conflux, Naya's forces moved "following the elves' decrees" — Mayael's role as the Anima likely positioned her as the religious authority organizing this post-Conflux march.

**What she represents**: Mayael is the purest expression of Naya's Green philosophy — a leader who literally cannot see the individual, only the larger pattern. She does not navigate the forest; she navigates the movements of forces larger than herself. She is the embodiment of "find your role in the gigantic puzzle."

**Overclaiming note**: Mayael leads the Cylian elves. She does NOT rule all of Naya. The official sources tie her strongly to the Anima role but do not crown her pan-Nayan ruler.

### Marisi
"A legend among the Nacatl of Alara" and "their most influential historical figure" — both are direct quotes from an official Wizards feature article. He shattered the Coil, destroyed the Empire of the Clouds, and created the Cloud/Wild split that defined Nacatl society at the time of the Shards era. He is to the Nacatl what Asha is to Bant — the foundational revolutionary whose act shapes everything that comes after, even centuries later.

**What he represents**: Marisi is Red's contribution to Naya's history. He rejected White's over-structuring (the Coil's 121 laws had made the Empire of the Clouds rigid and disconnected from nature), embraced Green's primal instinct, and chose freedom over law. His revolution is Naya's internal RW enemy-pair tension made historical: freedom broke the structure, and both the Wild and Cloud Nacatl survived the breaking.

### Ajani Goldmane
Wild Nacatl planeswalker. Albino — a physical outsider in his own tribe. His planeswalker arc originates on Naya (his spark ignited during or after the events surrounding his brother Jazal's murder) and takes him across the multiverse. He returned during the Conflux to rally Naya's forces and ultimately thwart Bolas's apotheosis by using auromancy to summon Bolas's own soul avatar against him — forcing Bolas to flee rather than face his perfect equal.

During the New Phyrexian invasion, Ajani was compleated. He was subsequently freed.

**What he represents**: Ajani is Naya's GW axis — the communal bond, the loyalty to those he loves, the willingness to fight for others rather than for personal gain. He is what Naya produces when you give its ideals a planeswalker's power and send them out into a multiverse that doesn't share those ideals.

### Jazal Goldmane
Ajani's brother. The kha of Ajani's Wild Nacatl tribe. Murdered on the orders of Nicol Bolas's operative network — not because he was a threat to Bolas personally, but because his murder was the most efficient way to trigger the Nacatl civil war that would bring down the Tower of Qasal. He is Naya's most significant political casualty: a good leader killed not because he was powerful, but because his death was useful.

### Godsire
A Shards of Alara Beast whose flavor text explicitly shows Nayans making sacrificial offerings to it. Confirms the religion of reverence for gargantuans as a documented practice, not just an implied cultural tendency. Creates 8/8 tokens — mechanically, even Godsire's offspring are enormous.

## 8. The Conflux — Naya's Role and Aftermath

### What happened to Naya

Bolas's manipulation of Naya was elegant and devastating precisely because it required no military force — only a single act of targeted murder:

1. Bolas's agents instigated the **murder of Jazal Goldmane**
2. Jazal's death triggered **tribal warfare** among the Nacatl — the wound between Cloud and Wild Nacatl opened violently
3. The warfare culminated in the **destruction of the ancient towers**, including the **Tower of Qasal**
4. The tower's destruction **exposed the Nayan Obelisk** — the planar stabilizer Bolas needed destroyed
5. The resulting metaphysical disruption caused the **full awakening of Progenitus**

This chain from a single murder to the awakening of a sleeping god-avatar is the clearest demonstration of why Naya's innocence — its lack of Blue's critical intellect and Black's cynical pattern-recognition — was existentially dangerous.

**Mana influx and immediate aftermath** (sourced to *Alara Reborn Player's Guide*, Wizards of the Coast, 2009):

The Conflux shattered Naya's pristine isolation. The full picture is now well-documented:

- The **massive behemoths** worshipped by elves and humans **ran rampant**, indiscriminately tearing through foreign lands — no longer contained by Naya's borders
- **Nacatl leonin prides were scattered**, torn by confusion and infighting, their leadership swayed by dark powers (the Bolas-manipulated Marisi crisis continued to echo)
- **Grixis undead infiltrated Naya's jungles** on a campaign of domination, drawn by its natural energy
- **Esper mages arrived**, viewing Naya as "a wild animal in need of etherium refinement" — the Noble Work's expansionist moment
- **Progenitus awoke and began roving the lands for inscrutable purposes** — it did not return to dormancy
- Some **Nacatl, calling themselves simply "leonin," took up arms in the armies of Bant** — the former shards' peoples began to mix
- **Demonic cults took root** among the towering tree roots of the jungle, held in check only by Leonin prides

**Naya's forces** left the jungle under the direction of the elves, seeking answers to the Anima's prophetic visions. Jund warriors expanded their Life Hunts into Naya's territories, treating Naya's gargantuans as exotic prey.

**Nacatl succession**: Ajani abdicated the Kha title to **Zaliki**, believing she was the least likely to be manipulated again and best positioned to make peace with the elves and Cloud Nacatl in his absence. This is the closest official canon comes to a post-Conflux Nacatl resolution — not a formal reunification of Cloud and Wild, but a succession gesture oriented toward healing.

**What remains open**: Whether Progenitus eventually returned to dormancy, how Naya's five-color ecosystem stabilized long-term, and whether the Cloud/Wild Nacatl schism formally healed under Zaliki are not documented beyond these snapshots.

## 9. Mechanical Identity

### 9a. Power 5 or Greater — The Unnamed Naya Mechanic

Naya's original shard identity does **not** have a named keyword mechanic like Exalted (Bant), Devour (Jund), or Unearth (Grixis). Its defining mechanical identity is a **threshold**: **power 5 or greater**. MaRo later described this as "the unnamed Naya mechanic" and acknowledged that the *Ferocious* mechanic from Khans of Tarkir block drew directly on this Nayan precedent.

**What the threshold represents**: Creature size is not just a mechanical advantage on Naya — it is sacred. The power 5 threshold is the mechanical expression of the dividing line between "ordinary creature" and "gargantuan worthy of reverence." Cards on or above that line are not just more powerful; they occupy a different category of existence.

**Key cards expressing the theme**:

| Card | Role |
|---|---|
| **Mayael the Anima** | Searches the top 6 for a creature with power 5 or greater; the Anima literally reaches into the unknown to call forth the enormous |
| **Drumhunter** | Ramps AND draws a card if you control a creature with power 5 or greater — abundance rewards scale |
| **Where Ancients Tread** | When a creature with power 5 or greater enters, deal 5 damage to any target — the gargantuans' footsteps are destructive by nature |
| **Mayael's Aria** | Checks power thresholds of 5, 10, and 20 — a tiered devotion to increasing scale |
| **Spellbreaker Behemoth** | Cannot be countered; other creatures with power 5 or greater you control cannot be countered — against Blue's restraint, size is immunity |
| **Rakeclaw Gargantuan** | Gives first strike to another creature with power 5 or greater — gargantuans support each other |

**Combat as worship**: Naya's combat identity is built around visible, on-board force rather than stack manipulation. *Spellbreaker Behemoth* is the most philosophically direct card: it embodies Green's rejection of Blue's harness-and-control approach by making its power literally immune to counterspells. You cannot think your way around a gargantuan. You can only survive or not.

### 9b. Secondary Identity — Natural Abundance

Alongside the power 5 theme, Naya has a secondary "lands and creatures" abundance identity:
- **Gift of the Gargantuan**: Finds a creature and/or land together — abundance in both pillars
- **Naya Panorama**: Fetches any of the shard's three basic land types
- **Keeper of Progenitus**: Enhances Mountains, Forests, and Plains simultaneously
- **Drumhunter**: Ramps AND rewards large creatures in the same card

This secondary identity reinforces the core: the same shard that produces massive creatures also produces the mana base to summon them. Abundance and scale are the same phenomenon at different scales.

### 9c. Ferocious and the Naya Legacy

In Khans of Tarkir block, MaRo confirmed that the *Ferocious* mechanic — triggering abilities when you control a creature with power 4 or greater — was directly inspired by "the unnamed Naya mechanic from Shards of Alara." He subsequently argued that Khans's Ferocious felt too much like a repeat of Naya. This body of commentary confirms that Naya's power-threshold identity is distinctive enough that later design teams consciously avoided recreating it.

## 10. Commander Expressions — RGW Archetypes

*Sourced from `vox_mana_second_commander_recommendations_enhanced.jsonl` — 10 RGW records identified.*

### Populate / Token Scaling
**Ghired, Conclave Exile** ("Primal Genesis") — Populate engine. Make powerful tokens (Ghired creates 4/4 Rhinos); Populate copies the best one repeatedly. White's token army, Red's aggressive momentum, Green's creature power snowball.

## 11. Vox Mana Placement Signals

**Naya as primary destination** for players who signal:
- Love of large creatures as a primary value — not "big is powerful" but "big is meaningful"
- Community-oriented combat: fighting together rather than individually
- Awe and reverence as emotional drivers: "I want my opponent to be afraid of what I put on the board"
- Instinct over calculation: "I want to feel my plays rather than solve them"
- Abundance as a gameplay feeling: ramp into something enormous and satisfying
- Creature-first, non-Blue identity: no counterspells, no library manipulation, trust your body
- Tokens or big creatures (or both) as the primary board expression

**Naya as stretch recommendation** from:
- Selesnya (GW) players who want Red's aggression and haste added to their creature communities
- Gruul (RG) players who want White's token scaling and protective enchantments added to their aggression
- Boros (RW) players who want Green's creature density and mana ramp grounding their combat instincts

**Confidence structure**:
- High confidence: all three RGW signals present (creature reverence + communal aggression + instinctive play style)
- Moderate confidence: GW signals dominant + large creature interest
- Low confidence: pure combo or control signals — routes Bant (for planning) or Esper/Sultai (for manipulation) instead

## 12. Naya vs. Adjacent Identities — Separator Notes

*Vox Mana expression-level placement guidance. Not canon diplomacy — operator routing logic.*

**Naya vs. Gruul (RG)**: Gruul is raw aggression without White's communal structure or social scaling. A Naya player who loses the White signal goes Gruul. The White signal on Naya manifests as: token interest, combat coordination beyond just "attack with everything," protective enchantments, or caring about the board as a whole rather than just one threat. Without it, route Gruul.

**Naya vs. Selesnya (GW)**: Selesnya is communal selflessness without Red's immediacy, haste, or aggressive energy. A Naya player who loses the Red signal goes Selesnya. The Red signal on Naya manifests as: haste preference, goad or combat-forcing play, impulsive extra combat, or wanting to apply pressure immediately rather than building slowly. Without it, route Selesnya.

**Naya vs. Boros (RW)**: Boros is fanatical directness without Green's creature density, mana ramp, or natural scale. A Naya player who loses the Green signal goes Boros. The Green signal on Naya manifests as: large creature preference, mana ramp interest, creature quantity as a value in itself, or wanting to "go big" rather than just "go fast." Without it, route Boros.

**Naya vs. Jund (BRG)**: Both share RG but Naya has White (communal reverence, organization, protection of the whole) where Jund has Black (predatory self-interest, death mastery, sacrifice for gain). Naya celebrates nature; Jund consumes it. Naya's gargantuans are worshipped; Jund's dragons are hunted. The separator: does the player relate to the largest things as **objects of reverence** (Naya) or **ultimate prey** (Jund)?

**Naya vs. Bant (GWU)**: Both share GW but Naya has Red (instinct, feral energy, immediacy) where Bant has Blue (planning, deliberation, tactical intelligence). Bant is orderly and deliberate; Naya is abundant and instinctive. The separator: does the player want to **plan carefully and protect** (Bant) or **act immediately and grow** (Naya)? Bant makes one champion powerful through collective support; Naya fields an abundance of powerful things.

**Naya vs. Enchantress (Selesnya/Bant expression)**: Enchantress strategies using Naya colors may be routing Bant or Selesnya rather than Naya specifically. The separator: does the enchantment player want enchantments as **protective structure** (Bant) or as **creature buffs that make things enormous** (Naya)? If the enchantments are primarily Auras on large creatures, it is Naya. If they are primarily engine/draw pieces, it is Bant or Selesnya.

**Naya vs. "Battlecruiser" player identity**: Players who self-describe as "battlecruiser Magic" or "big stompy" players are likely routing to Naya. But verify: battlecruiser without White's token/scaling layer may route Gruul; battlecruiser without Green's mana base may route Boros. The full Naya signal requires all three axes — reverence for scale, communal combat support, and instinctive play style — not just "I want big things."

### Thread 1 — Cylian Elf Cultural Details Beyond Mayael
**Status: MOSTLY CLOSED — Cylia confirmed mortal founding shaman; key institutions documented; broader hierarchy open**

Cylia's identity is now fully confirmed in official sources. She was a mortal female elven shaman who climbed a mountain during the Sundering, begged Progenitus to spare Alara, was blinded by the worldsoul, and responded by stabbing it with a thorn dagger dipped in a mixture of poison and her own blood. The blood+poison entered Progenitus's body, giving Cylia (and all successive Anima) a permanent connection to the worldsoul and the ability to sense magic. The elves named themselves Descendants of Cylia — the Cylian elves — in her honor.

Additional institutions documented: **Godtrackers** (warriors who track behemoth movements, ignoring territorial boundaries), the **Sacellum** (the Anima's living-tree temple), and the Whitecover Gaze ritual (blinding in exchange for spiritual sight and access to all previous Anima's memories back to Cylia).

**What remains open**: Broader internal Cylian social hierarchy beyond the Anima institution — rank-and-file structure, clan subdivisions, the succession process beyond the Whitecover Gaze — is not documented.

**For authoring**: Cylia is a confirmed mortal historical figure, not a planeswalker. Use the full founding story freely.

### Thread 2 — The Coil in Detail
**Status: CLOSED — Original purpose, physical form, degeneration, and Bolas manipulation all confirmed**

The Coil was originally a code of **individual liberty** — each Nacatl was free to pursue happiness as long as they did not harm another Nacatl. The Empire of the Clouds had **no spiritual culture**; the Coil was their ethics code. It was engraved on a giant granite disc and physically chiseled into the **Binding Wall**, a massive white granite slab outside **Antali**, the Empire's capital.

The Coil degenerated into coercive enforcement: the **Pride of Judges** threatened citizens with physical harm, displaying noose-like coils of rope over offenders' doors. The **Claws of Marisi** fought the Pride of Judges in open street warfare. **Bolas manipulated Marisi's revolution** — getting Marisi to break the Coil was part of Bolas's broader cross-shard chaos scheme, the same operation that later used Jazal's murder and the Obelisk chain.

**For authoring**: This thread is closed. All major elements are usable.

### Thread 3 — Progenitus's Exact Theological Status
**Status: CLOSED — "Avatar of the Alaran Worldsoul" confirmed in official Wizards sources**

The "world soul" concern is fully resolved. Jenna Helland's official Wizards web fiction **"The Soul of the World"** (January 2009) and the MTG Wiki Avatar article (citing the same) confirm: Progenitus is the **Avatar of the Alaran Worldsoul**. The scope is Alara-wide, not Naya-specific — Progenitus is the worldsoul of all of Alara, housed in Naya because that is where it slept. Due to the **absence of Blue and Black mana** in the Valley of the Ancients, **two of Progenitus's five heads withered** — a direct physical manifestation of the Sundering's damage to the worldsoul.

**For authoring**: Use "Avatar of the Alaran Worldsoul" as the confirmed designation. The two-withered-heads detail is confirmed and usable.

### Thread 4 — Naya's Post-Conflux Conditions in Detail

The Nacatl succession is confirmed: Ajani abdicated the Kha title to **Zaliki** as a peace-building gesture toward the elves and Cloud Nacatl.

**What remains open**: Whether Progenitus returned to dormancy, how Naya's five-color ecosystem stabilized long-term, and whether the Cloud/Wild schism formally healed under Zaliki.

**For authoring**: The immediate post-Conflux state is well-sourced and usable. Long-term equilibrium is a genuine open thread.
