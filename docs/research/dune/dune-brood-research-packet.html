Color Identity

Dune-Brood is the most martially complete color combination in Magic — ambition to conquer, passion to fight, strength to overwhelm, and community to forge armies. Everything the combination does is in service of the battlefield. It acts; it does not contemplate.

🔴 Black — Ambition
Ruthless execution, sacrifice for gain, efficient removal, and willingness to pay any cost for victory. Conquest has a price; Dune pays it.
🔴 Red — Passion
Haste, aggression, direct damage, and total commitment. Red fights now, without hesitation or calculation.
🟢 Green — Strength
Natural might, land ramp, large bodies, and the power of sheer numbers. The engine fueling the army's scale.
⬜ White — Community
Tokens, anthems, coordination, and the bonds of a fighting force. White turns individual combatants into an unstoppable mass.
🔵 The Absent Color: Blue

Blue provides intellect, patience, planning, card selection, and counterspells — solving problems through superior information rather than superior force. Dune's rejection of blue is the rejection of intellectualism itself: thinking wastes time better spent acting, and planning betrays doubt. Problems are solved here through overwhelming force, not clever maneuvering. If the strategy isn't working, more violence is applied.

Origin — The Nephilim

Dune-Brood Nephilim is a Rare 3/3 for BRGW from Guildpact (2006), card #110, illustrated by Jim Murray. Its art bears a strong resemblance to a painting by Polish surrealist Zdzisław Beksiński — appropriate for a creature venerated as an Old God before the Guilds were formed. It is the mechanically strongest of the five Nephilim for its intended purpose, with an ability that escalates rapidly if it connects.

Whenever Dune-Brood Nephilim deals combat damage to a player, create a 1/1 colorless Sand creature token for each land you control.

"When it awoke, it spawned nameless thousands to herald its arrival."

— Dune-Brood Nephilim, Guildpact (2006)

A single connection in the mid-game with 8–10 lands in play generates 8–10 tokens in one swing — one of the highest token payoffs ever printed on a combat damage trigger. Unlike Glint-Eye (which requires pump setup) or Yore-Tiller (fragile 2/2), Dune-Brood arrives as a 3/3 and produces tokens in the colors best suited to giving it evasion: green provides trample and enchantment-based buffs; white provides flying and vigilance effects. The token army it creates then feeds white's anthem effects and black's sacrifice synergies. It has appeared in episodes of Game Knights under house-rule Commander and is considered by many the most playable of the five Nephilim as a general.

Note: Like all Nephilim, Dune-Brood is not officially legal as a Commander. Mark Rosewater has stated that with hindsight they should have been printed as Legendary — a widely shared sentiment.

Key Commanders

Saskia the Unyielding
Legendary Creature — Human Soldier · Commander 2016
The sole dedicated BRGW legendary creature. A 3/4 with vigilance and haste — she enters attacking immediately, and when she enters the battlefield you choose an opponent. Whenever any creature you control deals combat damage to a player, Saskia causes that creature to also deal that much damage to the chosen opponent. In a four-player pod, this effectively reduces the life total you must attack through by a third. Particularly dangerous with double strike, lifelink, and high-power creatures, and creates instant political pressure on whoever is chosen. Her tags on EDHREC are Legends, Aggro, Humans, and Extra Combats — a broad identity that accommodates many builds.

Tana, the Bloodsower // Tymna the Weaver
Partner Pairing — Commander 2016 / Battlebond
The most popular Partner pairing at BRGW. Tana is a 2/2 Elf Druid with trample: whenever she deals combat damage to a player, create that many 1/1 green Saproling tokens — an escalating army generator that rewards repeated combat. Tymna draws cards equal to the number of opponents damaged by your creatures during combat. Together they create a self-sustaining loop of tokens → card draw → more tokens. The Tana side echoes the Dune-Brood Nephilim fantasy at a more accessible power level.

Bruse Tarl, Boorish Herder // Ikra Shidiqi, the Usurper
Partner Pairing — Commander 2016
A combat-multiplier pairing. Bruse Tarl grants one of your creatures double strike and lifelink when he enters and at the start of each combat. Ikra Shidiqi grants creatures with toughness 4 or greater lifelink and lets you gain life equal to a creature's toughness when it deals combat damage. Together they create a lifelink-doubling engine where a single swinging creature both attacks hard and replenishes your life total dramatically.

Dune-Brood Nephilim
Creature — Nephilim · Guildpact (House Rule Commander)
The namesake. 3/3 for BRGW — the largest of the five Nephilim by base stats. Token production scales with lands in play, making it exponentially more threatening as the game progresses. Best supported by evasion (trample enchantments, flying tokens, Sidar Kondo of Jamuraa-type effects), anthems (Honor of the Pure, Cathars' Crusade), and sacrifice synergies that turn the Sand tokens into resources. Appeared on Game Knights and remains a beloved casual Commander at house-rule tables.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

  • Green provides the best ramp and mana acceleration, enabling explosive early deployment
  • White and green together produce the most efficient token generation in the game
  • White anthems (Cathars' Crusade, Glorious Anthem) scale devastatingly with wide boards
  • Black provides efficient removal, sacrifice synergies, and recursive threats
  • Red closes games fast with haste, extra combat steps, and direct damage
  • Access to the best aggressive creatures across four colors — combat rewards compounding
  • Saskia's damage doubling effectively reduces opponent life totals without attacking them directly

Weaknesses

  • No blue means no counterspells — combo decks can resolve threats through your removal suite
  • No card selection (scry, brainstorm, cantrips) — relies on green draw engines and black tutors for consistency
  • Wide boards are vulnerable to mass exile (Farewell) — which you also can't cast
  • Aggro traditionally struggles in Commander's 40-life, 4-player format without Saskia's damage shortcut
  • Linear strategies are predictable — tables will target the aggro player first
  • No countermagic makes you vulnerable to the one blue player at the table

Key Strategic Lines

Representative win conditions and game-defining plays for Dune-Brood:

Saskia + Double Strike

Bruse Tarl grants a creature double strike and lifelink → Saskia's passive deals that damage to a second opponent → effectively dealing quadruple damage (two combat hits × two opponents). A 5-power creature with double strike and Saskia kills two players in one swing from 20.

Tana Token Avalanche

Tana connects with trample → creates 3 Saprolings → Parallel Lives doubles them to 6 → Cathars' Crusade triggers six times, stacking counters on the whole board → follow-up Craterhoof Behemoth for lethal. Scalable across turns.

Dune-Brood Land Scaling

Hit with Nephilim at 12 lands → create 12 Sand tokens. Attach Sidar Kondo of Jamuraa-style evasion so tokens can swing unblocked → another 12 tokens → Shared Animosity or Eldrazi Monument seals the game before opponents stabilize.

Extra Combat Kills

Aurelia, the Warleader gives two full combat phases per swing → Savage Beating or Moraug gives more → with Saskia in play, each extra combat is additional damage to the targeted opponent without additional attackers needed.

Vox Mana Design Notes

Placement signal considerations and design hooks:

  • ⚔ Dune's defining negative signal is absent blue: players who reject patience, card selection, countermagic, and "thinking before acting" score toward this quadrant. "I would rather attack than pass" is the Dune mindset.
  • ⚔ The Aggression label is the clearest of the five Commander 2016 themes — combat, tokens, and going-wide are universally understood game actions. Lowest conceptual barrier to entry of any Dune commander option.
  • ⚔ Contrast with Ink-Treader (WURG): both include white and green tokens, but Ink-Treader keeps blue's planning and card selection. Dune trades that for black's ruthlessness and red's haste. If the player wants "big attack with smart decisions," it's Ink; if they want "big attack right now," it's Dune.
  • ⚔ The Nephilim flavor text — "spawned nameless thousands" — is among the best flavor-to-mechanics alignments in Magic. It should be surfaced in any lore-facing presentation of this faction. The Sand token type is unique and evocative.
  • ⚔ Commander pool breadth: Saskia (~59% of BRGW EDHREC volume), Tana/Tymna (~21%), then a long tail of Partner pairings. The quadrant has more Partner variation than Yore but less than Glint-Eye. Recommendation diversity is possible through Partner exploration.
  • ⚔ Personality fit: Timmy players (big creatures, overwhelming armies, smashing faces) are the primary profile. Johnny appears in the Aristocrats and extra combat loops. Spike is present but limited — without blue, competitive cEDH in this color set is weaker than Glint-Eye or Yore.
  • ⚔ Placement quiz signals: prefers attacking to blocking, likes tokens/armies, values speed over control, doesn't care about counterspells, wants to win through combat damage specifically. All of these cleanly route to Dune over Glint-Eye (ramp + chaos) or Yore (artifacts + control).
