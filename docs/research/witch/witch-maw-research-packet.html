Color Identity

Witch-Maw is the most methodically complete color combination in Magic — patient, optimizing, and inevitable. All four colors share a belief in long-term planning over short-term gain. The combination does not rush; it cultivates. It is widely considered the poster combination of Phyrexia, whose four aligned Praetors represent four of these very colors.

🟢 Green — Growth
Natural expansion, land ramp, and organic inevitability. Green provides the mana engine and creature density to sustain escalating plans.
⬜ White — Community
Organization, efficiency multiplication, removal, and structured order. White turns individual components into a coordinated system.
🔵 Blue — Knowledge
Card selection, optimization, counterspells, and the superior information that enables perfect decisions. The planner's color.
🟣 Black — Ambition
Ruthless direction, tutors, sacrifice optimization, and the willingness to pursue long-term ambition without moral hesitation.
🔴 The Absent Color: Red

Red provides passion, spontaneity, haste, and emotional decision-making — living in the moment and acting without a plan. Witch-Maw's rejection of red is the rejection of impulse itself. Every action is calculated; every resource is a long-term investment. There is no "attack now and figure it out later" — only patient, methodical accumulation until the moment of inevitable victory.

Origin — The Nephilim

Witch-Maw Nephilim is a Rare 1/1 for GWUB from Guildpact (2006), card #138, illustrated by Greg Staples. It is the most mechanically demanding of the five Nephilim to activate — starting as a 1/1, it requires spell casts to grow, and rewards patience with a creature that earns trample only after reaching power 10 or greater. The fantasy is pure Witch-Maw: a slow accumulation that becomes unstoppable.

Whenever you cast a spell, you may put two +1/+1 counters on Witch-Maw Nephilim. Whenever Witch-Maw Nephilim attacks, it gains trample until end of turn if its power is 10 or greater.

"When it awoke, it shattered the hillsides to make way for its passage."

— Witch-Maw Nephilim, Guildpact (2006)

The mechanical arc is the faction's philosophical arc in miniature: fragile beginning → patient investment → critical mass → unstoppable conclusion. A 1/1 that gains two +1/+1 counters per spell can reach power 10 in roughly five spell casts, after which it tramples through any blocker. In practice, the absence of innate evasion until power 10 creates vulnerability — but that challenge simply asks the pilot to protect the investment, which is exactly what GWUB does best: white removal, blue counterspells, black tutors, and green bodies providing a shield until the inevitable moment arrives.

Note: Like all Nephilim, Witch-Maw is not officially legal as a Commander, but sees play at house-rule tables as a spell-slinger voltron threat. It has roughly 1,166 EDHREC decks listed.

The Atraxa Factor

Witch-Maw is unique among the five four-color quadrants: it is by far the most commander-dominated quadrant in all of Commander. Atraxa, Praetors' Voice held the #1 spot on EDHREC's all-time most-played commanders list for nearly a decade. Even after being recently surpassed by The Ur-Dragon in 2025 — itself a product of extraordinary set support from Tarkir: Dragonstorm — Atraxa remains #2 with approximately 38,000–42,000 logged decks. A second Atraxa card, Atraxa, Grand Unifier (Phyrexia: All Will Be One, 2023), added another ~17,000 decks. The two cards together account for roughly 59,000 EDHREC decks at GWUB, an unprecedented volume for a single color identity.

For Vox Mana placement purposes: any player who has ever built or is attracted to Atraxa belongs in Witch-Maw. The faction's identity IS Atraxa's identity in the popular imagination.

Proliferate — The Mechanic of Growth

Proliferate is Witch-Maw's defining mechanic. It reads: "Choose any number of players and/or permanents that have a counter, then give each one additional counter of each kind already there." Its power lies in universality — it scales every counter type simultaneously, compounding different strategies at once:

+1/+1 Counters

Creatures accumulate power over time. Every proliferate turn doubles investment already made in your board.

Poison Counters

Infect deals poison to players; at 10, they lose. Proliferate accelerates the clock without needing combat.

Loyalty Counters

Planeswalkers activate abilities by spending loyalty — proliferate rebuilds them faster than opponents can answer them.

Experience Counters

Commander experience counters (Meren, Ezuri, Saskia) scale permanently — proliferate accelerates every commander in a pod.

Charge Counters

Astral Cornucopia, Everflowing Chalice, and other artifacts scale in mana production with each proliferate trigger.

Lore Counters

Sagas advance to next chapter on proliferate, unlocking abilities faster and potentially recycling after completion.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

  • Best ramp in the game (green) combined with best card advantage (blue/black) and best removal (white/black)
  • Proliferate scales multiple win conditions simultaneously — each trigger advances every counter-based plan
  • Atraxa's four combat keywords (flying, vigilance, deathtouch, lifelink) make her both a threat and a stabilizer in one card
  • Blue counterspells protect the slow accumulation plan from disruption
  • White exile-based removal handles indestructible and difficult permanents
  • Enormous strategic flexibility — same commander enables infect, superfriends, and +1/+1 counters as entirely distinct decks
  • Grand Unifier's ETB triggers even off reanimation, flicker, or clone effects

Weaknesses

  • No red means no haste — slow starts are exposed to combo decks before the engine fires
  • No direct damage — must win through combat, poison, or combo; no "burn" finish
  • Atraxa is a known threat — tables will apply coordinated removal before proliferate accumulates
  • Infect builds draw disproportionate hate and political enmity at tables
  • Planeswalker builds require protecting multiple fragile permanents simultaneously
  • Grand Unifier at 7 mana can be countered for massive card disadvantage

Vox Mana Design Notes

Placement signal considerations and design hooks:

  • ✦ Witch-Maw's defining negative signal is absent red: players who reject impulsive action, emotional play, and "doing something now" score here. "I would rather accumulate advantage than attack early" is the Witch-Maw disposition.
  • ✦ Witch-Maw has the largest commander in Commander. Atraxa's brand recognition extends beyond dedicated players — it is the most accessible entry point for new Commander players across all five quadrants. "I want to build Atraxa" is itself a full placement answer.
  • ✦ The Growth label covers multiple mechanical expressions: counter accumulation, planeswalker escalation, poison clock, and incremental board advantage. Unlike Aggression (one clear strategy), Growth has internal diversity that supports different player types within the same faction.
  • ✦ Contrast with Glint-Eye (UBRG): both have blue and black, but Glint-Eye trades white's structure for red's chaos and sacrifices patience for cascade explosiveness. Witch-Maw keeps the order (white), keeps the patience (no red), and focuses on compounding investment rather than random detonation.
  • ✦ The Phyrexian lore connection is strong and available: Atraxa is literally built from Phyrexian Praetor components (Elesh Norn, Jin-Gitaxias, Sheoldred, Vorinclex — all GWUB). For lore-interested players, Witch-Maw is the faction of New Phyrexia's dominant ideology — compleation as patient perfection.
  • ✦ Personality fit: Johnny players (complex scaling systems, proliferate interactions, combo endpoints) are the primary profile. Spike is present through infect's clean win condition and Superfriends' inevitability. Timmy appears in the Grand Unifier builds — a massive creature that draws a huge hand is a pure Timmy moment.
  • ✦ Placement quiz signals: prefers patience over speed, likes watching resources accumulate over time, values planning and information, doesn't need to win "now," comfortable with complex multi-step strategies, attracted to counters/scaling/incremental advantage. All of these cleanly route to Witch-Maw.
  • ✦ Recommendation hook: Witch-Maw offers the widest range of thematically distinct decks under a single commander — infect, superfriends, +1/+1 counters, Phyrexian tribal, and good stuff are functionally different decks that all run Atraxa. This is the strongest quadrant for differentiating recommendations by play style.
