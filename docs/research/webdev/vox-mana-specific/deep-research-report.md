# Commander Themes Library Research

## Scope and methodology

For this section, the most important source distinction is simple: **Commander archetypes are community taxonomies, not official Wizards rules objects**. Wizards’ official Commander page focuses on format structure and the optional Commander Brackets system; it does not provide a canonical “deck theme library.” The actual vocabulary players use for archetypes and themes is surfaced by community data and strategy sites such as EDHREC’s Themes index and Commander archetype guides. EDHREC’s theme index currently tracks large, stable buckets like Tokens, Spellslinger, Aristocrats, Reanimator, Lands Matter, Treasure, Equipment, Control, Enchantress, Ramp, Mill, Voltron, Blink, Stax, Group Slug, Storm, Group Hug, Vehicles, Pillow Fort, Politics, and many others, while Draftsim explicitly notes that Commander archetypes are **non-exhaustive** and frequently **mixed and matched** within the same deck. citeturn2view2turn4view0turn2view1

That means the best “full list” for a site section like **Archetype Discovery / Commander Themes Library** is not a literal dump of every long-tail tag on EDHREC. It is a **curated, user-facing taxonomy** built from the most recognizable Commander themes, plus a specialist layer for more advanced or polarizing strategies. That is the approach below. Theme names come from recurring Commander community usage; **difficulty**, **recommended colors**, and **table perception** are editorial UX ratings meant for onboarding and search/filter design, not official policy. citeturn4view0turn2view1

A second important design takeaway is that several things your current mockup combines should be separated. Community sources consistently distinguish **Ramp** from **Lands Matter**, **Landfall** from generic land-ramp, **Aristocrats** from broader **Sacrifice**, **Spellslinger** from **Storm**, **Enchantress** from pure **Auras**, and **Group Hug** from **Pillow Fort** or general **Politics**. Treating those as separate discoverable themes will make the library more accurate and more useful. citeturn12view7turn14search12turn12view1turn12view8turn12view4turn12view6

## Rating scales and naming rules

The scales below are **editorial** and are meant to be consistent with how Commander players actually talk about deck experience, not with official bracket labels. Wizards’ official bracket system describes **game experience bands**, while this library describes **strategic identity**. A Tokens deck and a Stax deck can both exist at different brackets; archetype and power are separate axes. citeturn2view2turn2view3

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Low Difficulty | Clear battlefield plan, lower rules overhead, usually easier for a first or second Commander deck |
| Medium Difficulty | Requires sequencing, engine management, or more careful resource planning |
| High Difficulty | Often involves stack play, hidden information, politics, long sequencing chains, or punishing misplays |
| Fair / Normal | Usually reads as visible, combat-forward, or socially expected |
| Fair / Focused | Still broadly fair, but tends to pressure one player or one axis hard |
| Value / Snowball | Looks manageable until it compounds cards, mana, or board presence |
| Hidden Threat | Can appear harmless before a combo or burst turn |
| Resource Dominant | Draws attention by making too much mana, too many tokens, or too many cards |
| Threat / Spite | Commonly attracts removal or emotional pushback because it drains, mills, discards, steals, or repeatedly grinds |
| Annoying / Stalled | Often slows pacing, creates long turns, or reduces interactivity |
| Extreme Threat / Salt | Strong candidate for Rule 0 disclosure because many tables react negatively to it |

**Naming rule for the site:** use the most widely recognized theme name on the card, then a short plain-English subtitle beneath it. For example: **Aristocrats — Sacrifice Value**, **Voltron — Single Hero Commander**, **Stax / Lockout — Resource Taxation**. That mirrors how Commander teaching content tends to explain themes to newer players. citeturn12view1turn12view8turn12view5

## Important theme splits your current mockup should make

Your example cards are close, but several of them would be materially better if the library recognized the splits below.

For your exact mockup, I would therefore replace **“Ramp / Lands”** with three separate cards: **Ramp**, **Lands Matter**, and **Landfall**. I would also keep **Tokens** as the theme name but use **Go-Wide Combat** as the subtitle rather than treating them as perfect synonyms. Finally, I would treat **Stax / Lockout** as an advanced theme card and not surface it first for brand-new players unless they actively search for it. That recommendation fits both the archetype-writing landscape and the social burden those decks carry at real tables. citeturn12view5turn4view0turn2view3

## Recommended filter and search model for Vox Mana

The safest and clearest launch model is a **two-layer discovery system**.

The first layer should be what users see by default: the **core library** table above. Those are the themes most players expect to find quickly, the ones with the broadest community recognition, and the ones that are easiest to explain with beginner-friendly copy. The second layer should be **advanced / specialist** themes, surfaced through filters, search, or an “Show Advanced Themes” toggle. That structure matches the way Commander archetypes actually behave in the sources: broad, popular strategy families at the top, with more polarizing or rules-intensive variants branching below them. citeturn4view0turn2view1

One last product recommendation: **do not sort by power** on this page. Archetype is not power.
