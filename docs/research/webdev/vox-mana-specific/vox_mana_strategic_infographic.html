AUDIT SECTION 01 • Strategic Assessment

Audit Section 03

Audience Cohorts & Pain Points

Identifying the core target users and the exact psychological friction blocks current tooling fails to resolve.

New Commander Players

Faced with a massive catalog of $30,000$ legal cards, multiplayer stacks, and simultaneous triggers. They struggle with basic game mechanics and fear ruining the play flow of experienced players.

Returning Magic Players

Trapped in an outdated, $60$-card, competitive $1$-on-$1$ mindset. They do not understand the politics, threat assessment, or structural rules of casual, four-player tables.

Product-Overwhelmed Casuals

Unable to navigate the rapid cycle of preconstructed decks and expansion releases. They don't know what to buy or how to upgrade without breaking the balance of their deck.

Identity/Flavor Players

Values thematic storytelling, art, and emotional expression. They struggle to construct functional lists, resulting in decks that fail to execute their intended themes.

Table-Anxious Players

Anxious about matching power levels with strangers at a local store. They need communicative scripts and table safety tools to feel comfortable sitting down.

AUDIT OUTCOME

The Core Solution

Vox Mana addresses these points directly by skipping rule manual mechanics and delivering human communication guides and playstyle orientation instead.

Audit Section 04 & 05

The Unowned Gap

Current tools deliver extreme data granularity, leaving these three gaps wide open:

01

Social Friction Void

Existing resources assume Commander is a zero-sum card game. They offer zero guidance for navigating multiplayer table politics, targeting-etiquette, and the subjective social contract.

02

Color Translation Void

Instead of treating color identity purely as a deckbuilding deck-restriction, Vox Mana translates each color's worldview into actual behavioral predictions and table behavior.

03

Precon Upgrade Trap

Standard guides immediately push users to replace $30$ cards with expensive, uncoordinated staples, pushing their deck out of comfortable casual play and ruining the engine's synergy.

Concept 01

The Precon Formula

Avoid expensive upgrades. Stick to the basic functional ratio: 1 Commander, 36-38 Lands, 10-13 Ramp rocks, 8-13 Interaction elements, and 10 Card Advantage cards.

Audit Section 11

Quality Bar Thresholds

UX: The 30-Second Check

Any landing user must identify precisely why they are here and the value proposition above-the-fold in under $30$ seconds. Zero-state onboarding (no login/signups required).

Mobile-First optimization

Over $70\%$ of active users will evaluate scripts/checklists inside high-glare Local Game Stores. Font hierarchy and UI scaling must be tested explicitly on mobile devices.

WotC Dynamic Matching Sync

Keep guides perfectly in sync with the late-2025 updates from the Commander Format Panel (removing strict tutor limits and updating the Game Changers list).

Audit Section 12

Project Risk Management

Risk 01

The Poetic Void

Why it matters: Users enjoy reading poetic flowery copy once but find no recurring utility tools, leading to high initial bounce rates.

Mitigation: Ensure every single atmospheric concept immediately translates into a concrete strategic gameplay action.

Risk 02

The Database Compulsion

Why it matters: Getting distracted trying to build card indexes or collection managers, causing key onboarding targets to fail.

Mitigation: Enforce a strict "no custom database" rule. Externalize all card indexing/deck hosting to Moxfield or Scryfall.

Risk 03

Beginner Overwhelm

Why it matters: Drowning users in heavy rules, storm counts, stack interactions, or cEDH combo-lines.

Mitigation: Limit scope entirely to Brackets 1, 2, and 3. Defer competitive Bracket 4-5 discussions.
