# Contradictions and Compound Preferences
- Players want optimal play while preserving enjoyable social experiences.
- Many support strategic interaction but reject perceived bullying, grudge play, or mismatched expectations.

# Recommendation Successes
- Rule discussions, announcing phases, and explicit combo explanations reduced misunderstandings.

# Recommendation Failures
- Mechanically correct advice often failed when social expectations or power mismatches differed.

# Vocabulary and Classification Problems
- 'Casual', 'tryhard', 'threat', 'power level', 'playing to win', and 'Rule 0' were interpreted inconsistently.

# Beginner and Accessibility Evidence
- New players repeatedly cited phases, triggers, priority, and threat assessment as learning barriers.

# Social and Contextual Evidence
- Local meta, LGS culture, friendships, retaliation, and reputation substantially change player decisions.

# Building Versus Playing
- Numerous players distinguish enjoying deckbuilding from enjoying actual gameplay with that deck.

# Current Versus Desired Experience
- Many knowingly keep suboptimal habits because they value narrative, fun, or social cohesion over optimization.

# Evidence That Challenges Common Models
- Similar gameplay decisions arise from very different motivations; behavior alone is insufficient for player modeling.
