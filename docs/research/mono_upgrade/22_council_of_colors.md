# THE COUNCIL OF COLORS, REVISITED — Source Digest

> Paraphrased governance digest. Not verbatim. Pull quotes from the source URL under fair use.

## `GOV-COC-2024`
**Author:** Mark Rosewater · **Pub:** 2024-08-26 · **Publisher:** Wizards of the Coast
**URL:** https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/the-council-of-colors-revisited
**Tier:** Gold (governance / design-authority). Updates the 2016 Council of Colors article.

### What it establishes (bind as process/authority claims)
- `COC-origin` The color pie is one of Richard Garfield's three foundational ideas (with the CCG concept and the mana system). The Council of Colors is the standing body that maintains it; founded 2015, publicly revealed 2016. Pitched by Mark Gottlieb to replace single-person oversight.
- `COC-structure` One counselor per color (W/U/B/R/G) plus a **colorless** counselor, overseen by Gottlieb and Rosewater, with an administrator role. The colorless seat is the traditional entry/training slot before taking a color. Meets weekly; reviews every set.
- `COC-rotation` A rotation rule keeps perspectives fresh: occasionally the two longest-serving color counselors swap colors (two-at-a-time, for continuity).
- `COC-mandate` Core mandate: review all new card designs and prevent designs that break or unhealthily bend the color pie.

### Expanded responsibilities (use to justify why the pie shifts)
- `COC-shift-commander` **Oversees color-pie shifts driven by the environment**, notably Commander. Commander (4 players, 40 life) disadvantages aggressive strategies → White and Red (the aggressive colors) were underplayed. The Council deliberately added in-color resources: **Red → impulsive draw**; **White → once-per-turn, long-game card-draw build-arounds**. (This is the governance basis for the `MECH-CP-2021` card-draw lines.)
- `COC-setdesign` Resolves set/play-design concerns — e.g., gave **Blue secondary vigilance** for non-evasion combat options; allowed **Black tertiary enchantment removal** to ease its noncreature-removal restrictions.
- `COC-gaps` Fills gaps / finds new effects — e.g., Red is the narrowest color for unique effects, so the Council ran a hackathon to find new red spaces.
- `COC-writeups` Produces internal R&D write-ups of current design philosophy per topic (e.g., green card draw; differentiating W/U/R artifact interaction).
- `COC-longterm` Maintains a channel for long-term thinking on new mechanics/effects without a color, and on intended evolution — keeping printed cards aligned to the pie while colors evolve true to core philosophy.

### Review process (bind as authority-of-ruling claims)
- `COC-twolooks` Booster sets get **two looks**: (1) late vision design (mechanics/themes, areas of concern, limits); (2) later set design — a **card-by-card** pass, multicolor cards reviewed by all relevant counselors.
- `COC-notetypes` Set-design notes are tagged **Discussion** (bring to meeting) or **Quick check** (no blocking issue, wants to talk it through).
- `COC-ratings` Four post-meeting ratings, in escalating severity: **Fine** (stays as is) · **Second Look** (consider changes, may stay) · **Strongly Reconsider** (strongly encourages change, won't escalate) · **Must Change** (cannot remain; a break or severe bend; escalated if not changed — and per the article, always is).
- `COC-scope` Scope now covers **all products with original designs**: boosters, Commander decks, Jumpstart, non-randomized products, new-to-Magic Secret Lair, and digital/Alchemy. Every card now requires Council sign-off, parallel to Play Design's balance sign-off.

### Why it matters for the ledger
- This is the **authority-of-process** anchor: a claim that "X is/isn't in color" gains weight when tied to Council review reality. Use `COC-shift-commander` to ground any Commander-context bend (esp. white/red card-draw and aggression support), and `COC-ratings` to characterize how firm vs. flexible a given color-pie line is (a "bend" vs. a "break").
