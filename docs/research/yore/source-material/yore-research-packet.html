Color Identity

Yore's identity is defined not by what it contains, but by what it refuses. All four present colors share a rejection of the natural order: they manipulate, construct, and impose. The absent green is the color of growth, instinct, and acceptance of nature's limits — and Yore wants none of it.

⬜ White — Order
Law, structure, collective discipline, removal, exile, lifegain. The civilizing force that builds empires and prison pods alike.
🔵 Blue — Knowledge
Intellect, manipulation, countermagic, artifact affinity, card draw. The planner behind every constructed machine.
🟣 Black — Power
Ambition, self-determination, graveyard recursion, sacrifice, tutor effects. Willing to pay any price.
🔴 Red — Passion
Chaos, aggression, haste, impulse, direct damage. The creative spark that ignites invention — and everything else.
🌿 The Absent Color: Green

Green provides ramp, creature-based value, and harmony with the natural world. Yore's rejection of green means the combination is "comprised entirely of the unnatural, focusing on artifice, manipulation, and infinite potential. However, by forcibly recreating the world around it, it may fail to understand the necessity, purpose, and interconnection of the things it changes."

Origin — The Nephilim

The Nephilim from Guildpact (2006) were the first four-color cards ever printed in Magic — ancient, monstrous entities from Ravnica predating the guilds. Yore-Tiller Nephilim is a Rare 2/2 for WUBR (card #140), illustrated by Jeremy Jarvis.

Whenever Yore-Tiller Nephilim attacks, return target creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield tapped and attacking.

— Yore-Tiller Nephilim, Guildpact (2006)

The ability telegraphs graveyard recursion fused with aggressive combat — pulling powerful creatures back from the dead the moment it attacks. In practice, the 2/2 body is fragile and the strict WUBR cost demands a polished mana base, limiting its use as a formal Commander. Most tables treat Nephilim as generals by house rule only; they are not officially legal as Commanders without a variant ruling.

Note: Yore-Tiller Nephilim has since received a reprint in the Planeswalker Symbol Reprints series.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths

  • Best removal suite in the game — exile, destroy, counter, and bounce all present
  • Deep artifact synergy; Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, and Thopter lines compensate for absent ramp
  • Strong combo potential: infinite loops via artifact sacrifice are well-supported
  • Excellent card draw across blue and black
  • Flexible win conditions: combo, control, tokens, reanimator
  • White provides the best board wipes (Wrath, Farewell, Austere Command)

Weaknesses

  • No green = no efficient land ramp; entirely dependent on mana rocks
  • Demanding four-color mana base (fetch + shock + triome investment)
  • Vulnerable to artifact hate (Bane of Progress, Vandalblast hitting rocks)
  • Lacks the raw mana acceleration of green-inclusive color identities
  • May over-engineer answers without "naturally" closing games

Notable Combo Lines

Yore is one of the format's most combo-dense color identities. Representative lines:

Aminatou Loop

Aminatou, the Fateshifter + Nicol Bolas, Dragon-God + Oath of Teferi — exiles all opponents' permanents and hands. One of the most played combos in the quadrant (~6,652 decks).

Breya Damage Loop

Breya + Ashnod's Altar (or KCI) + any 0-cost artifact generates infinite damage through repeated self-sacrifice and recursion — a cEDH staple in the format.

Scrap Trawler Chain

Scrap Trawler + Myr Retriever + Blasting Station — sacrifice loops that continually return artifacts from the graveyard, generating infinite "enters/dies" triggers.

Yore-Tiller Reanimate

Medomai the Ageless (extra turn) enters attacking via Nephilim trigger → sacrifice to Altar of Dementia → recur next attack. Generates repeated extra turns with a sacrifice outlet.

Vox Mana Design Notes

Considerations for placement signals and faction representation in Vox Mana:

  • ◆ Yore's defining negative signal is absent green: users who reject naturalistic, organic, or "harmony with the world" playstyle themes should score toward this quadrant.
  • ◆ Mechanically, the Artifice label from Commander 2016 is the strongest thematic anchor — far more resonant than "not-green." Artifact, construct, and engineer archetypes map cleanly here.
  • ◆ Adjacent identity tensions: Yore shares white+blue with Esper (control) and white+red with Boros (aggro), but distinguishes itself through constructed complexity rather than raw authority or speed.
  • ◆ Dark-Jeskai is a useful alternate label for players who think in Tarkir wedge terms: Jeskai (WUR) + black = Yore.
  • ◆ The color identity supports all three main deck personalities: spike (cEDH Breya), johnny (Yore-Tiller reanimate loops), and timmy (big artifact payoffs). Broad placement applicability.
  • ◆ Commander pool is thin for a non-Breya WUBR commander (Breya accounts for ~59% of Yore EDHREC volume). Users wanting variety lean on Partner pairings, which can be a recommendation differentiation hook.
