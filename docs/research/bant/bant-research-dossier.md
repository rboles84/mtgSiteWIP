## 1. Core Identity

### What Bant Is

> **Confirmed Canon** — [SRC-001, SRC-004]

Bant is one of the five shards of Alara, a plane that was fractured into five separate miniature worlds, each containing only three of the five colors of mana. Bant is the shard associated with green, white, and blue mana.

The world of Alara was split into five shards long before the Shards of Alara block story era. Each shard developed its own culture, ecology, and magic in isolation from the other colors. Bant, containing only green, white, and blue mana, developed a culture oriented toward order, honor, and noble hierarchy — a society without the influence of black's self-interest or red's chaos and passion.

### Color Identity and Design Center

> **Confirmed Canon** — [SRC-001, SRC-036]

Bant is a green-white-blue faction. An official Wizards Making Magic article by Mark Rosewater, published for Bant Week on October 6, 2008, confirms white as "the central color to Bant." [SRC-036]

The same article describes how white's allies (blue and green) each contribute to Bant's character: blue provides structure and the drive toward perfection, while green provides interconnectivity, spiritual intuition, and the sense of a "higher power." White acts as the balance between them, directing societal improvement toward collective harmony rather than individual technical perfection (blue's tendency) or passive natural order (green's tendency). [SRC-036]

> **Design Note:** This design-level framing (white-centered, white as peacemaker between blue and green) describes Bant's mechanical and philosophical identity. It is not an in-world concept within Bant's lore.

### Alara's Shard Structure

> **Confirmed Canon** — [SRC-001, SRC-004]

Alara was split into five shards, each with only three colors of mana:

| Shard | Colors | Center |
|---|---|---|
| Bant | Green / White / Blue | White |
| Esper | White / Blue / Black | Blue |
| Grixis | Blue / Black / Red | Black |
| Jund | Black / Red / Green | Red |
| Naya | Red / Green / White | Green |

The five shards existed in isolation until the events of the Conflux brought them back into contact with one another.

### Bant's Core Themes

> **Confirmed Canon / Card-Supported** — [SRC-003, SRC-007, SRC-011, SRC-012]

Bant is a civilization defined by structured honor, martial excellence, and a nobility system built around earned recognition. The mechanic exalted, which appears primarily on Bant-watermarked cards in the Shards of Alara block, captures this culture mechanically: a society that rallies its full support behind a single champion who rides to battle alone.

Knights are a central institution in Bant. Sigils — marks of honor granted for great deeds — form the basis of Bant's social hierarchy. A knight who earns more sigils occupies a higher rank. Official card text confirms this system: the flavor text of Rafiq of the Many states that he is the only knight of Bant to have been decorated with the sigils of all five nations. [SRC-007]

- Direct-quotation-level detail for Jhess, Topa, and Eos beyond their bounded five-nation geography status.
- The specific pre-Conflux conditions that created Bant's distinct society.
- Bant's condition after the New Phyrexian invasion beyond what "A Man of Parts" directly states (see Section 5).

## 3. Faction Structure

> **Confirmed Canon / Card-Supported** — [SRC-007, SRC-011, SRC-016, SRC-018]

Bant's society is organized around a system of earned honors called **sigils**. Sigils are marks awarded to knights for great deeds, and a knight's sigil count determines their social rank. This system is confirmed by the flavor text of Rafiq of the Many [SRC-007] and by the names and oracle text of sigil-bearing cards (Sigiled Paladin [SRC-011], Sigiled Behemoth [SRC-027], Sigiled Blessing [SRC-028]).

## 4. The Great Tension

> **Official Design Commentary** — [SRC-036] + **Supported Interpretation** — [SRC-003, SRC-004]

The Mark Rosewater Bant Week article provides direct design commentary on Bant's core identity, framed through white's perspective. The following are direct quotes from that article describing why Bant is what it is:

- *"The absence of Black and Red has allowed us to focus on the utopia I've talked about before. Without the selfishness and chaos, we are able to push the people towards a greater good."* [SRC-036]
- *"Bant is the utopia that I've been striving to build. It's the culmination of my philosophy."* [SRC-036]
- *"Everyone is working towards a similar goal — making Bant as ideal as possible."* [SRC-036]

> **Note:** These quotes are from a design philosophy column written as an interview with the "color White." They are Mark Rosewater's description of what white represents as applied to Bant. They are design commentary, not in-world narrative. Do not present them as direct in-world lore.

The dossier's supported interpretation, synthesized from design commentary and mechanical context:

Bant's central tension is the relationship between **idealized collective order** and the reality of a fractured, then violently reunited, world. Bant's culture channels collective honor behind a single champion (expressed mechanically through exalted [SRC-003]), but the Conflux [SRC-004] exposed Bant to the black and red influences that had been entirely absent from its shard.

This tension plays out in two directions:

1. **Internal**: The sigil hierarchy rewards individual excellence but requires that excellence be channeled through collective endorsement. The single attacker of exalted is not a lone wolf — they are the designated champion of a society that closes ranks behind them.

2. **External**: The Conflux brought black's self-interest and red's chaos into direct contact with Bant's order — the very influences that white itself identifies as the precondition for Bant's utopia to exist.

> **Caution:** The interpretation above is synthesis from design commentary and mechanics. It is not directly stated in a reviewed in-world story source. Do not present it as confirmed in-world fact.

## 9. Faction Mechanics

| Mechanic | Associated Set / Block | Rules Summary | Design or Flavor Connection | Source ID(s) | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exalted | Shards of Alara block (ALA, CON, ARB) | Exalted is a triggered ability. Whenever a creature you control attacks alone, that creature gets +1/+1 until end of turn. The design connection is that Bant's culture of channeling collective honor behind a single champion is expressed by rallying multiple exalted triggers around one attacker. | SRC-003, SRC-011, SRC-012, SRC-013, SRC-022 | High | Exalted keyword text from official rules/release notes. ### Rules Note

The exalted rules text as printed on cards: **"Exalted (Whenever a creature you control attacks alone, that creature gets +1/+1 until end of turn.)"**

If multiple permanents with exalted are on the battlefield when a creature attacks alone, each one triggers separately. A player with three creatures having exalted will give the attacking creature +3/+3 (one +1/+1 from each trigger).

Rafiq of the Many does not carry the exalted keyword text itself; his oracle text includes a related but distinct triggered ability that gives the lone attacker +1/+1 for each Aura and Equipment attached to it, in addition to any exalted triggers from other sources. Verify against Gatherer. [SRC-007]

### Optional Related Gameplay Notes

- Auras and equipment interact with Rafiq's unique ability (not with exalted itself).
- Exalted does not require the attacking creature to have exalted — any creature you control with exalted triggers when any creature you control attacks alone.
- The "attacks alone" condition means only one creature is declared as an attacker. If multiple creatures attack, exalted does not trigger.
