To preserve humanity and prevent total self-destruction, Hazid and the ruling castes codified the Caste System and the Sigil System. The Great Resolution replaced mass warfare with highly ritualized, non-lethal skirmishes mediated by sigils (magical crests bestowed by angels). 1. The Exact Founding Event or Founding Figure of Bant's Social Order
The lore does not record a named mortal founder or a specific founding event for Bant's social order. What is established is that Bant's chivalric culture, caste system, and knightly orders developed organically over centuries of isolation following the Sundering of Alara. The shards evolved into wildly distinct entities over the thousands of years they were apart. The closest thing to an originating authority is the archangel Asha, whose rule predates the surviving records — but she is a ruler, not a documented founder of the social order itself. Fandom
Bant's theology is actually heavily based on Zoroastrianism, suggesting its social philosophy has thematic roots, but no single founding event or figure is named in the official sources. MTG Salvation
Verdict: No founding figure or founding event for Bant's social order is officially confirmed in the canon.

2. Asha's Exact Nature, Title, and Institutional Role
This is one of the most clearly documented aspects of Bant's lore. Asha "the Blessed" is a mythical figure from Alara, an archangel who was said to have battled Malfegor before the Sundering. She ultimately sacrificed herself to banish the demon, but contrary to the written record she was unable to slay him. Fandom
Asha was Bant's guardian angel. Before she died, she ruled the Cathedral of Bliss, a position that has remained vacant ever since. Her legacy includes the Prayer of Asha, the Sword of Asha, and the Sigil of the Empty Throne. Her name has become a title, although no angel has become great enough to assume it, and her throne remains empty in Alara's skies. Fandom
The ruler of Bant once was the supreme queen of the angels, the archangel Asha, who gave her life fighting the demon Malfegor long ago. No angel has assumed her title since her demise. MTG Wiki
The angelic hierarchy below her consisted of four ranks: the Asura (the seven highest, comprising the Court of Orderly Contemplation), the Amesha (who represent ideals like honor or justice), the Mahra (who carry out higher orders), and the Celebrants (who protect mortals).
Summary: Asha was an archangel, Bant's "supreme queen" and "guardian angel," who ruled the Cathedral of Bliss. She is both a historical ruler and a religious symbol whose empty throne defines Bant's institutional identity.

3. Full Official Confirmation of Jhess, Topa, and Eos as Bant Nations
All three are fully confirmed as official Bant nations in multiple sources. The nations of Jhess and Valeron control the coastal regions while the inland savannahs are divided between the three nations of Akrasa, Topa, and Eos. Fandom
Individually:

Jhess is confirmed as the island/coastal nation. The nation of Jhess is known for being much more freewheeling and flamboyant than the Inner Nations, with a "shockingly radical" level of social mobility between castes. TV Tropes
Topa is confirmed as one of the three Inner Nations, serving as a place of punishment: Even for those who do break the law, the worst punishment is temporary banishment to the savannahs of Topa. Fandom
Eos is confirmed as another of the Inner Three: Eos is one of the Inner Three nations of Bant on Alara. Eos is nominally Valeron's ally, due more to their disputes with Topa than any particular affinity for the Valeronians. The rulers of Eos possess ancient documents that they feel give them claim to several ancient watering holes in Topa, a claim that Topa steadfastly denies. Eos is home of the Olive Branch, a knightly order that seeks to broker peace and smooth the relations between all the nations of Bant. Fandom

These details are sourced directly from A Planeswalker's Guide to Alara (Beyer & Helland, 2008), the primary official sourcebook.

4. Specific Elspeth-on-Bant Story Claims Beyond Her Card-Era Connection
Elspeth's time on Bant is well-documented across several story sources, going significantly beyond just her card appearances.
She spent the next few years of her young life seeking a safe place, suffering unknown hardships until finally, she stumbled across Bant. In Bant, Elspeth found everything that was missing from her life: community, love, and most importantly, peace. Fandom
She found a home in the idyllic hills and peaceful rolling plains of the shard. More importantly, she found a purpose, starting her training as a knight at the age of 16. By 20, she was a fully-fledged Knight of Bant. She had learned how to protect herself, and to protect the people she loved, and for a while, that was enough. Card Kingdom
On Bant, Elspeth trained as a knight in the kingdom of Valeron, and soon became renowned as a healer and military leader. Elspeth fought to defend Bant from invasions by both Grixis and Esper, and was eventually able to kill Malfegor, the demonic ruler of Grixis. But protecting her new home required her to use her magic in ways that broke Bant's code of honorable combat. Card Kingdom
In the aftermath, Elspeth resigns from her post within Bant's order of knights, stating that she is leaving Alara forever. Magic Untapped
Her departure was specifically motivated by guilt: having broken Bant's chivalric code by using her planeswalker powers in open battle, she felt she no longer truly belonged on the plane she had come to love.

5. Bant's Detailed Post-Conflux and Post-Phyrexia Political Condition
Post-Conflux: Although the army of Bant fought the invaders, it was clear that the opposing beasts and fighters were weakening Bant. Bant would no longer be as powerful as it was. Its borders merged more and more with the other shards until it became part of New Alara. Following the invasions, the once shining castles of their lands lay in ruin, dangerous places to venture to. Society as a whole had been shattered, and isolated pockets of settlements still fall prey to roving bandits and worse. Fandom
In the years after the Conflux, travel between the former shards became normalized, and a new equilibrium was born across the plane. Naya's leonin join Bantian orders, Esper's artificers search Jund for resources, and the living refugees of Grixis escape to surrounding lands. Fandom
Post-Phyrexia: Alara was targeted by Elesh Norn's Machine Legion as part of New Phyrexia's Invasion of the Multiverse with a simultaneous strike on Bant, Esper, and Jund. As the invasion was beginning, Tezzeret sent a warning message to Rafiq, as the Knight-General of Bant, to employ the angels against the Phyrexians. Fandom
Tezzeret hoped that the angels of Bant could turn the tide against the Phyrexians as those of Capenna did long ago. However, the post-invasion political state of Bant specifically is not detailed in available sources — the Phyrexian Invasion arc focused heavily on other planes (Dominaria, Theros, Zendikar), and no dedicated Alara/Bant story content was released for that era. Fandom
Verdict: Post-Conflux Bant is described as a shattered, diminished society absorbed into New Alara. Its post-Phyrexia condition is only confirmed to have been invaded; no detailed political outcome for Bant specifically has been officially published.

1. Exact founding event or founding figure of Bant’s social order

Answer: There is no clearly confirmed single founding figure for Bant’s social order in the available official sources. The strongest promotable answer is that Bant’s social order developed from Alara’s Sundering / shard isolation, not from a named founder.

Promotable doctrine wording:

Bant’s social order formed during the centuries after Alara’s Sundering, when Bant existed as an isolated white-blue-green shard without black or red mana. That isolation shaped Bant into an orderly society centered on hierarchy, duty, nobility, angelic oversight, sigils, and honor-bound martial life. No official source currently confirms one exact mortal founder of Bant’s caste/sigil order.

Confidence: High for “formed through shard isolation”; low/unsupported for any single named founder.

2. Asha’s exact nature, title, and institutional role

Answer: Asha is best promoted as Bant’s guardian angel and archangelic sacred authority. She should not be promoted as the confirmed founder of Bant’s whole social order.

Official story text directly calls Asha “Bant’s guardian angel.” Secondary lore indexing further states that Asha ruled the Cathedral of Bliss, that this position remained vacant after her death, and that her legacy includes the Prayer of Asha, Sword of Asha, and Sigil of the Empty Throne. Her name also became a title that no later angel had become worthy enough to assume.

Promotable doctrine wording:

Asha was Bant’s guardian angel and highest remembered angelic authority. Bantian tradition preserves her through prayer, sacred relics, and the empty throne/title associated with her absence. Institutionally, Asha functions as Bant’s sacred model of protection, order, and righteous authority, but current support does not prove that she personally founded Bant’s caste system or national order.

Confidence: High for guardian angel / sacred authority; medium for “title/throne/Cathedral of Bliss” because that relies on secondary summary of older printed lore.

3. Official confirmation of Jhess, Topa, and Eos as Bant nations

Answer: This is fully promotable.

Wizards explicitly identifies Jhess as an island nation on Bant’s eastern coast. The same official source states that Bant’s central savannahs contain the Inner Three nations of Topa, Eos, and Akrasa. It also identifies Valeron as a southern coastal nation.

Promotable doctrine wording:

Bant’s confirmed nations include Jhess, Valeron, Topa, Eos, and Akrasa. Jhess is the island nation along Bant’s eastern coast. Valeron stretches along Bant’s southern coast. Topa, Eos, and Akrasa are the Inner Three nations of Bant’s central savannahs.

Confidence: Very high.

4. Specific Elspeth-on-Bant story claims beyond limited card-era connection

Answer: Elspeth’s Bant connection is promotable, but should be framed as an identity-shaping knightly chapter, not as political leadership.

Official later story shows Elspeth reciting the Prayer of Asha, identifies Asha as Bant’s guardian angel, and has Elspeth reflect that she had “thrown away her oath when she deserted Alara.” This supports the idea that Bant mattered to her personally, religiously, and knightly. Separate official Magic Origins material also places Gideon’s first planeswalk on Bant, confirming Bant’s continued relevance as a major story plane, though that is Gideon-focused rather than Elspeth-focused.

Promotable doctrine wording:

Elspeth’s time on Bant should be treated as a defining knightly and spiritual chapter in her identity. Bant gave her a framework of oaths, prayer, armor, duty, and righteous service. Later story confirms that Elspeth retained Bantian religious memory through the Prayer of Asha and regarded her departure from Alara as deserting an oath. Doctrine should not claim that Elspeth governed Bant, founded Bantian institutions, or directed Bant’s post-Conflux politics unless a stronger direct narrative source is added.

Confidence: High for prayer/oath/deserted Alara claims; medium for broader “knightly identity” framing; unsupported for governance or institutional leadership.

5. Bant’s detailed post-Conflux and post-Phyrexia political condition

Answer: This should not be promoted as a detailed political map. The support is enough to promote survival and cultural continuity, but not enough to describe Bant’s full government after Conflux or after the Phyrexian invasion.

Official Magic Origins material says Alara has been reunited and that Bant is now one region on a larger plane. The Phyrexian-era story A Man of Parts shows that Bant, Valeron, sigiled knights, and Knight-General Rafiq still exist as meaningful references during the New Phyrexian invasion era. That supports continuity of Bantian identity, geography, military hierarchy, and Rafiq’s relevance. It does not confirm a complete post-invasion political settlement.

Promotable doctrine wording:

After the Conflux, Bant was no longer an isolated shard but a region of reunited Alara. Its identity survived: later references still preserve Bant, Valeron, sigiled knights, and Knight-General Rafiq as meaningful cultural and military markers. However, current official support confirms continuity more strongly than political detail. Doctrine may say Bant survived as a recognizable region and tradition after Conflux and into the Phyrexian-invasion era, but should not claim a fully documented post-Phyrexia government.

Confidence: High for reunited Alara / Bant as region / Rafiq and Valeron still relevant; low for any detailed post-Phyrexia governmental structure.
