Executive Summary: The Temur Frontier

A comprehensive analysis of Tarkir's Green-Blue-Red clan, distinguishing official lore from mechanical identity and community interpretation.

Best Short Definition

The Temur Frontier is a rugged, green-centered survivalist clan from the plane of Tarkir, defined by their reverence for elemental nature, ancestral shamanism, and ferocious strength. Lacking the civilization-building laws of white and the parasitic ambition of black, the Temur operate on instinct and communal endurance, utilizing blue's observation and red's passion to thrive in an unforgiving wilderness.

What Not To Overclaim

  • Do not treat the Atarka brood (Dragons timeline) as culturally identical to the Khans-era Temur Frontier.
  • Do not equate all Green-Blue-Red cards in MTG with Temur lore.
  • Do not assume Surrak speaks for all Temur beliefs (he is a pragmatic warrior, whereas Yasova embodies more of the shamanic tradition).
  • Do not treat EDH "goodstuff" or "cascade" strategies as canonical Tarkir lore.

Confirmed Canon Foundation

According to official Khans of Tarkir release notes and storyline articles:

  • Identity: One of the five warring clans of Tarkir, representing the "savagery" aspect of dragons.
  • Colors: Green, Blue, Red (GUR).
  • Environment: The harsh mountains and tundras of Tarkir.
  • Species: Humans, Ainok (hound-folk), Aven (bird-folk, specifically icy/shamanic variants), Elementals, and Bears.
  • Timeline Shift: Under the original timeline, led by Khan Surrak Dragonclaw. In the altered timeline (Dragons of Tarkir), the clan submits to Dragonlord Atarka, losing their blue identity, their ancestral shamanism (which Atarka outlawed), and their clan name.

Color Philosophy & The Void

Analyzing Temur as a Green-centered faction defined as much by its components as by its lack of White and Black.

The Green-Centered Paradigm

Data representation of Mark Rosewater's design commentary on clan color weighting.

🌲 Core: Green

Provides the philosophical center. Values instinct, natural order, survival, and deep respect for the wild. The belief that strength is proven through thriving in harsh conditions.

💧 Ally: Blue

Provides elemental knowledge, foresight, and pattern recognition. Blue in Temur manifests not as institutional academia, but as ancestral whispers, shamanic magic, and adapting to the ice.

🔥 Enemy: Red

Provides passion, impulse, and ferocity. Ensures the clan acts with emotional immediacy and battle instinct, maintaining freedom against the restrictive empires of other clans.

The Absence of White and Black

Understanding Temur requires observing the mechanical and philosophical void of White and Black:

Without White:

The Temur have no centralized law, no grand architecture, no standing armies, and no formal hierarchy beyond localized strength and shamanic respect. Their "duty" is organic survival, not imposed legal obligation.

Without Black:

The Temur lack selfish ambition, exploitation, and necromancy. They hunt for sustenance, not sport or power-hoarding. They respect the dead (ancestors) rather than exploiting them as resources.

Society, Culture & The Frontier

The internal dynamics, peoples, and magic of the Temur.

Frontier Life & Shamanism

The Temur exist in nomadic family groups traversing the Qal Sisma mountains. Their culture heavily emphasizes oral tradition, guided by shamans who commune with the "Wide Whispers"—a collective elemental memory of the ancestors.

Magic is heavily elemental (ice, fire, earth) and deeply tied to nature. The 'dragon aspect' they revere is savagery, observing dragons as the ultimate expression of natural apex predators.

The Peoples of Temur

  • Humans: The primary leaders, warriors, and shamans.
  • Ainok: Dog/wolf-like humanoids suited for tracking and survival in the tundra.
  • Aven: Unlike the sun-loving Jeskai aven, Temur aven are often heavily feathered (owl-like), acting as scouts and ice-shamans.
  • Bears & Elementals: Revered companions. "Bear punching" is a legendary rite of passage, but working alongside beasts is the cultural norm.

The Atarka Shift (Timeline Variance)

In Fate Reforged/Dragons of Tarkir, Yasova Dragonclaw surrenders the clan to Atarka to prevent their annihilation. The result is the Atarka Brood. Under Atarka, Blue mana is virtually erased. Shamanism is outlawed to prevent ancestral uprisings, and the culture degrades from respectful survivalists to desperate hunters feeding a gluttonous dragon overlord. The Temur Frontier, functionally, ceases to exist.

Mechanical Identity & EDH Culture

How the lore translates to the table, and how players interpret Temur.

The Tarkir Mechanic: Ferocious

Ferocious is an ability word that cares if you control a creature with power 4 or greater. This perfectly marries Green's love for big creatures, Red's aggressive stats, and Blue's spellcraft (e.g., Stubborn Denial—a blue counterspell that gets stronger if you have a 4-power creature).

Commander (EDH) Interpretation

Data modeled from EDHREC archetypes. Notice the divergence from pure Tarkir lore into broader mechanical themes like "Cascade" and "Spellslinger".

The "Temur Goodstuff" Phenomenon

In modern Commander culture (r/EDH, EDHREC), "Temur" is often synonymous with massive mana generation (Green), drawing cards/interaction (Blue), and explosive turns/extra combat (Red).

Common Player Perceptions:

  • Cascade Value: Commanders like Maelstrom Wanderer dominate the perception of Temur as a "random explosive value" identity, disconnected from Tarkir lore.
  • Elementals/Lands: Omnath, Locus of the Roil shifts Temur player focus toward Landfall, supported mechanically but divergent from the "Frontier survival" aesthetic.
  • Player Appeal: Players enjoy Temur because it fixes Green's lack of stack-interaction, Blue's lack of board presence, and Red's lack of consistent card advantage.
