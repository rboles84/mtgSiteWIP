# Colorless Magic in Magic: The Gathering

Colorless in Magic: The Gathering has two distinct meanings:

It can describe objects with no color (like most artifacts or certain creatures).

It can describe a type of mana that is different from both colored mana and generic mana.

Colorless is not a color and does not appear on the color wheel. While some cards specifically require colorless mana (true colorless, represented by the ◇ symbol), these are relatively rare. Most “colorless” cards simply cost generic mana, which can be paid with any color.

Mechanically:

An object with no color is considered colorless.

Colorless ≠ a sixth color, even though it sometimes functions like one in deckbuilding.

Rules governing this appear in Rule 105 (Colors) and Rule 202 (Mana Cost and Color).

## Comprehensive Rules & Game Mechanics

* **Nature of Colorless:** Colorless is not a color and is not represented on the color wheel. If an effect asks a player to choose a color, they cannot choose colorless.
* **Dual Definition:** Colorless represents both a type of object and a type of mana.
* **Mana Types vs. Mana Colors:** Magic has five colors of mana (White, Blue, Black, Red, Green) but six *types* of mana (White, Blue, Black, Red, Green, and Colorless).
* **Color Identity in Commander:** Colorless does not contribute to a card's color identity. Colorless cards can fit into any Commander deck. However, if a deck's commander has a colorless color identity (e.g., *Kozilek, the Great Distortion*), the entire deck must be colorless.

### Colorless Objects

* **Default Status:** An object (a card or spell) with no colored mana symbols in its mana cost is colorless.
* **Lands:** Having no mana costs, lands are always colorless by default, with the sole exception of cards with a color indicator.
* **Frame and Set Numbering:** Colorless cards that are not artifacts or lands feature a transparent frame, allowing the art to run to the border. In collector numbers, colorless cards appear before colored cards, whereas artifacts and lands are numbered at the end of a set.
* **Design Philosophy:** Colorless cards generally have access to effects from all parts of the color pie but at an inefficient mana cost (e.g., permanent destruction costs a minimum of 7 generic mana). Artifacts trade efficiency for flexibility, serving as a "jack of all trades, master of none."

### Colorless Mana vs. Generic Mana

* **The Distinction:** Colorless is a type of mana, whereas generic is a type of cost. A player cannot produce generic mana. Colorless mana can be used to pay for generic mana costs, but generic mana costs can be paid with any type of mana (colored or colorless).
* **Colorless Costs:** Costs that explicitly require colorless mana ($\{C\}$) can *only* be paid with colorless mana.

### Selected Official Comprehensive Rules

> **107.4c** The colorless mana symbol $\{C\}$ is used to represent one colorless mana, and also to represent a cost that can be paid only with one colorless mana.
> **106.10** If an effect would add mana represented by a generic mana symbol to a player’s mana pool, that much colorless mana is added to that player’s mana pool.
> **202.2b** Objects with no colored mana symbols in their mana costs are colorless.

## Keyword Abilities & Card Mechanics

### Devoid

A characteristic-defining keyword ability stating that a card is colorless regardless of the colors in its mana cost. Devoid cards feature colored mana requirements in their costs, allowing them to utilize mechanics from their respective color pie alignments while maintaining a mechanically colorless status.

### Support

* **Mechanic Definition:** "Support N" means "Put a $+1/+1$ counter on each of up to N target creatures."
* **Restrictions:** If a permanent has an activated or triggered ability that supports, that ability cannot target the permanent itself. Counters must be distributed among distinct target creatures; they cannot all be piled onto a single creature.

### Surge

* **Mechanic Definition:** An alternative casting cost that players can pay if they or a teammate have already cast another spell during the turn.
* **Functionality:** The surge cost is valid regardless of whether the first spell resolved, was countered, or is still on the stack. Surge can provide mana discounts or trigger additional card effects.

### Cohort

An alliance-based mechanic requiring two Allies to work together. The cost of a cohort ability includes tapping the creature with the ability (which must not have summoning sickness) and tapping "an untapped Ally you control" (which can be a newly cast creature with summoning sickness).

## History of Colorless Mana

* **Alpha Roots:** Colorless mana was introduced in *Alpha* via *Basalt Monolith*, *Mana Vault*, and *Sol Ring*. Originally, this mana was used exclusively to pay for neutral costs like artifacts.
* **Text Evolution:** Early sets spelled out "Add 2 colorless mana." From *Onslaught* through *Theros*, formatting shifted to a numerical circle (e.g., $\{2\}$) to save space. This created mechanical ambiguity because the same symbol represented a colorless mana output and a generic mana cost on the same card (e.g., *Unknown Shores*).
* **The $\{C\}$ Redesign:** In *Oath of the Gatewatch* (2016), R&D introduced the official colorless mana symbol ($\{C\}$) to isolate colorless mana from generic costs. This required Oracle database updates to over 300 historical cards; *Sol Ring* was updated to read "Add $\{C\}\{C\}$."
* **Wastes:** Introduced in *Oath of the Gatewatch*, *Wastes* is a basic land that taps for $\{C\}$. It possesses no land types, meaning players cannot choose "Wastes" when an effect requires choosing a basic land type. In Limited formats (Sealed/Draft), players can only add *Wastes* to their deck if they explicitly opened or drafted them.
* **Set Availability:** Generating colorless mana is an evergreen mechanic. However, costs requiring explicit colorless mana ($\{C\}$) are non-evergreen. Outside of reprints, colorless mana costs have explicitly appeared in *Oath of the Gatewatch*, *Eldrazi Unbound (Commander Masters)*, and *Modern Horizons 3*.
* **The Brothers' War Exploration:** During vision design for *The Brothers' War*, R&D experimented with fifteen draft archetypes where colorless mana acted as a pseudo-sixth color, though the concept was ultimately scrapped.

## Lore & Flavor Profiles

### The Eldrazi

Native to the Blind Eternities, the Eldrazi are ancient cosmic horrors that travel between planes to devour mana and life energy. Their feeding processes strip magical color from reality, leaving behind barren *Wastes*. Mechanically, their alien nature is represented through abilities like *Annihilator*, *Ingest*, and *Devoid*. Philosophically, they exist outside conventional morality, alignment, or personality, operating on a primal necessity to consume.

### Artifacts and Technology

For the majority of artifacts, colorlessness represents cold science, engineering, or a lack of organic lifeforce and individual internal agency. Artifact creatures are typically synthetic beings or automatons (e.g., the Myr of Mirrodin, Assembly-Workers, and Constructs).

### Transcendence (Ugin & Karn)

* **Ugin:** The elder dragon planeswalker Ugin uses colorless spirit magic. His lack of color represents spiritual transcendence beyond the five colors of the mana wheel rather than a destructive separation from them.
* **Karn:** A silver golem planeswalker who embodies a philosophy of absolute cosmic balance.
* **The Colorless Philosophy:** Unlike the Eldrazi, planeswalkers like Ugin and Karn are capable of understanding and manipulating colored mana (e.g., *Ghostfire* or *Karn's Temporal Sundering*), but they remain detached from the core emotional biases of the five colors.

### Specific Card Restrictions

* **Treasure Tokens & Command Tower:** These cards instruct you to choose a "color" of mana. Because colorless is not a color, you cannot use a Treasure token or *Command Tower* to produce colorless mana ($\{C\}$). Colorless commander decks cannot utilize *Command Tower*.
* **Exotic Orchard:** If an opponent controls an *Adarkar Wastes* (which taps for $\{W\}$, $\{U\}$, or $\{C\}$), you cannot use *Exotic Orchard* to produce $\{C\}$, because colorless is not a color.
* **Reflecting Pool:** Because *Reflecting Pool* checks for mana *types* rather than colors, it can successfully produce $\{C\}$ if you control another land capable of generating colorless mana.
* **Snow Mana ($\{S\}$):** Snow is a permanent supertype, not a mana type or color. The symbol $\{S\}$ is a generic cost that can be paid with any color or colorless mana, provided the source has the "Snow" supertype.
* **Angelic Intervention / Mother of Runes:** Protection from a color does not protect a permanent from colorless spells, tokens, or sources. Cards must explicitly state "protection from colorless" (e.g., *Angelic Intervention*) to mitigate colorless threats.
