# Golgari Narrative Taxonomy

**Identity:** Golgari Swarm  
**Core axiom:** Nothing is wasted; endings are inventory.  
**Primary tension:** survival through reclamation, not purity.  
**Vox Mana placement mode:** you read systems by what they do with loss.

---

## 1. High-Level Read

Golgari is not simply “death,” “zombies,” or “gross nature.”

Golgari is the black-green worldview where:
- life and death are not opposites
- the graveyard is a resource layer
- decay is infrastructure
- the discarded become politically and materially powerful
- survival matters more than cleanliness
- growth is most honest when it admits what fed it

The Golgari player does not ask:

> How do I avoid loss?

They ask:

> What does this loss become?

## 3. Archetypal Roles

### 3.1 The Rot-Farmer

**Drive:** cultivate value from what others discard.  
**Cards:** Golgari Rot Farm, Deathsprout, Life from the Loam, Glowspore Shaman.  
**Narrative line:** “The ground is not empty. It is working.”

Use for:
- ramp plus graveyard setup
- land recursion
- long-game resource framing
- practical, non-glamorous survival

---

### 3.2 The Grave-Keeper

**Drive:** preserve the dead as future options.  
**Cards:** Jarad's Orders, Golgari Findbroker, Deadbridge Chant, Meren of Clan Nel Toth.  
**Narrative line:** “Nothing buried is necessarily gone.”

Use for:
- recursion panels
- commander deck recommendations
- placement copy about memory, inheritance, and retrieval

---

### 3.3 The Scar-Inheritor

**Drive:** turn dead bodies into living strength.  
**Cards:** Varolz, the Scar-Striped; Dreg Mangler; Deadbridge Goliath; Corpsejack Menace; Shambling Shell.  
**Narrative line:** “Power is inherited through wounds.”

Use for:
- +1/+1 counters
- scavenge
- creature upgrade loops
- combat identities that are still Golgari, not Gruul

---

### 3.4 The Swarm-Mother

**Drive:** transform death into many bodies.  
**Cards:** Izoni, Thousand-Eyed; Golgari Germination; Korozda Guildmage; Hatchery Spider.  
**Narrative line:** “A grave is a nursery if the swarm owns it.”

Use for:
- token decks
- sacrifice fodder
- insect/saproling visuals
- board-wide pressure

---

### 3.5 The Undercity Regent

**Drive:** rule from beneath through survival knowledge.  
**Cards:** Vraska, Golgari Queen; Savra, Queen of the Golgari; Jarad, Golgari Lich Lord; Sisters of Stone Death.  
**Narrative line:** “The throne belongs to whoever can make the dead useful.”

Use for:
- leadership tone
- placement identity copy
- lore panels
- political/psychological framing

---

### 3.6 The Patient Infection

**Drive:** win by erosion, not spectacle.  
**Cards:** Darkblast, Putrefy, Abrupt Decay, Assassin's Trophy, Necrotic Wound.  
**Narrative line:** “Decay wins by proving everything can be broken down.”

Use for:
- removal and interaction
- attrition identity
- anti-Rakdos guardrail

---

### 3.7 The Lich Accountant

**Drive:** count the dead and cash them in.  
**Cards:** Underrealm Lich, Lotleth Giant, Svogthos, the Restless Tomb, Old Stickfingers.  
**Narrative line:** “The graveyard is a ledger with teeth.”

Use for:
- graveyard-count scaling
- undergrowth-style logic
- big payoff panels
- late-game inevitability

## 4. Narrative Progression Model

Use this progression when building a Golgari result page, dossier panel, or Maze return flow:

1. **Surface Loss**  
   Something dies, is discarded, milled, sacrificed, destroyed, or forgotten.

2. **Burial / Intake**  
   The loss moves into a hidden but usable layer: graveyard, token pool, counters, land base, or memory.

3. **Composting / Conversion**  
   The dead material becomes mana, cards, bodies, counters, removal, or future selection.

4. **Accretion**  
   Small advantages stack. The opponent may not feel threatened yet.

5. **Emergence**  
   The swarm appears: a large threat, token mass, recursion loop, or finishing drain.

6. **Inevitability**  
   The game/state/story ends because the Golgari player turned every previous ending into material.

## 5. Placement Copy Blocks

### Core Placement Line

> You do not fear endings. You inventory them.

### Short Dossier Read

> Golgari reads the world as a lifecycle engine. What falls is not erased; it becomes soil, debt to the body, memory in the roots, or strength for whatever survives next.

### New Player Explanation

> Golgari decks often use the graveyard like a second hand. Creatures dying, cards being milled, and permanents being sacrificed are not only losses — they become fuel for recursion, tokens, counters, removal, and late-game pressure.

### Advanced Player Explanation

> Golgari’s strongest structural reads cluster around self-mill, recursion, sacrifice loops, graveyard-count scaling, +1/+1 counter inheritance, and attrition packages that make conventional one-for-one answers deteriorate over time.

## 7. Drift Guardrails

### Do Not Drift Into Selesnya

Selesnya says:
> harmony, community, collective flourishing.

Golgari says:
> community survives because the dead, discarded, and lowly are not wasted.

Correction phrase:
> Add rot, scarcity, sacrifice, hierarchy, or graveyard utility.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Dimir

Dimir says:
> hidden knowledge, secrets, surveillance, manipulation.

Golgari says:
> buried matter, memory in the soil, public usefulness of the dead.

Correction phrase:
> Make the graveyard material, not merely secret.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Orzhov

Orzhov says:
> debt, contract, obligation, spiritual economy.

Golgari says:
> ecological inheritance, material reuse, survival economy.

Correction phrase:
> Replace debt with compost, contract with lifecycle.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Simic

Simic says:
> improvement, adaptation, experimental optimization.

Golgari says:
> adaptation by reclaiming death and waste.

Correction phrase:
> Replace upgrade language with reclamation language.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Rakdos

Rakdos says:
> spectacle, impulse, catharsis, pain as performance.

Golgari says:
> attrition, patience, pain as material consequence.

Correction phrase:
> Replace spectacle with erosion.
