# Gruul Narrative Taxonomy

**Identity:** Gruul Clans  
**Core axiom:** The body knows before the law finishes speaking.  
**Primary tension:** freedom through instinctive force, not planned permission.  
**Vox Mana placement mode:** you read systems by what they try to stop, and whether the right response is to move faster, hit harder, or wake the terrain itself.

---

## 1. High-Level Read

Gruul is not simply “big creatures,” “barbarians,” or “random smashing.”

Gruul is the red-green worldview where:

- impulse and instinct are trusted before abstraction
- the body is a source of truth
- terrain, creatures, and emotion are active forces
- obstacles are not debated forever; they are tested by impact
- growth matters when it becomes motion
- ramp matters when it becomes pressure
- combat is not a minigame; it is the proof layer

The Gruul player does not ask:

> What is the perfect plan?

They ask:

> What is standing in the way, and what hits it first?

## 4. Narrative Progression Model

Use this progression when building a Gruul result page, dossier panel, or Maze return flow:

1. **Pressure Builds**  
   Something feels contained: law, delay, control, board stall, fear, or overthinking.

2. **Body Signal**  
   The reading identifies a bodily truth: anger, hunger, herd movement, territory, instinct, survival, play.

3. **Riot Choice**  
   The user/deck chooses the route: hit now, hit harder, go wider, go taller, break the obstacle, or wake the land.

4. **Impact Translation**  
   A creature becomes a combat trick, a land becomes mana, a ramp creature becomes attacker, or a threat becomes removal.

5. **Aftershock**  
   The hit changes the board state: blockers fail, systems burn, control pays, or the next body becomes inevitable.

6. **Uncontained Motion**  
   The game/state/story advances because the obstacle could not keep the wild still.

## 5. Placement Copy Blocks

### Core Placement Line

> You do not wait for permission from a world that forgot how to move.

### Short Dossier Read

> Gruul reads the world through force, terrain, instinct, and impact. Where other identities see systems to optimize or laws to obey, Gruul sees blockades, cages, and quiet machines that need to be tested by motion.

### New Player Explanation

> Gruul decks often win by attacking with creatures that are too fast, too large, or too hard to block cleanly. Gruul cards commonly reward attacking, ramp into bigger threats, grant haste or trample, and turn combat itself into the place where decisions get made.

### Advanced Player Explanation

> Gruul’s strongest structural reads cluster around riot-style speed/size choices, bloodrush-style creature-to-combat translation, trample finishers, fight-based removal, anti-control pressure, land-to-impact engines, and ramp packages that convert mana creatures or land drops into lethal attacks.

## 7. Drift Guardrails

### Do Not Drift Into Rakdos

Rakdos says:

> spectacle, indulgence, pain-as-performance, audience shock.

Gruul says:

> instinct, force, terrain, and obstacles breaking under motion.

Correction phrase:

> Remove performance language. Add land, body, impact, trample, or anti-obstacle pressure.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Golgari

Golgari says:

> decay, graveyard, recursion, compost, survival through reclamation.

Gruul says:

> living motion, immediate force, and the physical refusal to be contained.

Correction phrase:

> Replace burial/rot language with stomp, charge, land, bloodrush, riot, or aftershock.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Selesnya

Selesnya says:

> harmony, community, collective flourishing.

Gruul says:

> clan, pack, herd, and wild belonging that rejects imposed civilization.

Correction phrase:

> Make the group physical and untamed, not peaceful or moralized.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Simic

Simic says:

> adaptation, improvement, engineering, optimized growth.

Gruul says:

> growth that becomes force without asking to be improved in a lab.

Correction phrase:

> Remove laboratory/perfection language. Add instinctive size, territory, and collision.

---

### Do Not Drift Into Boros

Boros says:

> disciplined action, tactical formation, justice through force.

Gruul says:

> undisciplined action, clan force, and freedom from imposed structure.

Correction phrase:

> Remove duty, formation, and justice. Add impulse, wildness, and physical consequence.
