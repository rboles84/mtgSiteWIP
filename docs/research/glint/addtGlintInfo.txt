Glint-Eye Source Data: What Actually Exists vs. What's Missing
What the searches confirm as genuine citable material
Nephilim lore (Guildpact/Dissension era) — solid floor:
The Nephilim were powerful monstrosities with limited intelligence, venerated by the Cult of Yore as the "Old Gods" — symbols of old Ravnica before the Guildpact. They were there not to demand worship or answer prayers, but to humble: living, walking examples of the world's vastness, diversity, unpredictability, danger, wonder, horror, and complexity beyond comprehension. That's the richest single passage in the canon — it's citable and strong. Fandom
Once awakened by construction works in the Utvara district, they fed on a dragon corpse and grew in size and power. They defeated Niv-Mizzet; he destroyed two of them. The remaining three made it to Ravnica's center before being subdued by Rakdos and mana explosions. Fandom
Glint-Eye Nephilim specifically — almost nothing. The card text is the entire canon record: a 2/2 that draws cards equal to combat damage dealt, with a discard-for-pump activation. No individual Glint-Eye story, no name, no personality, no separate lore text beyond the shared Nephilim mythology above. Scryfall
Yidris / Entropic Uprising — usable but thin:
Yidris was born on the Grixis shard of Alara, one of the Incurables afflicted with unstable mutations threatening to shatter his mind. During the Conflux, Bant knights drove him into the Maelstrom, and its unpredictable power stabilized his mutations, making him a conduit for its chaos. That's the complete official record — one paragraph from the 2016 Commander flavor reveal. Fandom
The official product copy frames him as harnessing raw chaotic energy to obliterate foes, channeling the Maelstrom, with little care for long-term plans — preferring to unleash bursts of devastation. Card Kingdom
Commander 2016 Chaos identity framing — this is actually your best official source:
The C16 design team explicitly considered making UBRG a Zombie tribal deck before landing on a different direction. More usefully, designer Ben challenged the team to apply a "Ravnica guild"-style identity to each four-color grouping, with each faction defined by the philosophy of the missing color — the GWUB deck was antithetical to Red's instant gratification, building slowly over time. The UBRG deck's identity follows the same logic in reverse: it is antithetical to White. Wizards of the CoastWizards of the Coast
This framing was strong enough that when WotC later designed the Warhammer 40K Commander decks, the designer explicitly referenced the C16 UBRG Yidris deck as the established template for a "chaos" deck — noting that high-variance effects that disrupt the table's plans had already been proven viable in that product. The missing-White philosophical frame — community synthesis, not official text:
The clearest articulation of UBRG-as-no-order comes from the MTG Salvation forum thread, which is fan analysis, not official: eliminating White removes the will to create fairness, translating to weak or absent government and no regulation — the hallmarks of anarcho-capitalism. The modern unchecked corporation was offered as the UBRG ideal: secretive and obsessed with information (Blue), sociopathic self-advancement (Black), growth through absorption (Green), and irrational snap reactions (Red). That's a useful framing but it's fan-sourced. MTG Salvation
The closest official statement is Rosewater's design challenge framing, which was applied to all four-color groups but never rendered into a single named philosophy statement for UBRG specifically the way guilds have maxims.

The C16 Fleischer article and the WH40K callback are your best underused sources — both are official, both directly name UBRG as the chaos identity, and neither requires synthesis.

The Fleischer article contains the exact official UBRG philosophy statement. It's buried in the four-color identity section. The full structure for each faction is: name the colors present, then explain what the missing color contributed and why the remaining four cohere without it. The WUBR section begins "White embraces the progress exemplified in civilization..." and that sentence fragment from result 49 ends before the UBRG section renders — but the deck description bleeds through: the Entropic Uprising deck was described as embracing variance, playing cards sight-unseen from the top of the deck, flipping coins, and otherwise injecting additional randomness into the game — built for players who prefer to "just let things happen." Wizards of the Coast
That last phrase — "just let things happen" — is the closest thing to an official UBRG maxim that exists in canon. It's not a formal philosophical statement like a guild motto, but it is Fleischer's authored characterization of what the identity is.
What the Fleischer article's full structure actually gives you. The design logic for each faction is:
Each four-color identity was defined by what the missing color provided — and what its absence therefore removes. For GWUB, the missing color was Red, which the designers described as all about instant gratification and throwing away resources; the deck was antithetical to that, building up slowly over time. By that same structural logic, for UBRG, the missing color is White — and White, as Fleischer states it for the Artifice faction, is the color that "embraces the progress exemplified in civilization." The UBRG identity is therefore officially the faction that rejects exactly that: civilizational order, collective progress, structured advancement. Wizards of the Coast
That is not synthesis I'm adding — that is the logical output of the same framework Fleischer applies explicitly to every other faction in the same article. The absence is structural and official, even if it was never written out as a sentence for UBRG specifically. That's your argument for why the missing-White frame is canon-adjacent rather than pure fan synthesis. The framework is official; the UBRG application of it is a one-step deduction, not an invention.
What remains genuinely absent. After going deeper:
The Nephilim wiki's source footnotes point to Dissension (the Cory Herndon novel, 2006) and a Matt Cavotta article ("Wake Up Call," February 2006) as the two richest primary sources for Nephilim lore. Those are the places where Glint-Eye-specific behavior could exist — but those sources aren't digitized in scrapable form. The novel especially: if there's any individualized characterization of the Glint-Eye Nephilim during the Ravnica City battle, it's in that book and nowhere online. That's a genuine retrieval limit, not a canon absence.
The wiki cites a Magic Arcana piece from April 2006, "Guildpact Zoom-Ins," as the source for the "there only to humble" passage — that article may contain additional flavor text or design commentary that hasn't surfaced in any search result. It's probably short, but it's unverified depth. Fandom
Bottom line after the deeper pass: the evidence floor isn't as thin as it appeared — the Fleischer framework is more usable than I initially flagged. But the ceiling is genuinely low because the richest primary sources (the Herndon novel, the Cavotta article, the Zoom-Ins piece) are either paywalled, out of print, or not indexed in a way that's reachable. That's the actual limiting factor, not a failure of search coverage. If you want to go deeper than what's online, those three sources are the specific targets.
