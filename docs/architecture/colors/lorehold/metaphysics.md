## Metaphysical Thesis

Project framing boundary: this thesis is Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; it is not MTG canon.

Lorehold metaphysics, as Vox Mana project synthesis, is the thesis that the past remains materially and spiritually available. It can be preserved in records, embodied by relics, housed in monuments, contacted through spirits, restored through careful practice, and brought forward into present action.

The canon-backed floor is strong but narrower than the thesis: official-source claims identify Lorehold as the College of Archaeomancy, support passionate historical scholarship, artifacts, ancient tomes, spirits of long-dead historical figures, field studies, relics, battle sites, monuments, and restoration examples (`data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.profile.json`). The metaphysical layer translates that evidence into Vox Mana project architecture.

The shortest Vox Mana thesis is: history is evidence that can still answer.

## Philosophical Foundations

Direct source themes strongly support this foundation: spirits, relics, records, ruins, field sites, restoration, and the order/discovery tension. The metaphysical language below is Vox Mana synthesis and not MTG canon.

### The Past Is Not Absence

Lorehold evidence repeatedly refuses to treat the past as gone. Spirits of the ancient dead can be called, housed in statues, or treated as primary sources; artifacts and tomes preserve contact with what happened; ruins and battle sites remain active places of research (`data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_core_0007].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_mechanic_0001].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_mechanic_0016].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_core_0011].claim`).

Vox Mana reads this as availability: the past is not merely remembered. It remains available through evidence-bearing forms.

### Evidence Needs Stewardship

Lorehold is not loot-first discovery. The raw placement architecture strengthens Lorehold when artifacts are protected as belonging to history rather than treated as unowned loot, and the profile notes that excavation teams document significant findings for repositories (`data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_location_0009].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_placement_0009].claim`).

Vox Mana reads this as stewardship: evidence has power only when its context is protected.

### Order And Discovery Must Check Each Other

The direct Lorehold dichotomy is order and chaos. White uncovers, records, preserves, and cares for the past; red pursues reckless discovery, adventure, and the emotional truth of history (`data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_dichotomy_0001].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_dichotomy_0002].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_dichotomy_0004].claim`).

Vox Mana reads this as a methodological balance. Too much order freezes inquiry into precedent. Too much discovery becomes tomb-raiding. Lorehold needs both record and expedition.

### Spirits Are Witnesses Before They Are Fuel

The strongest metaphysical distinction from generic graveyard play is that spirits are evidence-bearing contact. The Fields of Strife claim says restless spirits are excellent primary sources because they are living their history rather than recounting it (`data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_mechanic_0016].claim`).

Vox Mana reads this as witness metaphysics: the dead can testify, teach, warn, and act, but the point is truth-bearing contact rather than death as generic resource.

### Restoration Is A Form Of Argument

Soovril's artifact restoration and the repository documentation claims support a material-history practice: old things are cleaned, identified, documented, and sometimes restored (`data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_figure_0018].claim`; `data/raw-factions/lorehold/lorehold.claims.json::claims[claim_id=claim_lorehold_location_0009].claim`).

Vox Mana reads restoration as argument: to rebuild a relic is to make a claim about what it was, what context it belongs to, and what responsibility the present has toward it.

## Vox Mana Read

This is a compression-only Vox Mana read. It adds no new nouns, mechanics, or doctrine; it is architecture language for placement and discovery, not official faction doctrine.

Lorehold's metaphysical grammar is:

1. The past leaves evidence.
2. Evidence survives as relic, record, ruin, monument, fossil, spirit, field site, or remembered spell.
3. The scholar enters carefully enough to preserve context and bravely enough to reach the truth.
4. The recovered past becomes present action: a witness, restored artifact, Spirit token, graveyard-leaves trigger, reconstructed spell, or lesson.
5. Action remains responsible only if it remembers where the evidence came from.

In Vox Mana language, Lorehold is the school where preservation and discovery make the past legible enough to act. Its sacred problem is not "How do we win with old things?" but "What does the past still prove, and what do we owe it before we use it?"

### Practical Deck Pattern

Lorehold decks become most legible when they convert historical contact into action:

- A graveyard card leaves as excavation, archive retrieval, or reconstruction.
- A Spirit appears as witness, mentor, or memory-bearing force.
- An artifact is copied, restored, returned, or rebuilt as a relic rather than generic value.
- A spell or combat moment carries battle memory, field-study urgency, or recovered precedent.
- The deck's strongest turns feel like uncover -> classify -> revive -> act.

This does not require every Lorehold deck to use every listed pattern. It defines the structural center to look for when deciding whether a card, commander, or theme is Lorehold in the Vox Mana sense.

### Matrix Floor

For Lorehold placement, require at least one of these to be central and preferably two or more:

- Historical evidence.
- Archaeology, ruins, relics, monuments, scrolls, records, or fieldwork.
- Spirits as witnesses, mentors, ancient dead, or primary-source contact.
- Artifact stewardship, restoration, or relic reconstruction.
- Graveyard-leaves play framed as excavation or recovered history.
- Rules-with-conscience: precedent and procedure serving evidence, justice, safety, or stewardship.

Without those signals, the safer result is usually not Lorehold, even if the card or user language is red-white, artifact-heavy, graveyard-adjacent, or combat-focused.
