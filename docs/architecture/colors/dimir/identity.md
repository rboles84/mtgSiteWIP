# House Dimir - Hidden Leverage Through Information

> Source note: identity.md is evidence-backed. metaphysics.md is project metaphysics, not canon doctrine. House Dimir is treated here as an expression-level Ravnica guild, not as a mono-color doctrine file.

## Identity Overview

House Dimir is Vox Mana's model for power that works because it is not publicly legible. It is not simply Blue plus Black secrets. It is the Ravnican expression where Blue's information hunger and Black's appetite for agency become hidden leverage, surveillance, infiltration, memory pressure, and control without credit.

The raw faction data directly supports House Dimir as the Blue-Black guild associated with deception, secrecy, spies, assassins, manipulation, secrets, backroom deals, Surveil, privacy, strategic patience, observation before action, and comfort operating without public recognition. Its strongest placement cluster is not generic manipulation or every Blue-Black deck. It is privacy, hidden knowledge, information asymmetry, discretion, invisible operation, and the ability to see without being seen.

At its best, Dimir is patience, preparation, and precision. It waits until action is safer because the table has already been mapped. At its worst, that same hidden engine can become brittle, paranoid, overfitted to control, or so dependent on invisibility that exposure becomes collapse.

## Core Drive

Dimir wants leverage to remain unseen until it is already decisive.

Its core drive is not public authority, legal legitimacy, debt collection, biological improvement, spectacle, or experimental velocity. Dimir is driven by the belief that information is power, and that power is safest when others cannot see its shape.

In Vox Mana terms:

- Blue supplies observation, planning, memory, curation, and control through knowledge.
- Black supplies self-preservation, leverage, ambition, and willingness to use asymmetric advantage.
- Dimir fuses them into hidden information control: learn first, reveal little, act from an angle the opponent cannot prepare to defend.

## Vox Mana Read (Core Axiom)

This section is strongly supported as Vox Mana compression derived from the already-strong `Core Drive`, `Philosophical Foundations`, `Mechanical Identity`, and `Gameplay Philosophy` sections.

Authoring rule: the read may add no new nouns, mechanics, or doctrine. It only compresses the already-supported Dimir evidence for secrecy, hidden information, observation, discretion, invisible operation, Surveil, mill, hand disruption, evasion, and control without public credit.

Dimir wins by knowing the room before the room knows Dimir is present.

It watches before acting, hides the shape of its power, filters memory through Surveil, removes options through discard and mill, and turns secrecy into leverage. Its question is not who has authority, but who knows enough to make authority irrelevant.

## Philosophical Foundations

### Information As Power

Direct Dimir profile evidence states that information is power and that power is safest when others cannot see its shape. The faction's central tension is that Dimir wants knowledge and control while denying others knowledge about itself.

### Secrecy Over Public Legitimacy

Dimir does not seek the Azorius route of public procedure or the Orzhov route of visible obligation. It is strongest when its work is deniable, compartmentalized, and need-to-know. Placement evidence reinforces this through "does not need public credit" and a calibrated guardrail against awarding Dimir when the user wants visibility, reputation, or formal power.

### Hidden Leverage Over Explicit Authority

Dimir is most distinct when leverage arrives from what others do not know. The raw claims support secrecy, spies, assassins, manipulation, and backroom deals; the raw placement model requires privacy, hidden knowledge, information asymmetry, discretion, or invisible operation.

### Observation Before Action

Dimir patience is not passivity. It is preparation. The placement model directly names "watches before acting" and "strategic patience" as Dimir signals. The action matters because the intelligence has already been gathered.

### Memory, Curation, And Control

Surveil is the cleanest official mechanical anchor for Dimir information control: it filters future draws and feeds graveyard resources. In Vox Mana language, Dimir treats memory and future access as editable surfaces, but this is project architecture derived from mechanics and placement evidence, not a claim that Magic canon names that architecture.

## Mechanical Identity

### Surveil

Surveil is the Guilds of Ravnica signature mechanic associated with Dimir. It expresses information control by filtering future draws and feeding graveyard resources. It is Dimir's strongest formal mechanic anchor because it turns hidden review into future advantage.

### Mill

Mill is supported by Dimir research and broader lore summaries as a major Dimir play pattern. In Dimir, mill should not be treated as random library damage. Commander guidance warns that mill must carry secrecy context; it works best as pressure on memory, future options, and the opponent's unseen resources.

### Hand Disruption

Discard, hand inspection, and option removal express Dimir as knowledge plus denial. The Dimir player sees the plan, removes the relevant piece, and leaves the opponent with less certainty than they had before.

### Evasion And Assassination

Unblockable threats, rogues, assassins, stealth creatures, and evasive pressure express the same premise at combat scale: the threat arrives from an angle normal defenses do not cover.

### Transmute And Prepared Answers

Dimir research records Transmute as a mechanism of prepared answers and hidden tool access. In architecture terms, Transmute is not generic tutoring; it is the right answer appearing because the plan was already concealed in the system.

### Cipher And Repeated Exploitation

Cipher turns a successful access point into a repeated order. Once the agent connects, the effect recurs through combat damage, making infiltration become a standing channel.

### Theft, Copying, And Identity Pressure

Lazav and Dimir commander guidance support identity theft, clones, and stealing value as Dimir-adjacent Commander expressions. These patterns should remain tied to hidden information, infiltration, graveyard knowledge, or stealth payoff rather than becoming broad Blue-Black theft by color identity alone.

## Gameplay Philosophy

Dimir gameplay should feel like the opponent's plan being mapped, edited, and narrowed before the opponent fully understands the pressure.

The player is rewarded for observing first, choosing the real pressure point, and winning through information asymmetry rather than visible force. Dimir often wins by seeing the hand, filtering the future, denying key options, milling libraries, slipping evasive threats through defenses, and turning one concealed access point into repeated advantage.

In Commander-facing terms, Dimir safely supports:

- Surveil, scry, filtering, and other information-curation patterns.
- Mill-control when secrecy, memory, future options, or hidden pressure remain central.
- Hand disruption, discard, extraction, and precision denial.
- Evasive rogues, assassins, unblockable threats, ninjas, and stealth combat.
- Cipher, Transmute, and hidden prepared-answer patterns.
- Theft, clones, and identity pressure when tied to infiltration, graveyard knowledge, or hidden leverage.
- Control plans that win through unseen inevitability rather than public law, debt, spectacle, biological improvement, or prototype velocity.

Dimir should not be treated as every Blue-Black control deck. Vox Mana should award Dimir for faction-specific secrecy, hidden information, observation, discretion, and invisible operation, not for color identity alone.

## Philosophical Weaknesses

Each weakness below is anchored to raw Dimir placement/shadow-risk evidence in `data/raw-factions/house_dimir/house_dimir.placement.json`. No separate Dimir shadow file was found.

### Exposure Fragility

Dimir loses much of its advantage when its position is exposed too early. The raw placement model names "acts impulsively in ways that expose position" as a poor fit and places Dimir high on `axis_concealment_vs_visibility`.

Raw placement/shadow anchor: `poor_fit_indicators`, `placement_axes.axis_concealment_vs_visibility`.

Derivation rule: treat exposure as a Dimir weakness only when it breaks hidden leverage, discretion, or observation-before-action. Do not turn this into a generic claim that Dimir cannot survive any public conflict.

### Transparency Mismatch

Dimir is a poor fit for users who need total transparency as a default condition. Its placement evidence requires privacy, hidden knowledge, information asymmetry, discretion, or invisible operation.

Raw placement/shadow anchor: `poor_fit_indicators`, `placement_summary.calibrated_primary_read`.

Derivation rule: suppress Dimir when the answer treats secrecy itself as the problem rather than as a tool with risk.

### Public Credit Distortion

Dimir becomes false when hidden work is replaced by a need for recognition, reputation, or formal status. The raw placement model names "requires public recognition to feel successful" as a poor fit and warns not to award Dimir for generic manipulation when the user wants visibility, reputation, or formal power.

Raw placement/shadow anchor: `poor_fit_indicators`, `placement_summary.calibrated_false_positive_guardrail`, `calibration_tuning.false_positive_guardrail`.

Derivation rule: if the answer wants the room to know who changed the room, compare Silverquill, Rakdos, Orzhov, or Izzet before Dimir.

### Generic Manipulation Overfit

Dimir is not every manipulative answer. The raw guidance says not to score from color identity alone, not to treat one favorite card or mechanic as enough evidence, and not to award generic manipulation when the user wants visibility, reputation, or formal power.

Raw placement/shadow anchor: `chatbot_guidance.do_not_overweight`, `calibration_tuning.false_positive_guardrail`, `calibration_tuning.required_positive_evidence_terms`.

Derivation rule: require at least a Dimir-specific positive cluster, such as secrecy, information, hidden, surveillance, discretion, unseen, memory, privacy, knowledge, or infiltration.

### Over-Compartmentalization

Rosewater's Dimir article supports secrecy as both strength and weakness, especially the risk that lack of trust prevents internal coordination. The raw placement model also requires faction-specific evidence and warns that corpus-enriched data needs review before being treated as tuned scoring fuel.

Raw placement/shadow anchor: `placement_quality.manual_review_recommended`, `placement_quality.notes`, `chatbot_guidance.do_not_overweight`.

Derivation rule: use over-compartmentalization as a bounded weakness: secrecy can become self-blinding when it prevents necessary coordination, but this remains a Dimir risk derived from approved evidence and placement guardrails, not a new doctrine.

## Operator Translation Signals (Maze / Scryfall)

### Strong Dimir Signals

- The text names House Dimir, Dimir, Duskmantle, Lazav, Szadek, Etrata, Circu, spy, assassin, infiltrator, courier, memory mage, or hidden agent.
- The card uses or evokes Surveil, mill with secrecy context, hand inspection, discard, Transmute, Cipher, unblockable pressure, stealth, theft, cloning, or identity pressure.
- The flavor centers secrecy, hidden information, surveillance, memory, infiltration, privacy, redaction, backroom deals, deniable action, or no public credit.
- The gameplay sees before acting, removes options before they matter, filters future access, attacks from an unguarded angle, or wins through unseen inevitability.
- The card feels like information asymmetry rather than public authority, contract, spectacle, biological adaptation, or visible invention.

### Inhibitors

- The text centers law, procedure, public authority, documentation, or formal review: lean Azorius.
- The text centers debt, obligation, contracts, hierarchy, tithes, prestige, or collectible legitimacy: lean Orzhov.
- The text centers spectacle, performance, transgression, danger, audience, or expressive chaos: lean Rakdos.
- The text centers decay, reclamation, death-to-life cycling, rot, or undercity survival: lean Golgari.
- The text centers biological adaptation, clades, biomancy, counters, specimens, or managed evolution: lean Simic.
- The text centers prototypes, experiments, invention, engines, lightning, technical infrastructure, or visible discovery pressure: lean Izzet.
- The text is only Blue-Black, generic control, generic manipulation, or a single mill card without secrecy context: keep Dimir weak.

### Placement Guidance

For placement, Dimir should require a specific positive evidence cluster: secrecy, information, hidden, surveillance, discretion, unseen, memory, privacy, knowledge, infiltration, observation before action, and comfort without public credit. A single Blue, Black, control, mill, or manipulation marker is not enough.

## Summary

House Dimir is hidden leverage through information. It believes the strongest power is not the loudest force, the cleanest law, the most ornate debt, the brightest experiment, or the most public performance. It is the unseen advantage that has already learned the system before the system knows it is being used.

For Vox Mana, Dimir should be read as the faction of secrecy, surveillance, memory, infiltration, discretion, and precise control without credit. Its signature question is not "who commands?" but "who knows enough to move unseen?"
