## Metaphysical Thesis

**Support status:** strongly supported as Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; not MTG canon.

Dimir metaphysics treats visibility as vulnerability.

In this project model, Dimir does not begin with a secret for its own sake. It begins with an asymmetry: whoever knows more, reveals less, and acts later can make the visible system behave as if it chose the outcome itself. Dimir is the pattern in which observation, memory, concealment, and leverage become architecture.

Because this file extends from evidence into project interpretation, its claims should remain bounded. The evidence-backed floor is Dimir as secrecy, deception, spies, assassins, manipulation, hidden information, Surveil, privacy, strategic patience, observation before action, and control without public credit. The metaphysical layer below translates those patterns into Vox Mana system language.

## Philosophical Foundations

**Support status:** strongly supported as Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; not MTG canon.

### Concealment As Armor

Dimir's first metaphysical premise is that exposure creates attack surface. To be known is to be mapped, predicted, and countered. Secrecy is therefore not decoration; it is protection through reduced legibility.

### Information As Terrain

Dimir treats knowledge as the real battlefield. Hands, libraries, graveyards, memories, identities, and future draws become terrain to scout, edit, and weaponize. The visible board matters, but the hidden map decides which visible action is safe.

### Curation As Control

Surveil is the cleanest official mechanical anchor for Dimir metaphysics. It models the act of deciding which future remains accessible and which record is sent below. In Vox Mana terms, curation is not neutral. It is control over what can become true later.

### Infiltration As Agency

Dimir action often begins with access rather than force. Evasion, assassins, rogues, Cipher, and stealth payoffs all express agency through entry into spaces where ordinary defenses are not aimed.

### Memory As Leverage

Dimir research repeatedly connects memory, secrets, and identity pressure. Mill, discard, graveyard knowledge, theft, and copy effects become metaphysical when they treat memory as a resource that can be extracted, erased, copied, or used against its owner.

### Secrecy As Risk

The same hidden architecture can become brittle. Raw placement guardrails warn against generic manipulation, public-credit drift, and broad color overfit; Rosewater's Dimir article supports secrecy as a weakness when lack of trust blocks coordination. Vox Mana should preserve that risk.

## Structural & Mechanical Architecture

**Support status:** strongly supported as Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; not MTG canon.

### Surveil: Curation Engine

Surveil models hidden review. The deck looks ahead, keeps what serves the plan, and buries what can become later fuel. In system terms, Surveil is future selection plus memory routing.

### Mill: Future Erosion

Mill models pressure on unseen possibility. Dimir mill is strongest when it reads as memory pressure, archive erosion, or loss of future options rather than isolated library damage.

### Hand Disruption: Option Extraction

Discard and hand inspection model the conversion of private knowledge into control. Dimir sees the plan, extracts the relevant option, and leaves uncertainty behind.

### Evasion: Unanswered Access

Unblockable threats, rogues, assassins, and stealth combat model force entering through an unguarded channel. The point is not brute speed; it is access where the opponent's defenses do not apply.

### Cipher: Encoded Standing Order

Cipher models repeated exploitation after successful access. One infiltrating hit carries an encoded instruction, turning a breach into a persistent channel.

### Transmute: Prepared Answer Index

Transmute models hidden contingency. The desired answer appears because the system already filed it under the correct cost, making tutoring feel like preparation rather than improvisation.

### Theft And Copying: Identity Override

Theft, clones, and Lazav-style identity pressure model the self as a surface that can be overwritten or repurposed. This is Vox Mana project architecture derived from Dimir Commander and research support, not official canon terminology.

### Disinformation And Redaction: Public Narrative Control

Dimir research frames misinformation, record tampering, and redaction as narrative hooks. In architecture terms, these are Override and ConstraintField patterns: change what the system remembers, then limit what can be checked.

### Matrix Implications

Dimir should rank high for secrecy, information control, memory pressure, evasion, hidden access, discard, surveil, mill with secrecy context, and delayed payoff.
