# Golgari Swarm - Rot As Infrastructure

Source boundary: this document treats Golgari Swarm as an expression-level Ravnica guild, not as mono Black plus mono Green and not as generic black-green. The schema shape follows `docs/reference/identity-metaphysics-markdown-schema.md`; the guild-specific content is evidence-backed by the approved repo sources. Vox Mana axiom, mapping, and operator language are project synthesis unless a sentence explicitly names direct source evidence.

## Identity Overview

Golgari Swarm is the black-green Ravnica guild whose identity centers on life, death, decay, reanimation, survival, and renewal as one continuous system. The raw faction profile describes Golgari as a guild of Ravnica and states its philosophy directly: death, decay, survival, and renewal are one cycle rather than separate moral categories.

The direct evidence gives Golgari three civic anchors:

- It lives mostly underground, in forgotten and unwanted places.
- It is tied to food production and rot farms, making waste and burial part of Ravnica's infrastructure.
- Its signature mechanical evidence includes Undergrowth, where dead creatures in graveyards become present power.

The light Vox Mana synthesis is therefore not "black-green death stuff." Golgari is the guild expression of the city below the city: an undercity society that turns what the surface abandons into shelter, food, political continuity, and threat.

## Core Drive

Golgari's core drive is to make every ending feed continuation.

It does not primarily seek purity, transcendence, escape, spectacle, or improvement. It asks what can be reclaimed from what has died, failed, been buried, been discarded, or been forced beneath notice. The Swarm's center of gravity is practical: if the city produces waste, corpses, hunger, lost populations, and forgotten spaces, then those things are not outside civilization. They are the material civilization is already built on.

Direct evidence supports this drive through the raw profile's cycle language, the claims file's underground and food-production claims, the placement file's emphasis on value in what others discard, and the Commander guidance's Golgari themes of recursion, graveyard value, sacrifice, attrition, and reclamation.

## Vox Mana Read (Core Axiom)

Core axiom: nothing is wasted; endings become material.

This is a compression-only Vox Mana reading, not MTG canon and not a new doctrine. It compresses the approved evidence that Golgari accepts death, decay, survival, and renewal as one cycle; treats the graveyard as a resource layer; and builds power from discarded matter, buried populations, and rot-fed continuity.

The axiom should be used as an interpretive handle only. It should not be expanded into unsupported claims that Golgari worships death for its own sake, treats decay as nihilism, or collapses into a generic Jund-style "devour the weak" survival logic.

## Philosophical Foundations

### Life And Death Are One Cycle

Direct evidence: the raw Golgari profile states that death, decay, survival, and renewal are one cycle. The Mark Rosewater black-green color-pie article, while explicitly about the two-color intersection rather than Golgari alone, supports black-green as the life/death pair that manipulates the graveyard, recurs threats, withstands death, and grows through death.

Vox Mana synthesis: Golgari does not solve the conflict between life and death by choosing one. It makes their continuity civic. Life is not cleanly separate from burial; burial is not the end of usefulness.

### Decay Is Nourishment

Direct evidence: Golgari is tied to decay, food production, rot farms, undergrowth, and graveyard value. The approved Commander guidance names recursion, graveyard value, sacrifice, attrition, and reclamation as owned themes.

Vox Mana synthesis: rot is the infrastructure by which waste becomes sustenance. Golgari treats decay less as a mood than as an operating system for survival.

### The Undercity Is Refuge And System

Direct evidence: the claims file says Golgari members live mostly underground in forgotten or unwanted places, and the profile says official guide material presents Golgari as an underground society that also feeds much of Ravnica.

Vox Mana synthesis: the undercity is both refuge and mechanism. It shelters what the surface discards, but it also converts that discard into food, bodies, memory, leverage, and future pressure.

### Survival Is Adaptation Through Reclamation

Direct evidence: the placement file describes ideal Golgari fit as finding value in what others discard, accepting endings as material for renewal, and surviving through adaptation and practical resilience.

Vox Mana synthesis: Golgari survival is not merely toughness. It is the ability to re-enter the cycle after apparent loss, to make the ruin useful, and to let the buried thing come back changed.

### Power Rises From What The Surface Discards

Direct evidence: raw claims support the undercity, forgotten places, food production, decay, and graveyard mechanics. `data/factions.json` also frames Golgari as building from the discarded and abandoned.

Vox Mana synthesis: Golgari's power is not only magical or military. It is social. The guild makes a civilization from waste, burial, forgotten people, old bodies, and systems the upper city prefers not to see.

## Gameplay Philosophy

Golgari gameplay accepts loss as the beginning of a second resource economy. Creatures die, cards enter the graveyard, permanents are sacrificed, and the deck asks whether those apparent losses can become bodies, counters, food, tokens, removal, or inevitability.

The player experience is often recursive rather than explosive. Golgari pressure returns. It makes removal feel temporary, sacrifice feel productive, and the graveyard feel like stored civic matter. Its tempo can be patient and grinding because its preferred resource loop does not stop at the battlefield.

This is distinct from:

- Witherbloom life-force exchange, pestcraft, medicine, toxins, and school-based craft.
- Rakdos spectacle sacrifice and appetite.
- Orzhov debt, hierarchy, contracts, and afterlife extraction.
- Jund-style predation where survival is mainly dominance and devouring the weak.

Golgari can be brutal, but the brutality belongs to a cycle of reclamation, not to spectacle, contract, or domination for its own sake.

## Philosophical Weaknesses

Direct evidence supports several Golgari weak points:

- The raw profile says Golgari is philosophically stable around cycles but politically unstable because succession, overthrow, and reanimation are built into the system.
- The Mark Rosewater black-green article identifies lack of control and lack of finesse as black-green weaknesses.
- The placement file marks poor fit for users who need purity or cleanliness as a moral principle, deny loss instead of integrating it, or reject the body, decay, and mortality.

Vox Mana synthesis: Golgari can mistake endurance for resolution. Because it can integrate almost any loss into the cycle, it may underweight the need to prevent harm before harm becomes compost. Its confidence in inevitability can become fatalism; its comfort with rot can become contempt for surface forms of care, cleanliness, and boundary.

Unsupported material to avoid: do not claim Golgari is simply evil, nihilistic, a death cult, or a zombie horde. Those framings flatten the evidence and erase the guild's civic, agricultural, and survival roles.

## Operator Translation Signals (Maze / Scryfall)

### Maze Signals

Strong positive Golgari placement signals:

- Finds value in what others discard.
- Accepts endings as material for renewal.
- Builds from abandoned places, failed systems, and buried resources.
- Is comfortable with mortality, bodies, rot, decay, and practical survival.
- Reads waste, food, burial, and social neglect as connected systems.

Suppress or redirect Golgari when the user centers:

- Generic resilience or nature without death, decay, reclamation, or graveyard logic.
- Pure life-force exchange, pestcraft, medicine, toxins, or school discipline, which points toward Witherbloom.
- Harmony and shared life without the dead or discarded, which points toward Selesnya.
- Managed biological improvement, which points toward Simic.
- Debt, contract, tithe, or afterlife hierarchy, which points toward Orzhov.
- Spectacle, appetite, and transgression, which points toward Rakdos.
- Anti-civilization rage and dispossession without underground civic continuity, which points toward Gruul.

## Summary

Golgari Swarm is draftable from current repo evidence as a cautious expression-level Ravnica guild identity. Its supported center is the civic/ecological continuity of life and death: decay feeds survival, rot becomes infrastructure, the undercity shelters and processes what the surface rejects, and graveyard value turns endings into future material.

The strongest sections are identity overview, core drive, philosophical foundations, mechanics, gameplay, weaknesses, system mapping, operator signals, and summary.
