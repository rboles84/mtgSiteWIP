## Metaphysical Thesis

Project framing boundary: this thesis is Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; it is not MTG canon.

Witherbloom metaphysics treats life and death as phases of embodied exchange. A body feeds another body, a wound teaches, a pest dies and leaves life behind, a spell uses living essence, and a mage learns by working close enough to the process to know what can heal, harm, grow, or return.

The canon-backed floor is strong but narrower than the thesis: official-source claims identify Witherbloom as Strixhaven's College of Essence Studies, say its mages draw power from opposing forces of life and death, and describe magic brewed from natural components and living essence to heal, harm, raise, or entreat the dead (`data/raw-factions/witherbloom/witherbloom.claims.json`). The metaphysical layer translates that evidence into Vox Mana project architecture.

## Philosophical Foundations

**Support status:** strongly supported as Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; not MTG canon.

This section translates approved Witherbloom evidence into project-facing metaphysical language.

### Essence As Workable Matter

Witherbloom does not treat life and death as distant abstractions. The raw evidence frames them as forces that can be studied, brewed, encouraged, exploited, and used through practical magic (`data/raw-factions/witherbloom/witherbloom.profile.json`; `data/raw-factions/witherbloom/witherbloom.claims.json`).

### Care And Harm As One Craft

The same official claim supports healing and harm. Vox Mana reads this as the school refusing a clean split between cure and poison. The question is not only what magic does, but what context makes the same embodied knowledge restorative, dangerous, or both.

### The Body As Site Of Knowledge

Witherbloom placement strengthens around embodied knowledge: life/death exchange, fieldwork, remedies, ecology, the body, practical magic, and comfort with material processes (`data/raw-factions/witherbloom/witherbloom.placement.json`). In project terms, Witherbloom learns through contact with living systems rather than detached abstraction.

### Death As Conversion

The Rosewater black-green philosophy article supports the broader color-pie backdrop that black-green values the life/death cycle, graveyard resources, recursion, growth, death, and strengthening through adversity. This is not Witherbloom-specific doctrine, but it supports the project reading when bounded by Witherbloom's school, essence, pest, medicine, toxin, and fieldwork evidence (`docs/research/canon/mark_rosewater_official_two_color/golgari_Life and Death _ MAGIC_ THE GATHERING.md`).

### The Shadow Of Practicality

Witherbloom's shadow is not villainy by default. It is the risk that practical knowledge of living essence turns every body into material. The raw profile names this tension directly: nurturing ecosystems versus exploiting life essence for personal or practical power (`data/raw-factions/witherbloom/witherbloom.profile.json`).

## Vox Mana Read

This is compression-only synthesis in a Vox Mana read. It adds no new nouns, mechanics, or doctrine; it is architecture language for placement and discovery, not official faction doctrine.

Witherbloom asks what a living system becomes when death is not outside life, but one of the ways life moves. Its metaphysical unit is the exchange: body into food, pest into life, loss into growth, toxin into remedy, death into future material, and vitality into pressure.

The metaphysical shape is life/death craft -> biological exchange -> resource conversion. Lifegain, drain, sacrifice, pests, graveyard play, Food, counters, or recursion are Witherbloom only when they preserve that shape.
