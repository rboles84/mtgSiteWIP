## Philosophical Foundations

Strongly supported as Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; not MTG canon.

### Obligation As Infrastructure

Orzhov treats obligation as a thing that can be stored, transferred, inherited, witnessed, and enforced. In Vox Mana architecture, debt is not just a theme. It is a durable relation between actor, institution, and consequence.

### Hierarchy As Memory

Hierarchy remembers who may collect. Rank, office, family name, clergy, Obzedat authority, and syndicate standing create continuity between past obligation and present power. Orzhov does not only preserve records; it preserves the authority to act on records.

### Legitimacy As Extraction Channel

Orzhov extraction works because it appears authorized. A tithe, tax, rite, contract, or debt notice does not look like a random attack. It looks like the system making a proper claim. That is the metaphysical difference between Orzhov and simple predation.

### Death As Failed Escape

Afterlife, spirits, haunt, the Obzedat, and deathless control all support a project model where death is not release from structure. In Orzhov metaphysics, death becomes another jurisdiction for obligation.

### Continuity As Control

Orzhov values what lasts: family, institution, debt, title, record, estate, ghost, and claim. Continuity is protective when it preserves meaning; it becomes predatory when it preserves extraction after the person has no practical exit.

### Trust As A Weak Foundation

The same architecture is unstable because contract can replace trust. Rosewater's White-Black article supports shaky subgroup trust and self-destruction as a major weakness. Vox Mana should keep Orzhov's power durable but socially brittle.

## Structural & Mechanical Architecture

Strongly supported as Vox Mana internal architecture derived from approved evidence; not MTG canon.

### Afterlife: Continued Service

Afterlife models the Orzhov refusal to let death end utility or obligation. A body leaves, but a spirit remains as value, labor, or presence. In system terms, Afterlife converts death into continuing service.

### Life Drain: Asymmetric Transfer

Life drain models extraction as a two-sided transaction: the opponent loses and Orzhov gains. The important pattern is not damage alone, but transfer under an Orzhov frame of payment, tithe, debt, or consequence.

### Lifegain Into Leverage: Credit Conversion

Orzhov lifegain becomes identity-rich when it converts into counters, removal, drain, political pressure, or future control. Life is not merely safety; it is credit that can become authority.

### Aristocrats: Death Clause Engine

Aristocrats patterns model the clause hidden in mortality. A creature dies and the contract pays out through cards, tokens, damage, drain, exile, or sacrifice value. Death is treated as a triggerable obligation.

### Taxes And Tithes: Constraint With A Price

Tax and tithe effects model action made payable. Unlike Azorius procedure, Orzhov constraints are strongest when the opponent can act only by acknowledging the cost, paying the price, or accepting consequence.

### Contracts And Payment Choices: Legible Coercion

Choice, payment, and "unless" patterns can express Orzhov when the choice is not morally neutral: pay, owe, lose, sacrifice, or accept the terms. In Vox Mana architecture, these are ConstraintField patterns built from approved Orzhov evidence, not official rules taxonomy.

### Spirits, Haunt, And The Obzedat: Deathless Office

Ghosts and spirits become Orzhov when they preserve office, debt, service, or authority. The Obzedat is the cleanest lore-facing emblem: leadership that survives death and keeps its standing.

### Extort: Secondary Accretion Signal

Extort is an approved Commander/search signal in `docs/reference/commander-faction-guidance.md` and useful prior Orzhov mechanical vocabulary. Because this pass has direct raw-claim support for Afterlife but not a separate local raw Extort claim, Extort should remain secondary and evidence-bounded.
